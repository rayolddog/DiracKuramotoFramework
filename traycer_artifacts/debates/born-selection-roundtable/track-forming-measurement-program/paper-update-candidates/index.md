# Candidate revisions for Born Selection, Heisenberg Cut, and Paper 3

## Editorial intent

These passages are **candidates**, not approved manuscript text. They preserve John's progression across the three papers while correcting an overgeneralization: the phrase “one site receives the full quantum” belongs to destructive absorptive detection, not to each mark made by a particle that continues through a tracking medium.

The recommended revision makes a track useful in two ways:

1. it provides a concrete example of unitary, energy-conserving wave evolution through a cascade of partial interactions; and
2. it exposes the first-mark selection problem more sharply than the later, already-conditioned part of the track.

## Shared terminology

| Term | Recommended meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Absorptive capture** | The incident travelling excitation is transferred into detector matter; a successful event can exhaust the incident quantum |
| **Partial track vertex** | The traveller scatters or ionizes a local degree of freedom, deposits some energy and momentum, and continues |
| **First-mark selection** | The unresolved choice of which candidate local interaction becomes the first actual durable mark |
| **Conditional continuation** | Evolution after an earlier mark is specified; this can explain track persistence without deriving the first mark |
| **Local track registry** | The ordered material history of marks along one detector track |
| **Shared entanglement registry** | Paper 1's stronger nonlocal registry proposal; it must not be inferred merely from a local track |

## Paper 1 — Born Selection

### Placement A: after the three-stage definition in §2

Target: [Paper 1 §2](/Users/john-bramble/Projects/Physics/DiracKuramotoFramework/drafts/PAPER1_DRAFT_born_selection.md:37).

**Author-reviewed candidate passage — accepted in principle**

<user_quoted_section>For present purposes, it is useful to distinguish two limiting forms of measurement. In a destructive absorptive event, the incident wave may first create a distributed, reversible detector excitation, but the travelling excitation is ultimately exhausted when one detector channel commits. In a typical track-forming vertex, scattering or ionization transfers only part of the traveller's energy and momentum, and the traveller continues in a modified state. A visible track is therefore a sequence of partial interaction vertices and local records, not one prolonged whole-quantum capture. The single-quantum detector mechanism developed here is physically instantiated for completed absorptive events. Its martingale share lemmas are more general, but applying them to a non-absorptive track vertex would require a separate derivation of the vertex stakes, their conservation and dropout law, the commitment hazard, and one-world exclusivity; the present paper does not supply that derivation. The unresolved first actual mark therefore requires its own selection law, while correlations among later candidate records can be studied conditionally from the material history already formed.</user_quoted_section>

**Review record:** John approved the two-class scope correction and then approved the cold review's distinction between the general mathematical lemmas and their present absorptive physical realization. See the [independent placement review](../paper1-placement-review).

**Why this belongs here:** it scopes the paper before the notation makes the whole-quantum energy ledger appear universal.

### Placement B: qualify the energy-share notation and P4

Targets: [Paper 1 notation](/Users/john-bramble/Projects/Physics/DiracKuramotoFramework/drafts/PAPER1_DRAFT_born_selection.md:39) and [P4](/Users/john-bramble/Projects/Physics/DiracKuramotoFramework/drafts/PAPER1_DRAFT_born_selection.md:53).

**Passage B1 — author-reviewed candidate qualification**

<user_quoted_section>In §§3–5, the quantities called site energies and shares belong to the paper's physical model of a completed single-quantum absorptive event. The abstract martingale results may have wider applications, but these site shares should not automatically be interpreted as the energy ledger of a non-absorptive track vertex. At such a vertex, conservation must include the incoming and continuing traveller, detector excitation and recoil, emitted quanta, and environmental degrees of freedom. A small energy transfer can nevertheless create strong which-path information when the material and environmental states associated with the alternatives become sufficiently distinguishable. The present paper does not derive the track-specific stakes or their selection dynamics.</user_quoted_section>

**Review record:** John approved this replacement after the independent review found that the earlier §§3–6 scope was too broad and its three-bin track ledger incomplete.

**Passage B2 — author-reviewed P4 scope paragraph**

<user_quoted_section>**Scope of P4.** This discrete–continuum distinction classifies completed absorptive registration channels; it is not an exhaustive classification of every interaction available in the same material. An atom or solid may also support elastic scattering, partial excitation, or ionization in which the incident traveller survives. Such a track-forming vertex lies outside the present absorptive taxonomy and requires its own continuing-traveller ledger and commitment law. Discrete versus continuum describes the spectrum relevant to the absorptive channel, whereas destructive versus non-destructive describes what happens in the particular interaction event.</user_quoted_section>

**Review record:** John approved retaining P4 within the absorptive model while treating track-forming interactions as a separate, not-yet-derived channel class.

### Placement C: after Stage 1's exclusivity discussion

Target: [Paper 1 §3](/Users/john-bramble/Projects/Physics/DiracKuramotoFramework/drafts/PAPER1_DRAFT_born_selection.md:73).

**Author-reviewed candidate passage**

<user_quoted_section>A track detector supplies a useful non-absorptive boundary case. The projectile can become jointly correlated with candidate ionizations at several locations while continuing in the corresponding wave components. Mott's multi-atom wave analysis shows that the joint ionization amplitude is strongly concentrated on approximately collinear configurations. Once an earlier ionization location is specified, the conditional support for later candidate records lies mainly along a compatible ray. This explains the geometry of a track after an earlier location is specified, but it does not explain why one candidate first ionization becomes the durable actual beginning of one observed track, or why the alternatives occur with their observed frequencies. In the present framework, that missing first actual mark is the track-forming version of the Born-selection problem.</user_quoted_section>

**Review record:** John approved the Mott-specific joint-amplitude language and the distinction between a candidate ionization and a durable actual mark.

### Placement D: add a focused open problem in §9.4

Target: [Paper 1 open problems](/Users/john-bramble/Projects/Physics/DiracKuramotoFramework/drafts/PAPER1_DRAFT_born_selection.md:351).

**Author-reviewed candidate open problem**

<user_quoted_section>**First-mark selection in a repeated-scattering detector.** Construct an unconditional two-rail, three-layer field–absorber model in which each record-history component can contain up to three sequential partial energy transfers and the complete state obeys an explicit energy ledger. Begin with the globally unitary calculation, without an outcome-conditioned trajectory, and test exact inversion of the microscopic record-like excitations. Audit whether the result is only a superposition of correlated histories, distinguishing one-excitation conservation within each history from one actual history. Only then ask whether an additional Born-free ontology or dynamics can identify one first actual mark while preserving norm, energy, and finite-time exclusivity. No-signaling should be tested later in a spatially separated extension with independent settings.</user_quoted_section>

**Review record:** John approved the branchwise-transfer language, the explicit actuality audit, and deferring no-signaling to a model capable of testing it.

### Claims to narrow in Paper 1

- For a completed absorptive event, state that the whole incident excitation is removed from the travelling field and its energy is accounted for across the correlated detector and bath products associated with the registered channel. Do not require that energy to remain at one microscopic site. Give reflected, transmitted, scattered, and absorbed-without-registration alternatives separate named ledgers.
- Do not describe later track alignment as a derivation of the first-mark weight.
- Do not call an ordinary conditional trajectory a selection mechanism; its outcome weighting is already Born-conditioned.
- Keep the local track registry separate from P5's nonlocal shared registry.

## Paper 2 — Heisenberg Cut

### Placement A: refine the cut table in §4.1

Target: [Paper 2 §4.1](/Users/john-bramble/Projects/Physics/DiracKuramotoFramework/drafts/PAPER2_DRAFT_heisenberg_cut.md:81).

**Candidate replacement principle**

| Detector event | What crosses the proposed cut | What continues afterward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Destructive absorption | One detector channel completes the absorptive commitment | No incident travelling excitation remains |
| Track-forming vertex | A local ionization or scattering record becomes operationally unrecoverable | A lower-energy, phase-shifted, possibly deflected traveller continues |

The current wording “one site assembles the full quantum” should be explicitly labeled as the absorptive class, not the universal definition of lock completion.

### Placement B: revise the track row in §4.2

Target: [Paper 2 detector taxonomy](/Users/john-bramble/Projects/Physics/DiracKuramotoFramework/drafts/PAPER2_DRAFT_heisenberg_cut.md:106).

**Candidate passage**

<user_quoted_section>A particle track is a sequence of partial scatter/ionize–commit–register vertices. At each vertex the traveller and a local material degree of freedom become correlated; a portion of energy and momentum is deposited; and the traveller continues in a modified state. The size of the deposited energy and the strength of the which-path record are different quantities. A small recoil or ionization can strongly distinguish the alternatives even when most of the traveller's energy remains available for later marks.</user_quoted_section>

### Placement C: add a per-vertex recoverability paragraph

**Candidate passage**

<user_quoted_section>Recoverability must be asked at every vertex. Before a durable local record forms, exact global reversal can in principle restore the incoming traveller and erase the correlated material excitation. Echo or rephasing can recover coherence when the reference history is structured and controllable. With an uncontrolled reference history, operational recovery can fail even though the enlarged closed state remains unitary. After a track vertex crosses the proposed locking layer, the projectile may continue, but the earlier path phase is no longer operationally recoverable from the accessible degrees of freedom. The cut therefore advances along the track one local record at a time.</user_quoted_section>

This wording ties the new model directly to Paper 2's three reversal cases: exact global inversion, echo/rephasing, and random-reference failure.

### Placement B: correct the calorimetric sentence in §4.7

Target: [Paper 3 §4.7](/Users/john-bramble/Projects/Physics/DiracKuramotoFramework/drafts/PAPER3_DRAFT_dk_framework.md:450).

**Candidate replacement**

<user_quoted_section>The calorimetric audit depends on detector class. In a destructive single-quantum event detector, a completed absorptive event routes the full incident quantum to one detector channel. In a track medium, each vertex deposits only part of the traveller's energy and momentum; the balance remains in the continuing wave and must be carried into the next vertex. In both classes the budget must close locally and globally, but “one address receives the full quantum” applies only to the absorptive event.</user_quoted_section>

### Placement C: narrow the Many-Worlds comparison

Target: [Paper 3 §7.1](/Users/john-bramble/Projects/Physics/DiracKuramotoFramework/drafts/PAPER3_DRAFT_dk_framework.md:698).

The strongest defensible distinction is not that the framework conserves energy while Many Worlds does not; unitary Everettian dynamics also conserves the global expectation of energy. The proposed advantage would be a **derived account of one actual material history**—for example one first mark followed by one evolving track—without discarding the wave or multiplying actuality. Until the first-mark law and the status of unselected amplitudes are derived, this remains the framework's goal rather than an achieved comparison.

## Acceptance conditions before manuscript insertion

1. Paper 1's theorems are explicitly scoped to the class they actually prove.
2. Paper 2's cut is defined by record recoverability, not by total absorption.
3. Paper 3 no longer universalizes the full-quantum-at-one-site sentence.
4. The local track registry is not used as evidence for the stronger nonlocal registry.
5. Conditional track persistence is not presented as a derivation of first-mark actuality.
6. Every partial mark has a closed energy and momentum ledger.

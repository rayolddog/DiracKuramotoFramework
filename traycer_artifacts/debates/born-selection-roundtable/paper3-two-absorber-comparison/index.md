# Paper 3 and the two-absorber first-mark test

## Verdict

The two-absorber model is the right microscopic test of Paper 3's **ambitious selection candidate**, but it does not test all of Paper 3 and it does not threaten the paper's more modest **inherited-outcome registration account**.

Paper 3 contains three levels that must stay separate:

1. Standard unitary/open-system physics produces capture amplitudes, bath-defined alternatives, decoherence, and conditional records.
2. The framework interprets those processes as capture, surface selection, and registration, while its defensible core inherits which record occurred.
3. A stronger candidate says real background fluctuations drive fair competition among local energy shares until first closure, while whole-quantum budget depletion enforces one winner.

The new calculation directly pressure-tests level 3. Its expected unitary baseline will also expose one sentence in Paper 3 that remains too strong: an attractor does **not** explain “exactly one outcome per run” without a conditioned record or an additional actuality law. Paper 3's own Appendix D already establishes this correction.

The manuscript's inherited-outcome core remains internally viable; the attractor-alone explanation of oneness does not.

## Priority findings

### Critical — a unitary two-absorber result can give energy exclusivity without unique actuality

In a one-excitation Hilbert space, the Hamiltonian may make a simultaneous A-and-B energy-bearing mark exactly impossible. The late state can nevertheless have the form

$$
|\Psi\rangle=c_0|\text{no mark}\rangle+c_A|M_A,B_A\rangle+c_B|M_B,B_B\rangle.
$$

There is no double-mark term, but there are still two alternative mark branches. This would validate the ordinary statement “one photon supplies at most one photon-scale excitation per branch.” It would **not** validate “one actual mark,” a nonlocal drain of remote amplitude, or a single-world registry.

This is the most important guardrail for reading the planned exclusivity audit. A calculated zero double-mark population may follow from excitation-number conservation or from how the model's state space was truncated. It counts as support for Paper 3's energy ledger, not as a derivation of its first-closure ontology.

### Critical — the unconditional baseline will falsify the attractor-alone claim, not the inherited account

If local baths produce durable, decohered A/B alternatives but no fact selecting A or B, that is the expected result of standard unitary dynamics. It would directly confirm Appendix D and the [first-mark audit](../cloud-chamber-spad-first-mark-comparison): decoherence can stabilize candidate histories without choosing one.

That outcome would falsify any reading of §3.3 in which bath dissipation or basin attraction by itself makes one outcome actual. It would not falsify Paper 3's stated core, because §§1.3, 3.3, 7.3, and 8 explicitly inherit the selected record and classify a background-driven choice as speculative.

### High — Rung 1 tests the premises of “first closure,” but excludes the paper's closure event

Paper 3 says Stage-2 energy loans remain provisional and the selection becomes final at Stage 3, with statistics defined by **first closure**, not first touch (§3.3, lines 670–679). The plan excludes amplification and registration from Rung 1. Its “durable mark” is therefore either:

- a true irreversible bath record, in which case it already plays much of Paper 3's Stage-3 closure role; or
- only a seed able to drive later amplification, in which case Rung 1 cannot yet test the paper's first-closure stopping rule.

The calculation can still test candidate shares, drift, return, and local energy accounting. It cannot claim to have validated first-closure statistics until its stopping event is mapped unambiguously to Paper 3's Stage 2 → 3 boundary.

### High — the martingale theorem is not the missing microscopic physics

Optional stopping is mathematically sufficient only after the model independently produces:

- a physically real nonnegative share at each absorber;
- pathwise conservation and zero drift;
- zero as an absorbing state rather than temporary depletion;
- finite fixation at exactly one winner; and
- initial shares equal to deposited energy shares without interpreting reduced-state populations as already-actual probabilities.

The plan's share, drift, return, and exclusivity audits target exactly these debts. An asymmetric splitter yielding reduced populations $|\alpha|^2$ and $|\beta|^2$ would validate amplitude-linear coupling and standard energy deposition. It would not yet show that those populations are ontic stakes or winner frequencies.

### High — the conditional trajectory is a control calculation, not a Born derivation

Paper 3's stochastic master equation is useful as the standard operational answer: given a monitored record and the usual quantum measurement rule, a single trajectory localizes and repeated trajectories have Born frequencies. The plan correctly forbids using that unraveling as the derivation under test. Reduced master equations have many possible unravelings; selecting one sampled trajectory cannot tell us why that trajectory is the actual world.

The conditional result should therefore be used only as a benchmark for reduced-state and timing calculations. Reproducing it would validate numerical implementation, not the framework's added selection physics.

### High — a successful routing law would change Paper 3's ontology

Paper 3's through-line is globally unitary evolution with effective, information-theoretic collapse. The plan's continuous winner-routing option explicitly transfers norm or energy out of losing alternatives and is no longer strictly unitary. If such routing is required for one actual mark, it would be a substantive objective-reduction law, not merely the registration mechanism Paper 3 currently describes.

A passive registry is more compatible with Paper 3's uncollapsed real wave, but it inherits Bohm-like debts: its initial distribution, guidance/update law, the efficacy and stress-energy of empty absorbed components, and global exclusivity.

### Medium — this is not a test of the specifically chiral mechanism

Paper 3 itself concludes that ordinary electromagnetic detectors select the charge/position or which-path basis and that the chiral mass coupling is a “passenger,” not the pointer (§§3.7–3.8, lines 938–1026). A photon plus two ordinary absorbers therefore tests the generic first-mark/registry ontology and the companion fair-game premises. It does not validate the chiral $0/\pi$ basins, $\mathrm{Im},\Sigma$ as a mass-channel lock, or a Dirac-specific detector mechanism unless those variables and a scalar-sensitive coupling are added separately.

That limitation is productive: a null result against extra selection physics cannot be rescued by appealing to the chiral clock, because Paper 3 already says the laboratory pointer does not run on that clock.

## Clean status separation

### Standard physics

- One-photon field–matter coupling, reversible excitation, named bath energy transfer, and outgoing/no-absorption modes.
- Unitary evolution of field + absorbers + baths; decoherence after tracing out baths.
- Bath-defined pointer alternatives and reservoir-powered downstream amplification.
- Conditional quantum trajectories and Born-weighted operational predictions once the standard measurement rule is used.

### Framework hypotheses

- A per-run background field is an ontic selector rather than merely meter noise.
- Local deposited energy shares are actual competing stakes.
- Surface noise makes their exchange exactly drift-free.
- Whole-quantum depletion or a shared registry enforces one finite-time winner across separated absorbers.
- A preferred foliation carries this enforcement without enabling signaling.

### Open derivations the model can expose

- Deriving the share variables and their stochastic kernel from the closed microscopic Hamiltonian.
- Showing zero drift under realistic, not idealized, detector and bath asymmetries.
- Making zero absorbing while allowing the known reversible-capture window.
- Distinguishing branchwise energy conservation from single-world actuality.
- Supplying finite exclusivity, explicit currents or registry updates, and a no-signaling proof.
- Mapping “durable mark” to the exact stopping/closure boundary used by the theorem.

## Bottom line for learning

The two-absorber model asks the right question because it removes the avalanche, electronics, and large-detector language that can make selection look solved downstream. The central lesson is:

<user_quoted_section>Standard dynamics can explain why the alternatives have weights, why they become distinguishable, why each branch conserves energy, and why a conditioned record persists. It does not, without an added ontic rule, say which durable mark is the one actual history.</user_quoted_section>

The model can therefore validate the physical premises beneath Paper 3's fair-game proposal, or falsify them one by one. It cannot convert reduced-state weights into actuality merely by reproducing $|\psi|^2$, and it should not count branchwise single-excitation bookkeeping as a one-world winner.

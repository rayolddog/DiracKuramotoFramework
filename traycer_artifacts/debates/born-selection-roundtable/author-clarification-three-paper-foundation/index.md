# Author clarification — three-paper foundation and detector microphysics

## Author's intended progression

John M. Bramble intends the three papers to form a foundation in stages:

1. **Born Selection:** explain why a physically distributed wave-energy pattern yields one registered outcome with Born weights.
2. **Heisenberg Cut:** locate the transition from reversible microscopic interaction to irreversible macroscopic registration.
3. **Dirac–Kuramoto Framework:** assemble the deeper spinor-clock, locking, preferred-frame, and one-world ontology supported by the first two papers.

The Born paper was separated because outcome probabilities have been the hardest part to fit into the framework.

## Physical intuition being preserved

- Computer-generated holography makes the quadratic relation between wave amplitude and deposited/recorded energy feel physically primary rather than probabilistic.
- Classical wave optics correctly predicts much of the propagation and first-order interference in Young-type experiments.
- Photons are treated in the framework as the uncoupled or massless limit; mass-coupled Weyl-spinor clock dynamics is therefore expected to be less directly visible in ordinary photon propagation.
- Measurement is intended to involve the incident wave, detector-electron wavefunctions embedded in condensed matter, and electromagnetic vacuum/environment fields at the detector surface.
- Vacuum and detector microstates are proposed as the source of apparently stochastic, mostly infinitesimal perturbations. Applied electromagnetic gradients may deliberately alter those detector conditions.

This photon statement is a framework interpretation, not standard particle classification: standard theory treats the photon as a massless spin-1 field. Maxwell's equations can be written in a helicity-separated, Weyl-like Riemann–Silberstein form, but that mathematical analogy does not make a photon a pair of spin-½ Weyl spinors. The distinction should be explicit in Paper 3.

## Why reviewers still request a microscopic model

The manuscript already contains a **mechanism sketch**:

`|a_i + δb_i|² = |a_i|² + 2 Re(a_i* δb_i) + |δb_i|²`.

This explains why an uncontrolled field can produce a zero-mean cross term whose scale is linear in site amplitude. It does not yet determine the full stochastic evolution. In the reviewers' usage, a microscopic model additionally specifies:

1. the incident-field, detector-electron, lattice/bath, and amplification degrees of freedom;
2. their Hamiltonian or equivalent dynamical law and initial state;
3. the reduction from those dynamics to effective site stakes `e_i(t)`;
4. the resulting drift, covariance, spatial correlations, memory, and complete energy ledger;
5. why zero is absorbing or how a first winner disables competing registrations;
6. why the registration hazard is linear in an ontically defined stake without using a Born probability in the derivation; and
7. how an applied electromagnetic gradient changes the derived coefficients while ordinary efficiency, gain, heating, and band-structure effects are controlled.

The review therefore does not say that vacuum and detector microphysics were omitted. It says the present expansion supplies a scaling argument but not yet the effective conserving generator and boundary law used by the probability theorem.

## Important ontology distinction

Two meanings of “knowing the vacuum wavefunction” must remain separate:

- In standard quantum electrodynamics, the ideal vacuum is itself a specified quantum state. Knowing that state gives correlation functions and probability distributions, not a definite classical field history for an individual detector event.
- If the framework posits a finer ontic vacuum microstate whose exact value would determine the outcome, that is a deterministic completion beyond the ordinary quantum-state description. It may be developed, but it must be stated explicitly and connected to the preferred-frame/nonlocal-registry commitments in U7.

Thus ignorance can explain why predictions are practically stochastic only after the theory identifies what objectively exists but is unknown. Ignorance by itself does not choose one outcome from a unitary entangled state.

## Recommended claim architecture

The intended three-paper strategy remains coherent if the claim levels are made explicit:

| Paper | Defensible role now | Required bridge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Born Selection | Conditional individual-selection program: a chosen ontology supplies a microstate preparation measure, while the standard quantum framework separately supplies the outcome comparator and a fixed field–matter selector maps each microstate to one irreversible record | Demonstrate conditional outcome compatibility without putting the comparator into preparation, coupling, sampling, thresholds, invalid-event handling, or analysis; reserve equilibrium preservation for a separate same-space equivariance theorem |
| Heisenberg Cut | Proposed boundary between reversible capture and irreversible registration | Derive one continuous detector model across the locking layer |
| Dirac–Kuramoto Framework | Ontological and dynamical completion tying wave realism, clocks, locking, and registry together | Show when spinor structure is load-bearing and recover the effective Born/Heisenberg models |

This positioning preserves the three-paper foundation while avoiding the claim that a quantum outcome measure automatically defines detector microstates. Paper 1 may test an individual selector conditional on one explicitly premised microstate preparation law; a later, separate typicality, equivariance, or relaxation argument would be needed to justify calling that law quantum equilibrium. The detailed revision is recorded in the [quantum-equilibrium selection plan](../quantum-equilibrium-selection-revision).

## Author-proposed revision direction — stochastic seed and amplification cascade

John proposes that a later revision should:

- explain the three-paper progression explicitly;
- state that each microscopic stochastic perturbation is ordinarily very small;
- expand the physical sequence beyond the detector surface; and
- describe amplification as a deliberately biased, detector-dependent process.

The smallness claim needs a precise qualification: the **energy or phase kick per microscopic correlation time** may be tiny, while its consequence is not. Repeated exchange and a metastable amplifier can convert a small symmetry-breaking seed into an order-one macroscopic record. Thus the paper should call the seed small, not call its role negligible.

### Proposed causal cascade

1. **Propagation:** the incident wave develops its spatial amplitude and phase pattern.
2. **Distributed capture:** candidate sites acquire reversible polarization/excitation stakes proportional to the local quadratic field measure.
3. **Microscopic selection:** detector, bath, and vacuum fields produce many small exchange increments; the conserving, zero-drift limit is the claimed fair game.
4. **Commit seed:** one localized degree of freedom enters an irreversible or effectively irreversible channel. This is the latest point at which outcome weights may be set without amplification bias.
5. **Biased amplification:** electric fields, impact ionization, quasiparticle multiplication, vortex entry, dynode gain, or another device-specific instability strongly favors growth of the committed seed.
6. **Macroscopic registration:** electronics cross a discrimination threshold and write the record.
7. **Quench and reset:** the detector dissipates energy, becomes temporarily insensitive, and returns to its ready state.

The amplification bias is not automatically a defect. An amplifier must be strongly biased toward turning a microscopic seed into a stable record. The load-bearing requirement is a **causal firewall**: after conditioning on a valid commit seed, the gain must primarily alter detection efficiency, latency, jitter, and pulse size—not retrospectively alter which candidate site supplied the seed. If amplification begins before selection is isolated, spatially varying gain or threshold can distort the observed outcome distribution.

### Quantities a revision should expose

- stochastic increment scale per correlation time relative to the quantum energy;
- correlation time and spatial correlation length of the effective noise;
- selection/commit time compared with amplification-onset and drain times;
- site-dependent coupling, threshold, and amplification efficiency;
- probability of no click, double click, afterpulse, and dark count;
- feedback from the growing amplifier into uncommitted sites; and
- response under electromagnetic-gradient reversal, separated from ordinary changes in absorption, carrier transport, gain, heating, and readout threshold.

This proposed addition remains planning guidance. No manuscript text has been changed.

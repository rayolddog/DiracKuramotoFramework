# Analogy and experimental design — the raised platform, repaired and tested

*Participant: analogy-and-experimental-design (Claude Fable 5). Sources read: `single_photon_derivation.md`, the [Born Selection final synthesis](../../../born-selection-roundtable/final-synthesis), the [photon/absorption bridge note](../../../born-selection-roundtable/photon-dirac-and-absorption-bridge), and standard SPAD device literature (McIntyre triggering probability, Chynoweth ionization, Cova et al. quenching-circuit reviews). No manuscript edits.*

## 1. Reading the analogy charitably first

John's picture: a distributed wave rolls along below a raised platform. Ordinary undulations never reach it. Occasionally a rare local peak rises above the platform's level; the energy above the platform is skimmed off into one channel; the rest of the wave continues or disperses.

Taken seriously, this is a **threshold-crossing picture with an energy-routing rule**, and the right question is not "is it true?" but "**which precise model is it, and at which stage of the cascade does it live?**" My central finding is that the SPAD contains **three distinct thresholds**, and the raw analogy conflates them. Separated, one third of the analogy is *literally correct standard physics*, one third is *engineering triviality*, and one third is *exactly the framework's open problem* — which is useful, because it tells us where the discriminating experiments must aim.

## 2. Event-stage placement: the three platforms

The shared cascade, with the analogy's platform located at each candidate stage. (Stage numbering follows `single_photon_derivation.md`.)

| Stage | Physical variable | Energy source/sink | Reversible? | Stochasticity | Bias role | Status |

**Placement verdict.** Platform B — the avalanche self-sustaining threshold — is a real, bias-tunable, EM-derived threshold, and the analogy describes it *well*. Platform C is a comparator level and needs no philosophy. Platform A — selection of which site absorbs — is where John *wants* the analogy to work, and there the platform is not yet a model: it is the name of the missing selection law. Everything discriminating in Section 7 is aimed at Platform A.

A clarifying fact that pins Platform B's character: **dark counts and afterpulses cross Platform B with no incoming photon at all.** A thermally or tunnel-generated carrier, or a trap-released one, triggers an avalanche indistinguishable from a photon-seeded one. So Platform B is seed-origin-blind: a click certifies only "a seed survived commitment," never "a photon arrived." All photon-specific information — which mode, which site, Born weights — is settled *upstream* of Platform B. This is the cleanest argument that selection and commitment are different stages and must not share one platform in the final picture.

## 4. Candidate precise models: what each preserves and breaks

Six candidate formalizations of "platform," ordered from worst to best fit.

| Model | Core mathematics | Preserves from the analogy | Breaks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Threshold clipping / level-crossing** (Rice statistics) | count upcrossings of level $u$ by a stochastic field; clip what exceeds $u$ | rare-peak imagery; "energy above the platform" | Everything quantitative. A clipper on classical intensity predicts (i) *fractional* energy removal, (ii) *multiple simultaneous* crossings, (iii) click rate set by the tail of the local intensity distribution — generally **not** linear in $\\lvert\\beta\\rvert^2$. Falsified by Born linearity and antibunching (Section 5, F1–F4). |
| **Nonlinear focusing / rogue wave** (NLS, modulational instability, Peregrine solitons) | nonlinearity self-focuses a broad wave into a giant peak | explains *why* a rare peak forms at all — the most attractive part of John's picture | One-photon fields in a photodiode have no known nonlinearity at the $\\hbar\\omega$ scale; deterministic nonlinear selection generically breaks Born linearity and enables signaling. Survives **only** in the stochastic, norm-conserving form — at which point it *is* the martingale model below, and "rogue wave" is its poster. |
| **Nucleation** (critical nucleus in a metastable medium) | seed above critical size grows by releasing the medium's stored free energy | the single best classical picture for **Platform B**: overbias ↔ supersaturation, $n_c$ ↔ critical nucleus, avalanche ↔ growth powered by stored energy, quench ↔ relieving the supersaturation | says nothing about *which* site — classical nucleation happens at many sites at once (needs the exclusivity rule imported); lives post-selection, so it cannot be the Platform-A mechanism |
| **Absorbing boundary** | a diffusing/branching variable is removed on first contact with a boundary | "removal into one channel," irreversibility at the edge, all-or-nothing removal (the boundary absorbs the *walker*, not a fraction of it) — this is the correct repair of the energy-skimming clause | by itself has no dynamics — it needs a process to absorb; it is the boundary condition of the next two models rather than a mechanism |
| **First passage / escape** (Kramers, optional stopping) | first time a stochastic process hits a threshold; two-basin escape | stochastic *timing*, exclusivity by stopping ("first crossing wins"), and — decisively — the Born Selection martingale lemma is *exactly this*: conserving shares $s_i$, absorbing boundaries at $s=0$ and $s=1$, linear hazard ⇒ first-passage probabilities equal initial weights | honest at both stages: at Platform B the noise and drift are EM-derived (done); at Platform A they are the open hypothesis — first passage is the *shape* of the answer, not yet the answer |
| **Branching escape** (Galton–Watson extinction vs. survival) | $P_{\\rm trig} = 1 - q$, $q$ the smallest root of $q = F(q)$ | this is not an analogy — it **is** Platform B, per `single_photon_derivation.md`, with $\\gamma(V)$ from Chynoweth + drift and the cut at the triggering threshold | not transferable to Platform A: it presupposes one seed already exists, i.e. selection already happened |

**Disposition.** The raw analogy is closest to threshold clipping, which is the one model the data rule out. The *repairable* content lives in the last three rows: **absorbing-boundary first passage** for Platform A (in martingale-share form) and **nucleation / branching escape** for Platform B. "Rogue wave" survives only as the informal name of the stochastic focusing that a Platform-A first-passage process would look like from outside.

## 5. Failure modes of the raw analogy

Each failure mode is stated with the observation that enforces it, so the repaired version knows its fences.

- **F1 — Fractional energy removal.** "Energy above the platform is skimmed" implies sub-quantum deposits. But absorption is all-or-nothing per event: the liberated carrier receives the full transition energy, and heralded single photons at a beam splitter show $g^{(2)}(0) \to 0$ — one photon never yields two partial clicks. Ensemble-averaged fractional flows are fine (that is what $\lvert\beta_i\rvert^2\hbar\omega$ means); event-by-event fractions are not.
- **F2 — No exclusivity.** A water platform can be crossed at two places at once. The detector cannot: one photon, one click, everywhere, including between space-like separated pixels sharing one mode. Any Platform-A model needs a *global stopping rule* — the same nonlocal-registry obligation the Born synthesis logged as U7. The analogy does not supply it; it must be added and given a finite law.
- **F3 — Linear waves do not focus.** In linear evolution a broad one-photon amplitude does not concentrate into rogue peaks; genuine nonlinear focusing at one-photon energy scales is absent from the device physics, and if present would spoil linear photon-number counting (F4). The peak-forming step must therefore be stochastic (bath-driven), not self-focusing.
- **F4 — Peak height is the wrong statistic.** If clicks happened when a local field peak exceeded a fixed height, click probability would follow the *tail* of the local-amplitude distribution (Rayleigh/Tayfun-like) — a nonlinear function of $\lvert\beta\rvert^2$. Measured detection is strictly linear: $P_{\rm click} = \eta_{\rm abs}P_{\rm trig}\lvert\beta\rvert^2$ over many decades of attenuation. Born linearity is a brutal constraint: among the candidate models only the conserving-martingale first passage passes it *by construction* (optional stopping), which is why it is the only viable formalization of Platform A.
- **F5 — The splash is not the wave's water.** The click's macroscopic energy ($10^5$–$10^6$ carriers accelerated by the bias field) comes from the power supply, not the photon. Any telling of the analogy in which the crest's own energy makes the recorded signal misassigns the ledger by five to six orders of magnitude.
- **F6 — A single platform with one height.** The real device has a bias-tunable commitment threshold (B) and, hypothetically, a selection criterion (A) that standard physics says is *not* bias-tunable beyond local efficiency. Merging them predicts, wrongly or interestingly, that selection statistics track overbias — which is precisely what Section 7 is built to test rather than assume.
- **F7 — "Disperses into the vacuum."** Not permitted as a sink. Every no-click joule has a named home: unabsorbed light exits in transmitted/reflected/scattered field modes; above-gap excess thermalizes to phonons; extinct-avalanche kinetic energy thermalizes to phonons; quench energy goes to the circuit. Note the two *physically distinct* no-click channels this exposes — **non-absorption** (the whole photon leaves; the "wave continues") and **absorbed-but-extinct** (the whole photon is gone, thermalized, and no click ever forms). The raw analogy conflates them; they are separately measurable (Test T6).

## 6. The strongest repaired analogy

Keep John's landscape but re-plumb it. I offer this as the teaching version the collaboration could standardize on:

<user_quoted_section>The dam with one spillway and many inlets. A reservoir sits behind a dam, charged not by the incoming waves but by a pump that has been filling it all along (the bias supply holding the junction above breakdown). The incoming disturbance is a broad, shallow ripple spread across many inlets at the dam's face (the one-photon amplitude spread over absorption sites).
Stage A (the open physics): the inlets play a fair lottery for the ripple. Fairness is exact: each inlet's chance equals its share of the ripple's weight, because the lottery is a conserving game stopped at first passage — no inlet can gain odds without another losing them (the martingale share process; Born by optional stopping). One inlet wins the whole ripple; the others end with exactly nothing. What is drawn into the winning inlet is the entire quantum — the platform is an absorbing boundary for the ripple itself, not a skimmer of its crest. If no inlet wins during the transit, the whole ripple rolls on intact.
Stage B (standard physics): the winning inlet's trickle must still climb the spillway lip ($n_c$). Whether it does is a branching gamble — most trickles die on the lip (avalanche extinction; no click despite absorption). Overbias lowers the lip. Once over, the flood that roars down the spillway is reservoir water, not ripple water: the ripple only pulled the pin. The flow gauge downstream (comparator) reads every flood at the same saturated size, so the record keeps no memory of the ripple's shape — one bit out.
Reset: the gate is slammed (quench), the pump refills the reservoir, and for a moment the dam is blind (dead time); occasionally silt shaken loose by the last flood trips the gate again (afterpulse), and sometimes groundwater seepage does (dark count) — the spillway cannot tell pin-pullers apart.</user_quoted_section>

What this version fixes, clause by clause: energy skimming → whole-quantum absorbing boundary (F1); one platform → lottery plus lip, A separated from B (F6); the splash → reservoir-powered (F5); peak height → fair-lottery shares, guaranteeing Born linearity (F4); focusing → stochastic lottery, no nonlinearity needed (F3); vacuum sink → every stream named (F7). What it still owes — honestly labeled, not hidden: the lottery's *physical implementation* (what are the shares ontically, what noise drives them, what enforces the global stop across space-like separated inlets — F2/U7). The repaired analogy is a picture of the *theorem the framework still has to prove*, and says so.

## 7. Test matrix: discriminating calculations and measurements

Design logic: standard physics predicts detection factorizes as $P(\text{click at }i) = \lvert\beta_i\rvert^2 \cdot \eta_i(V_i)$, with *all* bias dependence confined to the local detector factor $\eta_i = \eta_{\rm abs}(V)P_{\rm trig}(V)$. The framework's live hypothesis — bias or field gradients participating in *selection* — would appear as a violation of this factorization or of Born linearity after the detector factor is independently calibrated. Every test therefore has a calibration leg (localized light, one pixel at a time) and a discrimination leg (delocalized single mode, shared).

Confound budget carried by all tests: $\eta_{\rm abs}(V)$ shifts with depletion width and Franz–Keldysh absorption-edge movement; $P_{\rm trig}(V)$ per McIntyre; dark-count rate rises steeply with field (band-to-band and trap-assisted tunneling); afterpulsing (control: hold-off time sweeps and double-pulse characterization); timing jitter shrinks with overbias (faster buildup); self-heating shifts $V_{\rm br}$ (control: low duty cycle, temperature servo, in-situ $V_{\rm br}$ tracking between runs).

*Summary for the round: the raised-platform analogy is three thresholds wearing one coat. Commitment (avalanche self-sustainment) is a literal, bias-tunable platform and the analogy is good physics there — best formalized as nucleation/branching escape. Registration is a comparator. Selection is the open problem, and the only formalization that survives Born linearity, antibunching, and the energy ledger is a conserving-share first-passage process with absorbing boundaries — the "fair lottery at the dam's inlets." The discriminating experiments all take one shape: calibrate every local bias dependence to death, then ask whether a delocalized single photon distributes its clicks by anything other than local weight × local efficiency.*

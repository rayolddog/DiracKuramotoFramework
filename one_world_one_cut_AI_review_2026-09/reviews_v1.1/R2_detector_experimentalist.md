## 2. Summary

The manuscript states an interpretation with four commitments — a real wave (for more than one quantum, a real state on configuration/Fock space), one world, a Heisenberg cut that is the decoherence crossover at the absorption vertex of a detector site with a computed location and regime, and outcome selection as a stochastic quantum jump at that vertex — and prices it. It concedes, in v1.1, that the cut's placement and width are decoherence theory's own; that in-principle recoverability ends only by postulate; that the selection law is the quantum-jump unravelling of photodetection with the Born branch weights and a nonlocal null-conditioned renormalization put in; and that it needs a preferred foliation it chooses rather than derives. Its claimed single result is a negative one: a real-wave, one-world programme with no new constant, forced to reproduce quantum mechanics, is pushed to the projection postulate's branch weights, and nothing in a detector's record, amplifier, or cascade, nor a synchronization substrate, can move those weights. No prediction differs from quantum mechanics.

## 4. Major concerns

### M1. The headline half-width formula and the prose identity attached to it disagree; the coupling-set "location" is definition-dependent by a factor √3, and the manuscript states an identity that holds in no limit.

The abstract: "the crossover in detuning at which irreversible capture sets in has half-width √(Γ²/4 + 2K²), which is the coupling's population-transfer rate 2K where the coupling exceeds the record channel's rate Γ". §4.2 repeats it: "For Γ ≪ K the half-width is 2K". But √(Γ²/4 + 2K²) → √2·K ≈ 1.41K as Γ → 0, not 2K. Run A's own calibration rows show the mismatch: the record's midpoints are 1.54 and 1.28 at Γ/K = 0.2 and 1 against the formula's 1.42 and 1.50 (8 % and 15 % off), and neither is 2.

I re-implemented Run A's model (`gamma_regime_sweep.py`: 2 × 2 non-Hermitian propagation, rate-based midpoint from the echo) and varied the observation time the script fixes at t = 3. At Γ/K = 0.2 the rate-based midpoint is 1.54 (t = 3), 1.70 (t = 10), 1.41 (t = 30), 1.23 (t = 100), 1.19 (t = 300), converging on the exact long-time value 2K/√3 = 1.155K, which is the half-point of the photon-like dressed state's absorber occupation (1 − Δ/√(Δ² + 4K²))/2 read off the slow eigenvalue of H_eff. The 2K figure the manuscript quotes is a different quantity: the half-point of the time-averaged occupation 2K²/(Δ² + 4K²) starting from the photon state, which is what `RESULTS.md` F3 reported. So there are three numbers in the Γ ≪ K regime — 2K (short-time occupation average), √2·K (the fit at t = 3), 2K/√3 (long time) — and the manuscript asserts the first while printing the second. The exact eigenvalue half-point is also non-monotonic through the exceptional point Γ = 4K (it is 0.88K at Γ/K = 3), a region Run A's grid (0.2, 1, 10, 100, 1000) skipped.

None of this threatens the physics conclusion — the crossover is of order K and coupling-set for Γ ≪ K, and Γ/2 and record-set for Γ ≫ K, which is the regime of every deployed detector. But the abstract's quantitative headline is wrong as an identity and the paper says "verified" of a fit. **Fix:** state √(Γ²/4 + 2K²) as an interpolation accurate to 15 % at the record's observation times; delete "which is ... 2K"; state the coupling-set half-width as "of order K, between 2K/√3 and 2K depending on the observation time", and since §4.2 already says "the location is a statement about rates", say which rate. Add Γ/K ≈ 3–4 to the sweep or note the exceptional point.

### M2. §5.1 clause 3 still carries a finite-time bound from the model v1.1 discarded; it contradicts the same clause's "kinematic" exclusivity and §7's "a null retires nothing".

Clause 3: "every other amplitude of that photon vanishes — including in the other arm of an interferometer, metres away — within a time the record's model bounds at a thousandth of the opening time, orders of magnitude faster than light crosses the arms"; then, three sentences later, "the paper's 'stop' is the projection, not a dynamical race." Both cannot stand. The bound comes from `vertex_exclusivity_discriminator.py`, which runs the gated energy race — the hazard-on-local-drawn-energy model that Run B has just falsified — with a finite-speed switch-off, and finds τ_x ≤ 1.5 × 10⁻³ race units by requiring the double-click fraction to stay under g²(0) = 0.0023 from a heralded 505 nm SPDC source. Three problems, in my specialism:

(a) The number is a property of a model the manuscript no longer holds. In the unravelling adopted in v1.1 the conditional-state update after a jump is instantaneous on the foliation by construction; there is no "opening time" to take a thousandth of.

(b) g²(0) = 0.0023 for a heralded SPDC source is set by multi-pair emission at the pump power used, not by any failure of detector exclusivity. It is an upper bound on an exclusivity-delay contribution only in the trivial sense that the whole residual could be attributed to it; the true bound is tighter by however much of the 0.0023 the source's known pair statistics account for, and the number measures nothing about detectors.

(c) If a finite exclusivity delay is entertained at all, it is a finite collapse speed in the preferred frame. On the record's own silicon conversion (0.01–0.9 fs against port separations of 1 mm–1 m) that is v ≳ 3 × 10⁵ – 4 × 10⁶ c. That is precisely the quantity Salart et al. 2008 bounded from below (> 10⁴ c in the relevant frames) with a 24-hour Bell experiment — the very paper §7 cites while saying "a null retires nothing". A finite-speed collapse in a preferred frame is testable and has been tested; an instantaneous one is not. The manuscript must choose.

**Fix:** delete the bound and the "orders of magnitude faster than light" sentence; state that the conditional-state update is instantaneous on the chosen slicing by postulate and that this is what makes the Salart-type nulls uninformative. If a finite speed is kept for any reason, cite Salart et al. as the existing bound on it and correct §7 accordingly.

### M3. The transmon row of the width table contradicts the paper's own regime rule.

§4.2 introduces the proxy with a caveat: "Under the passive-absorber proxy K ~ Γ — defensible where the coupling to the field is the interaction that sets the linewidth, dropped where K is engineered independently — the width is Γ/ω" and then tabulates "10⁻⁷–10⁻⁶ for a transmon" using Γ = 1/T₂ (10⁴–10⁵ s⁻¹) and ω ≈ 3 × 10¹⁰ s⁻¹ (the long form's table makes the inputs explicit). A transmon absorbing a microwave photon does so through an engineered coupling g/2π ~ 50–200 MHz to a readout resonator or a waveguide; 1/T₂ is set by dielectric loss, two-level-system defects, and quasiparticles, and is not "the interaction that sets the linewidth" for the field being detected. By the paper's own regime statement this is Γ ≪ K, the half-width is 2K = 2g, and the share width is 2g/ω ~ 10⁻²–10⁻¹, four to five orders above the tabulated value. The same caution applies to a cavity- or waveguide-coupled quantum dot; Kuhlmann et al.'s transform-limited dots satisfy the proxy, so that row can stand with the caveat. The alkali and clock rows are safe either way, because for a free-space atom Γ ≳ K and the width is Γ/2ω to Γ/ω on both readings.

The manuscript already says the table "dissolves no puzzle on its own". **Fix:** drop the transmon row or recompute it with g; add a column giving each row's regime (Γ/K) so the reader can see which formula applies.

### M4. Run B rediscovers the semiclassical photodetection formula, and §7's corresponding falsifier is mis-stated.

Clause 2 says the v1.0 rule makes "a detector's click probability an exponential of intensity rather than linear (Run B ...)". A memoryless hazard linear in locally absorbed energy, driven by a classical pulse envelope, gives P(click) = 1 − exp(−ηI) — I confirmed that Run B's c = 0.05 columns follow it (0.834 at the 10° intensity implies 0.593 at the 45° intensity; the run gives 0.584). That is Mandel's semiclassical photodetection formula (Mandel 1958, *Proc. Phys. Soc.* 72, 1037; Mandel & Wolf 1995, ch. 9), whose inability to give sub-Poissonian statistics or g²(0) < 1 is the textbook evidence for field quantization (Kimble, Dagenais & Mandel 1977; Short & Mandel 1983; the Grangier et al. 1986 anticorrelation already cited). Run B is a correct and useful check that the v1.0 hazard is a semiclassical detection model, but "the record's own model forced the form" (§1, §5.1) overstates it: the form was forced by 1977.

Worse, §7's falsifier — "a single-photon detection efficiency nonlinear in intensity" — is not a falsifier of quantum mechanics. For a coherent-state input quantum mechanics predicts exactly the exponential, and every attenuated-laser detector calibration confirms it; every real detector is also nonlinear in intensity through dead time and pile-up. What would falsify the postulate is a click probability for a one-photon Fock state that is nonlinear in the branch weight |ψ_A|², or a coincidence statistic outside the Fock-space projection (which §7 does list). **Fix:** reword §7; in §5.1 and §5.2 say that Run B reproduces the semiclassical-detection result, and cite it.

### M5. The "one result" has textbook ancestors the paper does not cite, and its scope is stated more broadly than it is established.

§5.2: "nothing in the physics of a detector and no synchronization substrate can move the outcome weights, and the family of 'hazard at the absorber' mechanisms collapses onto the projection postulate." For any detector whose response is a POVM this is Gleason's theorem in its POVM form (Busch 2003, *PRL* 91, 120403; Caves, Fuchs, Manne & Renes 2004, *Found. Phys.* 34, 193): any consistent probability assignment on effects is Tr(ρE), i.e. the Born rule, and no efficiency, cascade, or amplifier — all of which are absorbed into E — can make the assignment non-affine in the state. For any detector response that is *not* a POVM (nonlinear in the state), Gisin 1989 and Polchinski 1991, both already cited, show it signals, and Run B shows the particular non-POVM model tried here gives semiclassical statistics. So the no-go, stated exactly, is: POVM ⇒ Born (Busch), non-POVM ⇒ signalling or wrong statistics (Gisin; Mandel), with the race package as one worked example of the second branch. Stated that way it is sound and the manuscript is entitled to it; stated as "this paper's one result", the novelty claim is inaccurate.

Also align the wording: §4.3 says correctly that registration "contributes no *interference-sensitive* weight" — an efficiency η(x, E) plainly does change the observed outcome frequencies, which is why detector calibration exists — while §5.2 and the abstract say the stronger "cannot move the outcome weights". Use the §4.3 wording throughout.

### M8. The regime number is unsourced, and K is undefined for the detectors it is applied to, by the paper's own words.

## 7. Rubric scores (1–5)

- **Novelty — 2.** What is new is a re-description: the projection postulate given an address at the decoherence crossover, with a regime, plus a dense-record requirement and the echo/flip distinction. The claimed "one result" is a worked instance of Busch's POVM-Gleason theorem and Gisin's no-signalling constraint, with Mandel's semiclassical formula as the failure mode, none of which is cited; the novelty claim as stated is inaccurate.
- **Internal consistency — 3.** Much repaired since v1.0, and the postulate is now the right formal object. Two contradictions remain in load-bearing places (M1: formula versus prose identity; M2: instantaneous projection versus a finite bound), and one table row contradicts the paper's own regime rule (M3).
- **Evidential grounding — 3.** Every number is traceable and the two new runs match the record to the figures quoted (Run A: 1.538, 1.284, 5.088, 50.009, 499.987 against the paper's 1.54, 1.28, 5.09, 50.0, 500.0; Run B: the nearer channel's win fraction equals its click probability at every angle at c = 1, delay 8, and the 36 % excess at half intensity is 0.584 against 0.43). The ties to the world are consistency tests quantum mechanics passes, as the paper concedes; the one number tied to a measurement (g²(0) = 0.0023) is misused.
- **Reproducibility — 4.** Predictions on record before the runs, scripts and raw outputs beside the results, run times of seconds to minutes. I reproduced Run A independently to three figures and extended it. The race package's own diagnostic-only label is carried.
- **Citation integrity — 4.** Every source I checked is real and characterized correctly where I could judge (Renema, Engel, Korzh, Minev, Katz, Murch, Hatridge, Böttger, Kuhlmann, Kjaergaard, Ludlow, Grangier, de Riedmatten, Afzelius, Zbinden, Stefanov, Salart, Gisin 1989, Polchinski, Srinivas–Davies, Kelley–Kleiner, Dalibard et al., Plenio–Knight). Korzh is used loosely; Norsen is uncited; Mandel and Busch are missing where they are load-bearing.

## 8. Overall assessment

**2 — weak but in scope.** v1.1 is honest about what it is (quantum mechanics with the projection postulate, priced) and its formal core is now correct; but its abstract's quantitative headline states an identity that holds in no limit, its postulate still contains a finite-speed remnant of the model it discarded that collides with an experiment it cites, a row of its one table contradicts its own regime rule, its one claimed result is uncited textbook material, and the record it points to has not been brought into line with it.

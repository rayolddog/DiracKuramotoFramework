## 2. Summary (to fix terms)

The paper affirms that quantum measurement has a third stage beyond entangling interaction and decoherence — one outcome in one world — and postulates it. The wave is real; for more than one quantum the beable is the quantum state on configuration/Fock space (§3.1). The Heisenberg cut is identified with the decoherence crossover (half-width √(Γ²/4 + 2K²) in detuning, coupling-set for Γ ≪ K and record-set for Γ ≫ K), of finite width, and located in the chain at the absorption vertex of a detector site, a placement the paper concedes is unverifiable by von Neumann's mobility result. The event is the quantum-jump unravelling of photodetection — jumps at rate Γ_i⟨ψ|P_i|ψ⟩ on the null-renormalized conditional state — read as what happens rather than as a calculational device (§5.1); this is the projection postulate at the vertex, and the paper says so. No-signalling is claimed from the linearity of the unravelling's Lindblad average; a preferred foliation orders spacelike-separated jumps and is recorded as a choice with covariant alternatives (§6). The paper's "one result" is a negative one, called a no-go: a real-wave, one-world programme with no new constant that must reproduce quantum mechanics is pushed to the projection postulate's branch weights, and no detector physics or synchronization substrate can move them (§5.2). No prediction differs from quantum mechanics.

That is rare and worth preserving.

This is a genuine repair (with the caveat of M2 below that the record did not supply it).

- **The transactional comparison (§2, §5.3) is now accurate and generous**: Kastner and Cramer's prior placement of the cut at absorption is credited, the Born-rule dispute is presented as open, and the possibilist ontology is distinguished on the paper's own first commitment.

## 4. Major concerns

### M1. The postulate is a law only relative to a system/record split in the Markov limit; it inherits the FAPP status of the cut it was introduced to sharpen, and it conflicts with the beable of §3.1.

The formal statement: "The matter–field state evolves by the stochastic master equation of a photodetection unravelling: between jumps, the no-jump Hamiltonian H − (iħ/2)Σ_i Γ_i P_i with P_i the site's absorbing projector, renormalized; at rate Γ_i ⟨ψ|P_i|ψ⟩ a jump onto site i's absorbed state."

(a) *The operators of the law are those of a reduced description.* Γ_i and the site basis are obtained by a system/bath split, the Born–Markov approximation and a trace over the record channel. The paper says so itself for the detectors it cites: for a band absorber "neither Δ nor a 'site' is given by the Hamiltonian, the site basis is einselected by the local phonon coupling" (§4.2). An einselected basis is an approximate, timescale-dependent feature of a reduced density matrix. So for silicon, a nanowire and a calorimeter — every detector in §7 — the P_i of the law has no referent given by the Hamiltonian. That is exactly the word "approximately" inside a fundamental law that Bell's *Against "measurement"* [Bell 1990, which the paper cites] objected to; §4.5 concedes the objection for the cut and §5.1 re-incurs it for the event.

(b) *The unravelling is not unique.* A Lindblad equation admits infinitely many unravellings: jump unravellings related by unitary mixing of the jump operators, and diffusive (homodyne) unravellings, each corresponding to a different detection scheme [Wiseman & Milburn 2010, ch. 4]. Reading *one* of them as "what happens" requires an argument the paper does not give; einselection supplies a pointer basis for ρ, not a privileged trajectory decomposition. The direct challenge is in print — Wiseman & Gambetta, "Are dynamical quantum jumps detector dependent?", *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 108, 220402 (2012) — and is not cited. The dispersive readouts the paper itself cites in §7 [Murch et al. 2013; Hatridge et al. 2013] are diffusive unravellings with no absorption vertex on the system; the paper's law does not say what happens there.

(c) *Outside the Markov regime the law is not merely approximate; it is unstatable.* Pure-state trajectories do not exist for general non-Markovian environments (Wiseman & Gambetta, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 101, 140401, 2008). The paper's "the record channel must be dense on the capture timescale" (§4.3) is therefore not a physical requirement for irreversibility but a condition on the statability of its own postulate, and "dense" is a matter of degree: the paper's own table says a 64-mode channel "agrees with 256 to 0.005 for t ≤ 3" and not at t = 10. Does a jump occur at 64 modes? At 16? The law has a vague application condition.

(d) *Which state is the beable?* §3.1: "the beable is the quantum state of the field, real, on that space" — the universal state, record channel included. §5.1's law governs the state with the record traced out. If the universal state is real, §5.1 is not a law for it (the universal state evolves unitarily until something else is said). If the conditional reduced state is real, the beable is relative to a split, and §3.1 is wrong as written.

(e) *Comparative consequence.* Bohm and GRW have exact laws at every scale; this paper's is the only account in §2 whose law exists only in a regime. The paper says of Bohm only "no event and no cut; this paper postulates an event", and never says why a regime-bound stochastic postulate is preferable to an exact deterministic one with the same price list (ψ on configuration space, nonlocality, a foliation).

**Fix.** Either (i) state a universal jump law — which will be a new theory with parameters and predictions, contrary to the abstract — or (ii) reframe the position honestly as *quantum mechanics with the projection postulate applied in the einselected site basis at the Markov rate: a ψ-ontic Copenhagen with a physically motivated placement*, and then argue directly against Bell's FAPP objection instead of conceding it in §4.5 and re-incurring it in §5.1. In either case: say what happens in the non-Markov regime; cite and answer Wiseman–Gambetta 2012 and 2008; reconcile §3.1 with §5.1 by saying which state is the beable; and answer "why not Bohm."

### M2. "Forced by the record" is overstated: the record excluded v1.0, the literature supplied v1.1, and nothing in the record tests v1.1.

§1: "its claim about selection (§5) is a postulate whose form the record's own models forced." §5.1: "The rule that reproduces quantum mechanics is the branch weight." Run B is a race with a classical energy hazard on a frozen clock grid; the quantum-jump unravelling was never run in the record. That v1.1 reproduces quantum mechanics for time-separated detectors is a theorem of the adopted formalism (Srinivas & Davies 1981), not a finding of the record, and negative evidence against one hazard does not *force* a particular replacement: every unravelling of the same Lindblad equation reproduces the same statistics (M1b), so the record forces nothing about which one is real. **Fix:** replace "forced" with "excluded the v1.0 form; the v1.1 form is taken from the photodetection literature, whose theorems reproduce the statistics, and is untested in the record."

### M3. The no-signalling claim is valid in substance and invalid as stated.

§5.1: "Its ensemble average is a Lindblad equation, linear in ρ, and therefore does not signal [Gisin 1989; Polchinski 1991]." Linearity of the *global* map is necessary but not sufficient: a Lindblad generator with a nonlocal jump operator (P_A ⊗ P_B), or a Hamiltonian coupling the wings, is linear in ρ and signals. What does the work is that the generator is a sum of site-local terms, so each wing's reduced state obeys a closed linear equation independent of the presence, placement and efficiency of detectors elsewhere — Gisin's condition applied to the *local* map. That premise holds for the paper's site-projector jumps but is not stated. The citations are also for the converse (nonlinearity permits signalling), which is fine but should be said. **Fix:** one sentence: "Because the Hamiltonian and every dissipator are local to a site, the reduced state of each wing obeys a closed linear equation, and its statistics are independent of what is done at the other wing [Gisin 1989]; this is the sense in which the unravelling does not signal." Add that the argument is written in the preferred frame and that the marginal statistics are frame-independent, so the foliation does not reintroduce signalling.

### M4. The foliation is unforced, mis-argued, and the paragraph contradicts itself.

§6: "for spacelike-separated jumps on one state a global ordering is needed to say which conditional state the second jump acts on." For the paper's own law this is false. Site-local jump operators and site-local no-jump generators commute at spacelike separation ([P_A, P_B] = 0; the renormalization is a scalar), so the conditional state on any later hypersurface, and the probability of any history of jumps and nulls, are independent of the order in which spacelike-separated jumps are applied. This is precisely why Aharonov & Albert 1984 and Myrvold 2002 — cited only as "see also" — conclude that collapse can be applied hyperplane by hyperplane without a preferred foliation, and why Tumulka 2006 can make a jump history covariant. What a foliation buys is not consistency of the jump rule but a fact about "the state now": it is a cost of §3.1's realism about a single global state, not of §5.1, and the paper should say so and then choose. Note also that the *rate* Γ_i⟨P_i⟩ "at a moment" is foliation-dependent bookkeeping while the history probabilities are not; if the paper wants the rate to be physical it must say that this is what the foliation is for.

The same paragraph then says both "By the paper's own no-signalling the foliation is unobservable in any correlation, exactly as Bohm's is" and "as written it is a low-energy Lorentz violation whose clearance of every established bound is owed; whether the frame is a vacuum rest frame (Lorentz violation under the full weight of the clock-comparison constraints) or a matter or radiation rest frame ...". These cannot both stand. An unobservable foliation is a violation of the symmetry of the *law* (Maudlin's point, [Maudlin 2011]) to which no clock-comparison bound applies; bounds apply only if something couples to the frame, which is the separate non-covariant H′ postulate that §7 disowns. Bohm's foliation is not "under the full weight of the clock-comparison constraints" and neither is this one.

Finally: with events at vertices the paper already has a flash ontology (M5), and for flashes Tumulka's construction shows covariance is available in the non-interacting case; open problem 2 is partly answered in the literature the paper cites.

**Fix:** delete the ordering argument; state the foliation as a metaphysical choice about a global state, with hyperplane-dependence (Fleming; Aharonov–Albert; Myrvold) and the flash construction on equal footing; delete the "bounds owed" sentence or restrict it explicitly to H′.

### M5. Configuration-space realism is adopted but neither coherently nor priced.

(a) *Disjunctive ontology.* §3.1: "For one quantum the wave is a physical field on spacetime: the Dirac field for matter, the Maxwell field for light. For more than one quantum the state of the field is a functional on configuration or Fock space." A one-quantum state is a sector of Fock space, not a different kind of thing; there is no separate "field on spacetime" beable for N = 1 unless the paper wants it for N > 1 too. And the photon "wave function" of §3.4 [Bialynicki-Birula 1996] is not a position amplitude of the kind the sentence needs. A state is a vector in Fock space, not "a functional on Fock space" (it is a functional on field-configuration space in the Schrödinger representation). Pick one ontology and state it.

(b) *PBR is mischaracterized.* "the ψ-ontic commitment is supported ... by the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph theorem [Pusey et al. 2012], whose conclusion concerns exactly the configuration-space state adopted here." PBR shows, within the ontological-models framework and under preparation independence, that ψ is *ontic* — a function of the ontic state λ. It does not show λ = ψ, and it says nothing about whether ψ lives on configuration space, on spacetime regions (Wallace & Timpson's spacetime state realism, which the paper cites for a different point), or supplements a primitive ontology. For a ψ-complete theory such as this one PBR is trivially satisfied and lends no support. Delete "exactly the configuration-space state" or restate as "is consistent with".

(c) *The price is not itemized.* The known cost of wavefunction realism is the recovery of a three-dimensional world of detectors and clicks [Maudlin, "Can the world be only wavefunction?", in Saunders et al. eds, *Many Worlds?*, 2010; Allori, Goldstein, Tumulka & Zanghì, *Brit. J. Phil. Sci.* 59, 353 (2008); Albert, *After Physics*, 2015; Ney, *The World in the Wave Function*, 2021]. The paper's "one world" needs an answer to "what, in three-space, is a click?" — a flash at the vertex, a matter density read off the state, or nothing but the state with a functionalist gloss. "The absorption vertex of a detector site" is a spacetime location, which suggests flashes; if so, say it, and then the relativistic-collapse literature on primitive ontology [Bedingham, Dürr, Ghirardi, Goldstein, Tumulka & Zanghì, *J. Stat. Phys.* 154, 623 (2014)] is the relevant comparison.

(d) The fermionic honesty is welcome and should stay.

**Fix:** choose (i) wavefunction monism with a functionalist recovery of three-space, or (ii) wavefunction plus flashes at jump events; state the price of the choice made; correct the PBR sentence.

### M6. §5.2's "no-go" is not a no-go, and its theorem-level content is already in the literature the paper cites.

The bold claim: "a real-wave, one-world programme with no new constant, forced to reproduce quantum mechanics, is pushed to the projection postulate's branch weights at the decoherence crossover; nothing in the physics of a detector and no synchronization substrate can move the outcome weights ... That is this paper's one result, and it is a no-go."

(a) It has no premises, no statement and no proof; it is a summary of three toy mechanisms on a phase-oscillator substrate at a budget the package itself labels `diagnostic_only`. In a philosophy-of-physics venue "no-go" names a theorem.

(b) Read as a physical claim, "nothing ... can move the outcome weights" is false by the paper's own record — the first-to-align rule moves them to exponent 1.5 — and read with the qualifier "forced to reproduce quantum mechanics" it is a tautology: whatever reproduces quantum mechanics has quantum mechanics' weights.

(c) The non-trivial content — that a one-world, no-signalling stochastic process reproducing quantum mechanics must be an unravelling of its linear dynamics and therefore carries Born weights — is a corollary of results the paper already cites or should: Gisin 1989 (a stochastic dynamics that does not signal has a linear mean), so a process that reproduces quantum mechanics' mean is an unravelling of its Lindblad/unitary dynamics; and Gleason 1957 (any noncontextual probability measure on the projection lattice is the Born measure). The downstream-POVM point of §4.3 (efficiency multiplies weights, cannot move phases) is standard detection theory [Kelley & Kleiner 1964, cited]. The simulations rediscovered, in a toy, a consequence of theorems.

**Fix:** retitle §5.2 "A negative result about hazard-at-the-absorber mechanisms"; state exactly which three mechanisms were tested and at what budget; credit Gisin and Gleason for the general statement; drop "no-go" and "nothing in the physics of a detector."

### M7. The §4.5 concession is not fully propagated.

- §4.1 still opens: "The cut is where *in-principle recoverability ends*: the first point in the chain at which no operation on the site and the field, however idealized, restores the pre-event configuration," and two sentences later "This is the irreversibility of the reduced dynamics." After §4.5 these are inconsistent: the reduced dynamics is irreversible for all practical purposes, environment excluded, recurrences not forbidden.
- §9 still lists as a gift to the postulate "a criterion that separates in-principle recoverability from the reversal an experimenter can apply." After the concession the criterion separates the reduced-dynamics echo from the coupling flip; "in-principle" should go.
- The abstract's "the Heisenberg cut is a physical crossover with a computable location and width and a physical address" sits beside §4.3's admission that "moving the event from the vertex to the amplifier within a decohered chain changes no prediction; 'the record is written later' is a statement the paper makes about its own ontology and the world cannot contradict." The abstract should say that the address is a stipulation.
- "Location" is used in two senses — the position in the chain (the vertex) and the position in detuning (Δ_1/2) — and the abstract's "a location for the event with its regime" conflates them. The first is unverifiable by the paper's own argument; the second is decoherence's.

### M9. Clause 3 retains a relic of the dynamical race it has abandoned.

§5.1 clause 3: "every other amplitude of that photon vanishes — including in the other arm of an interferometer, metres away — within a time the record's model bounds at a thousandth of the opening time, orders of magnitude faster than light crosses the arms" and, in the same clause, "the paper's 'stop' is the projection, not a dynamical race." The "thousandth" is the long-form §4.2 bound on a *dynamical* one-quantum stop from the Grangier coincidence ratio (2 × 10⁻³); a projection has no speed, and the paper now says exclusivity is kinematic for a one-photon state. If an empirical number is wanted it is Salart et al. 2008 (> 10⁴ c in the relevant frames). Delete the thousandth.

### M10. What remains for a philosophy-of-physics venue, and how to make it count.

After the concessions the position is: quantum mechanics with the projection postulate applied in the site basis at the Lindblad rate, plus configuration-space realism, plus a preferred foliation it does not need. That is a ψ-ontic Copenhagen with a placement, and the placement is admitted unverifiable. Three things would count, and none is yet done: (i) a direct argument that decoherence plus a realist reading of the site-basis unravelling is a coherent collapse ontology *despite* Bell's FAPP objection and unravelling non-uniqueness — the paper's actual philosophical thesis, currently asserted; (ii) the Run B result connected to single-photon nonlocality [Tan, Walls & Collett, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 66, 252 (1991); Hardy, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 73, 2279 (1994)]: a local hazard fails for unequal arms because the null at the near detector is a real nonlocal update — a genuine, teachable point; (iii) the negative result as a case study in method (pre-registration, wrong predictions kept), which `NEGATIVE_RESULT.md` already does better than §5.2.

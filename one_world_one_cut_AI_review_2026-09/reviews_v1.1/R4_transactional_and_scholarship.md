## 2. Summary (to fix terms)

The manuscript states, prices, and declines to derive an interpretation with four commitments: the wave is real (on configuration or Fock space for more than one quantum); there is one world; the Heisenberg cut is a decoherence crossover in detuning whose half-width the paper gives as √(Γ²/4 + 2K²) in the smallest capture-plus-record model, with the event assigned by postulate to the absorption vertex of a detector site; and outcome selection is the quantum-jump unravelling of photodetection read as what actually happens, one jump per quantum, with the Born branch weights and the nonlocal null-conditioned renormalization put in. The paper's one claimed result is negative: a synchronization-substrate programme that tried to derive the weights failed, and the paper generalizes this to the statement that no physics of the detector and no such substrate can move the outcome weights, so that a real-wave, one-world, no-new-constant programme is pushed onto the projection postulate at the decoherence crossover. Relative to the transactional interpretation, the nearest neighbour, the paper now concedes that RTI placed the cut at atomic absorption first, distinguishes possibilist from spacetime ontology, declines to accept the transactional Born-rule derivation, and admits it carries a foliation that TI claims not to need.

## 3. Strengths

- **The transactional concession is correct and specific.** §2's statement that RTI "already places the Heisenberg cut at the level of absorption by individual atoms or molecules with the coupling as the physical variable" is nearly verbatim from Kastner & Cramer (2018), whose closing paragraph speaks of "placement of the 'Heisenberg Cut' at the appropriate microscopic (or possibly mesoscopic) level of absorption by individual atoms or molecules" and identifies the coupling constant *e* as the amplitude for offer and confirmation generation. A first-round misclaim of priority has been withdrawn cleanly.
- **The possibilist/spacetime distinction is right.** "Kastner's offer and confirmation waves are elements of a space of possibilities, not fields on spacetime, so possibilist TI is not a neighbour on this paper's first commitment; Cramer's original is closer" is an accurate reading of Kastner 2012 and of the RTI footnote that "the RTI ontology involves actualization of possibles." Few comparisons in the non-TI literature get this right.
- **Numbers match the record, and the labels travel.** Every figure I checked — Run A midpoints (1.54, 1.28, 5.09, 50.0, 500.0 against 1.42, 1.50, 5.20, 50.0, 500.0), the 1.3–2.0 rate-based midpoint, the 1.7→5 fixed-time drift, the Bloch–Siegert coefficient range 1.1–1.7, the 2 % half-width constancy for ω/K ≥ 8, the 0.004/1.1 slopes, the 36 % excess and the "wins in every trial in which it fires" of Run B, the 1.5 and 2.1–2.2 exponents, the thousandth-of-commit-time exclusivity bound, the 0.04 coupling-flip return — is in the record's files at the value quoted, and the race numbers carry the `diagnostic_only` statement as promised.
- **The frame tests are withdrawn with the right citations.** Zbinden et al. 2001, Stefanov et al. 2002, and Salart et al. 2008 are the before-before, moving-beamsplitter, and 24-hour null experiments respectively, characterized correctly.
- **The concession of the in-principle recoverability claim (§4.5) is made in full**, with the Bennett distinction between erasure and reversal stated correctly.
- **Every reference I web-checked exists** with the volume and pages given, including the two arXiv-only items in the transactional exchange and the new Steck revision.

## 4. Major concerns

### M1. The Marchildon dispute is misattributed, and the misattribution inverts what RTI is credited with (specialism)

§2 states: "Kastner and Cramer claim to derive the Born rule for radiative processes from the requirement that a real photon needs both an emission and an absorption vertex; that derivation has been disputed [Marchildon 2017; Kastner 2017 reply], and this paper's view — that the identification of the confirmation amplitude with ψ* stipulates the square — is one side of that dispute, not a settled fact." §5.3 repeats it: "this paper reads the identification of the confirmation amplitude with ψ* as a stipulation of the square, which is Marchildon's side of an open dispute [Marchildon 2017; Kastner 2017]."

I read Marchildon's note in full. It does not dispute the Born-rule derivation and does not mention ψ*, complex conjugation, or the two-vertex product. Its abstract: "I briefly examine how RTI stands with respect to unitarity in quantum mechanics. I then argue that a specific proposal to locate the origin of nonunitarity is flawed, at least in its present form." Its three objections are (i) that microscopic non-unitarity has not been shown to explain macroscopic non-unitarity ("An explanation of the one by the other should be buttressed by detailed calculations. I am not aware of any such calculations"); (ii) that Kastner's own buckyball estimate predicts non-unitarity for sixty-atom systems that display interference; and (iii) that identifying the coupling amplitude *e* with the amplitude for confirmation-wave generation, and α with its probability, "is unlikely to hold as any general principle" because a hypothetical charge 12*e* would give a probability of 1.05, and colour charges exceed one in natural units. His conclusion: "the quantitative criterion proposed by Kastner for the generation of offer and confirmation waves faces serious difficulties. This leaves the measurement problem unsolved and absorbers ill-defined." Kastner's reply is titled accordingly: "absorption and non-unitarity remain well-defined."

So the dispute the manuscript cites is about exactly the RTI feature the manuscript credits without qualification one sentence earlier — "the coupling as the physical variable, absorption 'quantitatively defined'" — and not about the feature the manuscript declines to accept. As written, v1.1 credits RTI with the contested claim and contests the uncontested one.

**Fix.** (a) Re-describe the 2017 exchange as a dispute over whether the coupling amplitude quantifies absorber response, and say that this bears on RTI's placement of the cut, which the paper adopts. (b) Source the manuscript's own view that the transactional square is stipulated. It is a real position in the literature, but not Marchildon 2017's; the long form cites Boisvert & Marchildon (2013, *Found. Phys.* 43, 294) and the short form dropped it — restore it, or cite Marchildon's 2006 causal-loops analysis, or state the view as the authors' own. (c) Say where the square enters in Kastner & Cramer precisely. In their derivation the confirmation amplitude is not stipulated as ψ*: their eq. (14) is the absorption matrix element and "is just the complex conjugate of (13)" because the interaction Hamiltonian is Hermitian. The contestable step is eq. (15), where the product of the emission and absorption amplitudes "clearly turns out to be the probability" — that is, the Born identification of |amplitude|² with probability is made there, for the joint two-vertex process, rather than for ψ alone. Stated that way, the manuscript's objection is sharper (the square is not derived, it is relocated from one amplitude to a product of two) and is charitable to what the paper actually argues.

### M2. §5.3 conflates Cramer's 1986 derivation with RTI's, and the "no foliation" sentence glosses over why possibilist TI exists (specialism)

§5.3: "The transactional interpretation claims to derive the square from the requirement that a real photon has both an emission and an absorption vertex." That is the Kastner–Cramer direct-action argument of 2018. Cramer 1986's argument is the offer–confirmation echo, ψ at the absorber times ψ* returned, with the transaction amplitude ψψ*; §2 has this right ("claims that the Born weight follows as their product [Cramer 1986]") and §5.3 should say "RTI" where it says "the transactional interpretation."

§2: "both Cramer and Kastner claim their nonlocality comes without a preferred frame, the transaction being tied to a spacetime interval rather than an instant." Cramer's abstract does claim TI is "explicitly nonlocal ... yet is relativistically invariant," so the attribution is fair. But the sentence treats the two claims as one, when Kastner's move to a possibilist ontology (Kastner 2012) was motivated in part by relativistic and pseudo-time difficulties in Cramer's spacetime account and by the Maudlin contingent-absorber challenge — the best-known objection to TI, absent from the manuscript, and the one Kastner's ontology was built to answer. For a paper whose largest admitted price is a foliation, this matters: TI's "no foliation" is bought with an ontology the manuscript rejects on its first commitment. One sentence saying so would make §2's "cuts against this paper" accurate rather than generous.

**Fix.** Change "The transactional interpretation claims" to "RTI claims" in §5.3; add one sentence in §2 on the Maudlin challenge and Kastner's response (Kastner, arXiv:1610.04609, cited by Kastner & Cramer themselves), and note that the price of TI's no-foliation claim is the possibilist ontology.

### M3. The abstract's one formula contradicts its own stated limit (outside my specialism; arithmetic)

Abstract: the crossover "has half-width √(Γ²/4 + 2K²), which is the coupling's population-transfer rate 2K where the coupling exceeds the record channel's rate Γ." §4.2: "For **Γ ≪ K** the half-width is 2K." But √(2K²) = √2·K ≈ 1.41 K, not 2K. The record shows both numbers side by side: at Γ/K = 0.2 the formula gives 1.42, the analytic occupation half-point is 2.0, and the measured rate-based midpoint is 1.54 (`REVIEW_RUNS_RESULTS.md`, `RESULTS.md` F3). The formula is the steady-state power-broadened half-width with Ω = 2K (Ω²/2 = 2K²), whose Γ → 0 limit is Ω/√2; the "2K" is the half-point of the undamped time-averaged occupation 2K²/(Δ² + 4K²). These are different observables and the paper quotes both as one.

**Fix.** Either state the Γ ≪ K limit as √2 K and say the occupation half-point 2K is a different observable that the record's measured 1.3–2.0 straddles, or drop "which is ... 2K" from the abstract and write "of order K." The abstract must not carry a formula whose limit it misstates.

### M4. Clause 1 of the postulate is not the formal statement that follows it (internal consistency; outside my specialism but checkable)

§5.1 clause 1: "*Every site draws, in proportion to the square.* ... The energy the site draws from the field is proportional to the square of the local amplitude." The formal statement: "between jumps, the no-jump Hamiltonian H − (iħ/2)Σ_i Γ_i P_i ... renormalized; at rate Γ_i ⟨ψ|P_i|ψ⟩ a jump onto site i's absorbed state." In the quantum-jump unravelling of a one-photon state no site draws energy before the jump; the no-jump evolution renormalizes amplitudes and the energy transfer *is* the jump. Clause 1 is the linear-absorber picture of the withdrawn programme, and the sentence "Clause 1 is the golden rule's matrix element squared" does not reconcile them: a matrix element squared is a rate, not an energy drawn. Clause 3 has the same problem in the other direction: it quotes "within a time the record's model bounds at a thousandth of the opening time" — a bound obtained in a dynamical race with a finite-speed stop — and then says "the paper's 'stop' is the projection, not a dynamical race." A projection has no speed to bound.

**Fix.** Rewrite clause 1 as a statement about the conditional state's branch weights (or about the golden-rule rate per site), and either delete the race-derived speed bound from clause 3 or say what it bounds in a theory where exclusivity is kinematic.

### M5. "Computable location" and "physical address" are two different things, and the abstract merges them

Abstract: "the Heisenberg cut is a physical crossover with a computable location and width and a physical address, the absorption vertex of a detector site." After the §4.5 concession, the computable location is the decoherence crossover — "in both regimes the placement is decoherence theory's own" (§4.2) — and the physical address is where the postulate puts the event, which §4.3 concedes "no experiment can contradict." §4.1 still opens with the criterion "The cut is where in-principle recoverability ends" although §4.5 now says in-principle recoverability ends only "by postulate." So the criterion does no locating work, and the abstract reads as if the address were computed.

**Fix.** One sentence in the abstract and one in §4.1: the crossover is computed and textbook; the address is assigned by the postulate within the decohered chain and is unobservable by von Neumann mobility. The paper says both things in the body; the abstract should not let a reader miss that they are different.

### M6. The "one result" is stated too generally to be a result, and the transactional comparison shows why

§5.2 (bold): "a real-wave, one-world programme with no new constant, forced to reproduce quantum mechanics, is pushed to the projection postulate's branch weights at the decoherence crossover; nothing in the physics of a detector and no synchronization substrate can move the outcome weights, and the family of 'hazard at the absorber' mechanisms collapses onto the projection postulate." Any programme forced to reproduce quantum mechanics' statistics must reproduce the Born weights — that is Gleason's theorem, and Busch's POVM version for the affine structure §7 itself invokes ("any local POVM makes the conditional statistics ... affine"). What the record actually established is narrower and worth stating as such: one class of race models, at diagnostic budget, produces Born only when the golden rule's memorylessness and hazard linearity are imported. The general sentence adds nothing to Gleason and is not supported by anything in the record beyond that class.

The transactional comparison makes the overreach visible. RTI *is* a "hazard at the absorber" mechanism — an absorber responds with amplitude *e*, and the two-vertex product gives the weight — and the manuscript does not say whether the no-go includes it. If it does, RTI is a counterexample to "collapses onto the projection postulate" only if one grants that the two-vertex product is the projection postulate relocated, which is the manuscript's contested view (M1). If it does not, the "family" is smaller than the sentence says.

**Fix.** State the result as the specific negative finding it is (the tested class, the budget, the imported structure), cite Gleason 1957 and Busch 2003 for the general statement, and say in §5.3 whether RTI falls inside the family and why.

## 7. Rubric scores (1–5)

- **Novelty — 2.** The paper's own accounting is that its additions are narrow: a regime-labelled rate-balance location that is textbook adiabatic elimination, a dense-record requirement that is Weisskopf–Wigner, the echo/flip distinction, and a negative result whose general form is Gleason's theorem (M6). What is genuinely new is the specific, recorded failure of the synchronization class; the manuscript's bold sentence claims more.
- **Internal consistency — 3.** Much improved and mostly coherent after the concessions, but the abstract's formula misstates its own limit (M3), clause 1 and the formal statement of the postulate describe different processes (M4), and the abstract merges the computed crossover with the postulated address (M5).
- **Evidential grounding — 3.** Exact small models with predictions fixed first and wrong predictions recorded, matched to the record at every number I checked; no laboratory datum bears on the paper's distinctive content, which the paper admits ("No experiment separates this interpretation from quantum mechanics").
- **Reproducibility — 4.** Scripts, predictions files, and result files are named and public; the numbers in the text match them; the diagnostic labels are carried. I did not execute the code, so I cannot score 5.
- **Citation integrity — 3.** Every reference I could check exists with correct details, and the new photodetection, collapse-model, and null-experiment citations are apt. But the one dispute in the paper's own specialist neighbourhood is cited for a claim its source does not make (M1), §5.3 attributes RTI's argument to Cramer (M2), one reference is orphaned, and the analytic identity in §5.2 is presented as a record result.

## 8. Overall assessment

**2** (weak-but-in-scope). The revision is honest, the concessions are real, and the record audit passes; but the abstract carries a formula that contradicts its stated limit, the postulate's prose and formal statements disagree, the transactional comparison — rewritten "against the sources" — cites the sources for a dispute they do not contain and credits RTI with the very claim they contest, and the paper's one result is stated at a generality only Gleason's theorem supports. All of this is fixable in one more revision; none of it should reach a journal as it stands.

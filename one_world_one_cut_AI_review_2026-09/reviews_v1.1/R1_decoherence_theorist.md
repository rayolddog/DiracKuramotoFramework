## 2. Summary (to fix terms)

As I read it, the paper proposes the following. The quantum state is a real thing on configuration/Fock space. Measurement has three stages; the first two (entangling, decoherence) are standard; the third, the event, is postulated. The postulate is that the quantum-jump unravelling of photodetection — no-jump evolution under a non-Hermitian effective Hamiltonian, with jumps at the detector's absorption channels at rate Γ_i times the branch weight of the conditional state — is not a calculational device but what physically happens, in one world, ordered by a preferred foliation. The cut is where these jumps occur, the absorption vertex; its location in detuning is the detector's own decoherence crossover, of half-width √(Γ²/4 + 2K²), coupling-set at strong coupling and record-set (hence temperature-set) for every real photon-counting detector. Nothing in this differs from quantum mechanics with the projection postulate, and the paper says so. What it claims as its own is: the location with its regime; the requirement that the record be a continuum on the capture timescale; a distinction between the echo and the coupling flip; and a "no-go" — that a real-wave, one-world, no-new-constant programme forced to reproduce quantum mechanics is pushed to the projection postulate's weights, and that nothing in a detector or a synchronization substrate can move them.

## 4. Major concerns

### M1. The formal statement of the postulate is mis-specified: the jump operator is not a projector, and the paper never writes it down.

§5.1 *Stated formally*: "between jumps, the no-jump Hamiltonian H − (iħ/2)Σ_i Γ_i P_i with P_i the site's absorbing projector, renormalized; at rate Γ_i ⟨ψ|P_i|ψ⟩ a jump onto site i's absorbed state."

In a quantum-jump unravelling the no-jump generator, the jump rate, and the post-jump state are all determined by one operator c_i: H_eff = H − (iħ/2)Σ c_i†c_i; jump probability in dt is ⟨c_i†c_i⟩dt; post-jump state c_iψ/‖c_iψ‖. If the jump operator were a projector, c_i = √Γ_i P_i, the post-jump state would be P_iψ/‖P_iψ‖ — the excitation still sitting at site i, unabsorbed — and the site would keep jumping at rate Γ_i forever. That is a which-site nondemolition monitor, not photodetection, and it contradicts §2's "exactly one jump per quantum." The statement is consistent only if the jump operator is a *transition* operator, c_i = √Γ_i |a_i⟩⟨e_i| (or, in the Γ ≫ K regime, the field annihilation operator at the site with rate ∝ K²/Γ — the Glauber/Kelley–Kleiner limit), in which case c_i†c_i = Γ_i|e_i⟩⟨e_i| and P_i is the projector onto the site's *excited* (captured) state, not an "absorbing projector." The text has the rate operator and the post-jump map coming from different operators, which is not an unravelling of any Lindblad equation.

Three consequences follow from writing c_i properly. (a) The "branch weight" in clause 2 is the occupation of the site's excited state, fed by capture with the Lorentzian in Δ of §4.2 — so clause 1's "proportional to the square of the local amplitude" is right only after adiabatic elimination (Γ ≫ K), and the paper should say which it means. (b) Clause 1's "background rate, thermal or current-assisted, exists at every site" has no counterpart in the formal statement: dark counts need thermal jump operators √γ_th|a_i⟩⟨g_i| added, or the sentence removed. (c) "The first sink to open anywhere is the one that opens" (clause 2) is a residue of the race: in the unravelling there is one jump, and after it c_j|a_i, vac⟩ = 0 for every j. Clause 3 says this correctly ("the paper's 'stop' is the projection, not a dynamical race"), and then says "the paper postulates all three" while also saying "for a one-photon state exclusivity is kinematic." It cannot be both a postulate and a kinematic consequence; it is the latter, given c_i.

Also: what is written is a stochastic Schrödinger equation for the conditional pure state, not a "stochastic master equation" (which governs conditional density matrices). Fix: write c_i explicitly, state H_eff, the jump probability, and the post-jump map in one line; delete "the first sink to open anywhere"; reconcile "postulates all three" with "kinematic"; add or drop the dark-count channel.

### M2. The no-signalling inference is stated as a sufficiency it does not have.

§5.1: "Its ensemble average is a Lindblad equation, linear in ρ, and therefore does not signal [Gisin 1989; Polchinski 1991]." §6: "no-signalling is the theorem of §5.1."

Gisin and Polchinski show that *nonlinearity* of the ensemble evolution permits signalling; linearity is necessary, not sufficient. A Lindblad equation whose generators are nonlocal signals: take two qubits with a single jump operator c = σ_A^− ⊗ σ_B^−; then dρ_A/dt contains σ_A^− ⟨e_B|ρ|e_B⟩ σ_A^+, so Alice's reduced state depends on whether Bob excited his qubit. What makes the paper's process non-signalling is that H is a sum of local terms and every c_i acts on one site, so the ensemble map factorizes as local CPTP maps that commute with the partial trace over the other wing. That is the theorem, and it is one sentence; but it has to be the sentence, because the paper's ontology has the *conditional* state updated nonlocally and a reader must be told exactly why the *ensemble* is not. Note also that this locality condition is what makes the ensemble statistics foliation-independent, which §6 asserts but does not derive.

Fix: replace "linear in ρ, and therefore does not signal" with "linear in ρ with local generators (H local, each c_i acting at one site), and therefore does not signal; nonlinearity would (Gisin 1989; Polchinski 1991), and so would nonlocal generators."

### M3. The ontic reading of one unravelling is asserted without a selection principle, and the paper does not say which dissipative channels are events.

§5.1: "The rule that reproduces quantum mechanics is the branch weight." `REVIEW_RUNS_RESULTS.md` B4, which the paper adopts: "The only stochastic process with these ingredients that reproduces quantum mechanics is … the quantum-jump unravelling."

"The" and "only" are wrong. Every unravelling of the same Lindbladian reproduces quantum mechanics: the jump unravelling, the diffusive (homodyne/heterodyne) unravellings, and the continuous family obtained by c_i → c_i + β_i and by unitary mixing of the c_i (Wiseman & Milburn 2010, ch. 4, which the paper cites). An ontic reading must pick one, and the paper's pick — photodetection jumps — needs a physical selection principle. The paper offers einselection: "This paper accepts every einselection result, including the one that selects the site basis its postulate uses" (§2). But einselection selects a *basis* for a reduced state; it does not select an *unravelling* — the diffusive unravellings decohere in the same pointer basis. The obvious principle available to the paper is that the beables are the absorption events themselves, wherever a photon is physically absorbed into a dense record, so that the unravelling is fixed by the physical absorber and not by a distant observer's choice of detection scheme. If that is the position, it must be stated, and then two consequences must be faced:

(i) **Wiseman & Gambetta (PRL 108, 220402, 2012), "Are dynamical quantum jumps detector dependent?"** show that the stochastic evolution of a source system (jumps versus diffusion) depends on the existence and nature of a distant detector, and propose tests. A paper whose entire postulate is "the jump unravelling read as what happens" cannot omit this. The paper's escape — that only absorptions are ontic and the source's trajectory is derived — is available, but the paper has to take it explicitly. Related: Gambetta & Wiseman, PRA 68, 062104 (2003), on why non-Markovian stochastic Schrödinger equation trajectories cannot be read as hidden-variable histories; and the standard identity that every spontaneous-collapse model *is* an unravelling of a Lindblad equation read as ontic (Bassi & Ghirardi, Phys. Rep. 379, 257, 2003; Diósi 1988; Gisin & Percival, J. Phys. A 25, 5677, 1992), which places the paper in the collapse family it distinguishes itself from in §2 — with the detector's own absorption dissipator as the collapse generator instead of a universal one. That is a fair description of the proposal and the paper should give it.

(ii) **Which Lindblad channels are events?** §6: "The slicing enters wherever a jump occurs, which is at every absorption with a dense record channel." What about pure dephasing (L = √γ σ_z), which has a jump unravelling too and is the Γ(T) of §7's T₂ test? What about elastic scattering, which localizes a dust grain (Joos & Zeh 1985) without absorbing anything? If only absorptions are events, then the grain's superposition is not resolved when the photon scatters but when the scattered photon is absorbed in a wall, possibly far away and later — the Joos–Zeh localization becomes nonlocal projection at distant absorbers. That is a coherent ontology with real content, and the paper does not mention it. If instead every environmental jump is an event, then every decoherence event is a collapse and the cut is wherever decoherence is, which contradicts the paper's placement "at the absorption vertex." The paper must choose and say what follows.

Fix: state the selection principle (absorption events as beables), engage Wiseman & Gambetta 2012 directly, place the proposal inside the "collapse = ontic unravelling" family with the citations above, and say what the postulate does for non-absorptive channels.

### M4. The "no-go" of §5.2 is a restatement, and as written it is refuted by an interpretation the paper discusses in §2.

§5.2, boldface: "a real-wave, one-world programme with no new constant, forced to reproduce quantum mechanics, is pushed to the projection postulate's branch weights at the decoherence crossover; nothing in the physics of a detector and no synchronization substrate can move the outcome weights, and the family of 'hazard at the absorber' mechanisms collapses onto the projection postulate. That is this paper's one result, and it is a no-go."

Three problems.

(a) The first clause is tautological. "Reproduces quantum mechanics" *means* has the Born weights; that a programme forced to reproduce quantum mechanics ends up with the Born weights is not a result about the programme.

(b) As a general claim it is false. Bohmian mechanics (§2: "Bohm solves the measurement problem with a deterministic guidance law and no event and no cut") is a real-wave, one-world, no-new-constant programme that reproduces quantum mechanics without the projection postulate; so is Bell's jump process for fermion number (Bell, "Beables for quantum field theory," 1984, in *Speakable and Unspeakable*, 1987) and its development as Bell-type QFT (Dürr, Goldstein, Tumulka & Zanghì, J. Phys. A 38, R1, 2005) — a stochastic *jump* ontology whose rates are set by the Hamiltonian's off-diagonal elements, no new constant, quantum mechanics exactly. Both put the weights in as an equilibrium hypothesis on the beables, not as a collapse at a detector. The paper's phrase "Bell's 'jump'" (§2) refers to Bell 1990; Bell's own jump theory, the nearest ancestor of "jumps read as ontic," is not cited. The boldface holds, if at all, only for programmes that (1) have a collapse and (2) locate the weights in detector physics.

(c) Restricted that way, it is a known theorem, not the paper's. A stochastic collapse whose ensemble is linear (required by no-signalling, Gisin 1989) has branch weights that are martingales, so the outcome frequencies equal the initial weights whatever the detector does: Pearle, PRD 13, 857 (1976); Gisin, PRL 52, 1657 (1984); reviewed in Bassi & Ghirardi 2003. "Nothing in the physics of a detector can move the outcome weights" is the martingale property. The paper cites Gisin 1989 for no-signalling and does not notice that the same line of work is the theorem it is claiming.

What the record actually established is narrower and worth stating as such: for one family of models — Adler-clock races and energy-linear hazards on a phase-oscillator substrate, at diagnostic budget, every number labelled `diagnostic_only` — no rule tried gives Born without importing the golden rule's structure and an exclusivity constraint, and the surviving residual (2.09–2.21) is "consistent with an extreme-value ingredient and not established." That is a negative result for that family, and the paper's abstract calls it one. §5.2 and §5.3 upgrade it to a "no-go." Fix: retitle it a negative result, restrict its scope to the family tested, cite Pearle 1976 and Gisin 1984 for the general statement it instantiates, and remove or qualify the first clause.

### M5. The regime statement of §4.2 has an arithmetic inconsistency, a protocol dependence the "15 %" conceals, and a consequence for the paper's own stage structure that it does not draw.

§4.2: "half-width … Δ_{1/2} = √(Γ²/4 + 2K²), verified across Γ/K from 0.2 to 1000 … For Γ ≪ K the half-width is 2K." Abstract: "half-width √(Γ²/4 + 2K²), which is the coupling's population-transfer rate 2K where the coupling exceeds the record channel's rate Γ."

(a) *Arithmetic.* √(2K²) = √2·K ≈ 1.41 K, not 2K. The formula is the steady-state power-broadened half-width of the optical Bloch equations, √(Γ²/4 + Ω²/2) with Ω = 2K (Steck's or Loudon's expression), whose Γ → 0 limit is Ω/√2. The "2K" is a different textbook number — the half-point of the time-averaged excited-state occupation 2K²/(Δ² + 4K²) of a closed two-level system (`RESULTS.md` F3). The paper asserts both as one statement. A third number is available from the same model: the asymptotic (slowest-eigenvalue) leak-rate half-point at Γ → 0, which I compute as 2/√3·K = 1.155 K. The Γ ≪ K "half-width" in this model is O(K) with a prefactor between 1.15 and 2 depending on the observable; the paper should say so rather than pick one.

(b) *Protocol dependence.* "Verified across Γ/K from 0.2 to 1000" is five points, with nothing between Γ/K = 1 and 10 — exactly where the regime boundary sits. I re-ran the Run A model (`gamma_regime_sweep.py`, same code) at Γ/K = 1.5, 2, 3, 5, 7 and at several capture times. Run A's rows were read at a total resonant leak exponent 2·rate(0)·t ≈ 2 (0.63 for the Γ/K = 0.2 row). At that reading the formula does hold to within about 7 % in the intermediate regime. But the rate-based midpoint is not a property of the model alone:

| Γ/K | t = 1 | t = 2 | t = 3 | t = 5 | formula √(Γ²/4+2K²) | Γ/2K |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3.76 | 1.69 | 1.01 | 1.35 | 1.73 | 1.00 |
| 3 | 3.92 | 1.93 | 1.16 | 0.91 | 2.06 | 1.50 |
| 5 | 4.41 | 2.78 | 2.35 | 2.07 | 2.87 | 2.50 |
| 0.2 | — | — | 1.54 (t=3) | 1.70 (t=10); 1.41 (t=30); 1.23 (t=100) | 1.42 | 0.10 |

At Γ/K = 3 the midpoint moves by a factor of 4.3 between a leak exponent of 0.8 and 14, and at Γ/K = 0.2 it falls from 1.7 to 1.2 as t goes from 10 to 100. The paper says this of the *fixed-time* reading ("drifts outward with Γt … the location is a statement about rates") and presents the *rate-based* reading as if it were free of it. It is not. The formula describes the model read at one leak exponent. The honest statement is the one the paper already half-makes: the crossover is a scale with relative width of order one, and a "location" quoted to two decimals (Run A: "50.0 against 50.0") is a property of the protocol in the regime where it is Γ/2 and of the observation time everywhere else. Fix: state the leak-exponent protocol, report the t-dependence, replace "verified … to 15 %" with what is verified, and correct the √2.

(c) *Consequence for §4.3.* For Γ ≫ K — "every room-temperature semiconductor detector, at Γ/K of order 10⁵" — the absorber is adiabatically eliminated: the excited site is a virtual intermediate, the effective jump operator is the field annihilation at the site with rate K²Γ/(Δ² + Γ²/4), and capture and commitment are not separable stages. §4.3's table ("capture | unitary, distributed across candidate sites"; "commitment | the absorption vertex") therefore collapses to one stage for exactly the class §4.4 calls "the only class for which per-event Born claims are properly made." The paper concedes that for a band absorber "the location claim has no referent beyond the Γ/2 regime statement," but does not say that the three-stage anatomy loses its first stage there too. What is left of "a physical crossover with a computable location and width and a physical address" (abstract) for real photon-counting detectors is the address, and the address is postulated ("recoverability ends here by postulate"; "by von Neumann's mobility result … the world cannot contradict"). The computed location applies to discrete-transition absorbers at strong coupling, where it is the Rabi/power-broadened width — textbook, as the paper says. The title should not promise more than that.

### M6. The concession in §4.5 leaves residue in §4.1, the abstract, §9, and §5.1 clause 3.

§4.5 concedes: "Recoverability of the reduced dynamics ends for all practical purposes, as decoherence says; what ends it in principle is the event of §5, which is postulated, and nothing else." Good. But:

- §4.1 still opens: "The cut is where *in-principle recoverability ends*: the first point in the chain at which no operation on the site and the field, however idealized, restores the pre-event configuration." That definition — the environment excluded by fiat — is the one the concession says is FAPP. Combined with §4.5, the paper's criterion now reads: the cut is where the postulated event is, because the postulated event is what ends recoverability in principle. That is circular as a location claim. §4.1 should define the cut as where recoverability *of the reduced dynamics* ends (FAPP) and say the event is placed there by postulate.
- The abstract and §9 still list "the distinction between in-principle recoverability and the reversal an experimenter can apply" as something the paper adds. After §4.5 the distinction the paper actually has is between *two reversal protocols* (echo versus coupling flip), both of which are FAPP quantities. Rename it.
- §5.1 clause 3: "every other amplitude of that photon vanishes … within a time the record's model bounds at a thousandth of the opening time." That bound is τ_x ≤ 1.5 × 10⁻³ race units from `adler_two_channel_exploratory/RESULTS.md`, the exclusivity discriminator of a race model the paper has withdrawn, and the same clause says "the paper's 'stop' is the projection, not a dynamical race." A projection on the foliation is instantaneous; a finite-speed bound from a hazard density of a discarded model is a vestige. Either delete it or restate it as what it is: any finite-speed version of the projection is bounded by single-photon anticorrelation data (Grangier et al. 1986) to act far faster than light crosses the ports, a Grangier-type argument that does not need the race.
- §4.5 keeps the v1.0 "heat is the writer … e^{S/k_B} configurations" paragraph as the setup for the concession. That is acceptable if labelled; it currently reads as a live argument until the last four sentences.

### M7. The literature the postulate "restates" is cited incompletely, and one of the gaps is load-bearing.

The cited set is Glauber, Kelley–Kleiner, Srinivas–Davies, Dalibard–Castin–Mølmer, Carmichael, Plenio–Knight, Wiseman–Milburn. The characterization of the cited works is otherwise accurate; "a position close to a realist reading of Carmichael's" is fair, provided the paper notes that Carmichael did not himself read the trajectories as ontic.

## 6. Specific questions for the author

1. Write the jump operator c_i. Is it the site lowering operator √Γ_i|a_i⟩⟨e_i| or, for Γ ≫ K, the field annihilation operator at the site? In the regime of every real detector, is there any physical content to "capture" as a stage distinct from the jump?
2. Which Lindblad channels are ontic events — absorption only, or every dissipative channel including pure dephasing and elastic scattering? Where, on your ontology, does a scattered-light-decohered dust grain localize: at the grain when the photon scatters, or at the wall when the photon is absorbed?

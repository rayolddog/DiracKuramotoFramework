## 2. Summary (to fix terms)

The paper proposes a single-world, ψ-ontic, nonlocal interpretation in which the wave (Dirac and Maxwell fields) is real, exactly one outcome occurs, and the Heisenberg cut is a physical crossover located at the absorption vertex of a detector site, at the point where the off-shell return rate of a site balances the coupling's population-transfer rate, κ_ret/K = 1. Outcome selection is a postulated stochastic event at that vertex: every site draws energy in proportion to the local squared amplitude; a "sink" opens at one site with a hazard linear in the energy drawn; the whole quantum then flows through that site, the amplitude elsewhere vanishing faster than light. The location and width of the cut were tested in exact small models and found consistent with decoherence theory. The paper claims as its own only: that recoverability ends in principle rather than for all practical purposes; that the crossing is coupling-set rather than mass-set; the one-world commitment with its Bell-nonlocality; and a specification of what any selection mechanism must supply. It introduces no constant and makes no prediction differing from quantum mechanics. Nonlocal commitments are ordered by a preferred foliation entering "through the measurement sector only."

## 3. Strengths

- The paper is unusually candid about what it does not do. "The paper derives none of the three" (§5.1); "We hold the claim, flag it" (§4.5); "the quantitative demonstration that the residual anisotropy clears every established bound is owed" (§6); "No new constant is introduced and no prediction differs from quantum mechanics" (abstract). This candour should be preserved through any revision.
- Every quantitative statement I checked reconciles with the repository record: the rate-based midpoint 1.3–2.0; the 25–75 % points at 1.2–3; the exponents 1.56, 2.16, 1.70, 3.25 (reported as 1.5, 2.1–2.2, 1.7, 3.2); the mode-count ladder; the amplifier slopes 0.004 and 1.1; the exclusivity timing 10⁻³ and the factor 400 (reported as "two orders of magnitude"). The predictions-before-results discipline is real and documented, including predictions that were wrong.
- The three-process decomposition of §1 (entangling interaction / decoherence / the event) and the sentence "Irreversibility is purchased; selection is not" state the logical structure of the measurement problem correctly and concisely. The paper's insistence that decoherence does not deliver an outcome is right, and correctly attributed to Schlosshauer's own review.
- The detector taxonomy (§4.4) and the observation that the downstream cascade is "photon-agnostic" and "contributes no statistics" are useful and, as far as I can tell, correct; see Major concern 9 for what I think they could be made to do.
- The treatment of the transactional interpretation (§2, §5.3) is more careful than most comparisons in this literature: it draws the shared and differing commitments exactly, and it concedes that neither party derives the square.

## 4. Major concerns

### M1. The selection postulate, as written, cannot reproduce the Born rule when candidate sites are reached at different times — which is every Bell experiment and every unequal-arm interferometer. The record tested only synchronous exposure.

Clause 2 of §5.1: "At each site a sink can open at any moment, with a probability per unit time proportional to the energy that site has drawn so far, the same rule at every site; the first sink to open anywhere is the one that opens." The formal statement adds "with a site-independent time profile," and §5.2 says "its timing is not [load-bearing]." Commitment is placed at the vertex, "10 fs–1 ps" (§4.3), and the hazard is the golden rule's rate at the absorber (§4.2 gives Γ ~ 10¹³–10¹⁴ s⁻¹ for silicon).

Take a Mach–Zehnder with a 30 cm path difference (1 ns) and a 10 : 90 amplitude-squared split between the near detector D1 and the far detector D2, both silicon SPADs. On the paper's clause 2, D1's sites hold 10 % of the quantum and a sink opens there at a rate of order 10¹³ s⁻¹ × 0.1 = 10¹² s⁻¹. The probability that a D1 sink opens before the wave reaches D2 is 1 − exp(−10¹² s⁻¹ × 10⁻⁹ s) = 1 − e⁻¹⁰⁰⁰ ≈ 1. Clause 3 then sends the whole quantum through D1. Quantum mechanics, and the laboratory, give 10 %. The same arithmetic applies to any two detectors at unequal distances from the source, so to essentially every Bell test.

The paper's own requirement, "a site-independent time profile," is violated by geometry in these cases; the paper does not notice because the record tested only the synchronous case. `adler_two_channel_exploratory/RESULTS.md` states the construction: two populations driven "while a common photon-pulse envelope opens and closes their Arnold tongues," and the exclusivity discriminator uses "the same race as the energy variant." I found no staggered-arrival run anywhere in the record. Under synchronous exposure the estimand P_A/(P_A + P_B) is a ratio in which the survival factor cancels; that is exactly the quantity that hides the problem.

A simpler form of the same defect: on clause 2, the probability that a detector clicks at all is 1 − exp(−γ E t), an exponential of the intensity, whereas quantum mechanics (and single-photon detector-linearity measurements) make it linear in the intensity. Matching an efficiency of 0.5 at full intensity gives 0.29 rather than 0.25 at half intensity — a 17 % nonlinearity at the single-photon level, which is not observed. So the postulate, read literally, does make a prediction that differs from quantum mechanics, contradicting the abstract.

The root is the phrase "Fermi's golden rule read as an event rather than a rate." The golden rule's rate multiplies a *branch* weight: the probability that the quantum has been absorbed at D1 by time t is |ψ₁|² (1 − e^{−Γt}). A hazard proportional to |ψ₁|² acting on the *whole quantum's* survival is a different object, and it is not the golden rule. The only stochastic process that reproduces quantum mechanics here is "the D1 branch is absorbed at rate Γ; the world ends up in that branch with probability |ψ₁|²" — which is the projection postulate with Born weights, not a hazard linear in energy drawn.

**What would fix it.** (i) Run the two-channel race with channel B's envelope delayed by many commitment times and report the outcome exponent and the unconditional click probabilities, not only the conditional ratio. (ii) Restate clause 2 as an explicit stochastic process (jump operators, rates, the state after a jump) and show it reproduces the joint click statistics of time-separated detectors. (iii) If the only such process is the one with Born weights put in, say so plainly and withdraw "a specification of what any mechanism for the selection event must supply" as a result: the specification would then be "supply the Born rule," and the numbers 1.5, 2.16 and 10⁻³ would be facts about one model class (Adler races under synchronous drive), not constraints on "any mechanism." I am outside my formal specialism on the arithmetic, but the point is elementary and I would ask the formalism reviewer to confirm it.

### M2. No-signalling "from Gisin's argument in reverse" is asserted, not derived; the paper contradicts itself about its status; and the inference as stated is invalid.

§6: "no-signalling is not assumed but follows from the hazard's linearity in the squared amplitude, which is Gisin's argument run in reverse as a consistency condition — a nonlinear hazard would signal [Gisin 1990]." §8 item 3: what is owed is "the covariant formulation of the selection event on the foliation, with its no-signalling proof independent of the statistics it reproduces." A claim cannot both "follow" and be owed.

On the logic: Gisin (1990) shows one direction — nonlinear deterministic state evolution permits superluminal signalling. The converse the paper needs is that the *ensemble-averaged* evolution of the density matrix under the stochastic process is a linear (completely positive) map; that is the standard condition under which stochastic reduction models do not signal (Gisin 1989, Helv. Phys. Acta 62, 363; Polchinski 1991, PRL 66, 397; this is also why GRW and CSL are safe). "Hazard linear in the squared amplitude" is not that condition, and M1 shows it fails: with hazard γE over an exposure, outcome probabilities are exponentials of linear functionals of ρ, hence nonlinear in ρ. Concretely, take |Φ⁺⟩ = (|HH⟩ + |VV⟩)/√2, Alice behind an H polarizer with one absorber site. If Bob does not measure, Alice's site draws intensity ½ and clicks with probability 1 − e^{−γ/2}. If Bob measures H/V first in the foliation, Alice's conditional intensity is 1 or 0 with probability ½ each, and her click probability is ½(1 − e^{−γ}). By Jensen these differ for every γ > 0 (at γ = 2: 0.63 versus 0.43). Alice's singles rate then depends on whether Bob measured: a signal. The escape is to make outcome probabilities linear in the branch weight, which is to assume the Born rule and the Lüders update — the very things the paper says it does not assume.

A second gap: §6 never states what happens to the partner wing's amplitude when one wing commits. "The amplitude at every other site vanishes" (§5.1) concerns the same quantum; for the entangled pair one needs the partner to be updated to the conditional state. That is an additional postulate (Lüders), not "a restatement of the real-wave commitment."

The record is instructive. Ledger E-13: the "inverted Gisin" repair "became [P1] Theorem 5 — demoted in v0.7 to a robustness result. The note-level dependency was never revisited after the demotion ... No paper claim rests on it." The ledger's drafting note for the single paper lists "no-signalling restated as Gisin's argument in reverse (hazard linearity)" among the paper's *demotions*. The short form has re-elevated a restatement into a "follows."

**What would fix it.** Write the ensemble-averaged master equation of the postulated process; show it is linear in ρ (or show where it is not); state the partner-update rule; cite Gisin 1989 and Polchinski 1991 rather than Gisin 1990 for the general condition. Failing that, state no-signalling as assumed, which is consistent with §8 item 3 and with the record.

### M3. The ontology is bifurcated, and the configuration-space objection is not "held open" — it is used.

§3.1: "The wave is a physical field: the Dirac field for matter, the Maxwell field for light." These are fields on three-space. §6: "The correlation comes from the second object: the non-separable field configuration carrying the entangled two-particle mode across both wings — one configuration of the real field spanning A and B. Locating the nonlocality there adds no postulate; it restates the real-wave commitment."

It does not restate it; it changes it. For a polarization-entangled photon pair there is no configuration of the Maxwell field on three-space that carries the correlations: the field expectation values vanish and the state is a two-point object on ℝ³ × ℝ³. Bell's theorem for local beables is, in one reading, precisely the result that no three-space field configuration evolving locally reproduces them; to have a three-space "configuration" do so one must update it nonlocally, and then one must say what the configuration *is*. Norsen's "theory of exclusively local beables" (Found. Phys. 2010) is the sustained attempt and shows how hard it is. The paper's own parenthetical concedes the point — "Conditional on the real-wave commitment extended to the non-separable two-particle configuration" — but the body then uses the extension as if it were free. "Held open" would be honest if §6 did not depend on the answer; §6 is built on it.

Two related gaps in my specialism. (a) The ψ-ontic commitment is not argued. PBR (Pusey, Barrett & Rudolph, Nature Phys. 2012) is the standard argument, given preparation independence, and is not cited; nor is Harrigan & Spekkens (Found. Phys. 2010), where "ψ-ontic" is defined, though the paper uses the term. If the authors invoke PBR, they should note that its conclusion concerns the state on configuration space for N systems — it forces the choice the paper holds open. (b) "The Dirac field for matter" as a real field: the classical Dirac field is Grassmann-valued, and a real-valued fermionic field beable is not available in the naive sense (see Struyve, Rep. Prog. Phys. 2010, on field ontologies for fermions). The paper should say whether its beable is the one-particle spinor, a state functional, or something else.

**What would fix it.** Either adopt configuration-space realism explicitly (Albert 1996; Ney & Albert eds. 2013; Maudlin's contribution there) and rewrite §3.1 accordingly, or state that a three-space real-wave ontology for the entangled sector does not yet exist (citing Norsen 2010) and mark §6 as conditional on an unsolved problem rather than on a "restatement."

### M4. "Nonlocal commitments must be ordered, and the ordering is a preferred slicing" is a necessity claim contradicted by the relativistic-collapse literature; the contrast with Bohm is unfair; "confined to measurement" contradicts the paper's own §1; and the record shows an unadjudicated fork in what the frame is.

(a) Necessity. Tumulka's relativistic GRW with flash ontology (J. Stat. Phys. 2006) is a Lorentz-invariant collapse theory with no preferred foliation; Bedingham (Found. Phys. 2011) and Pearle (Phys. Rev. D 2015) give relativistic CSL; Dürr, Goldstein, Norsen, Struyve & Zanghì (Proc. R. Soc. A 2014) extract a foliation covariantly from ψ; Myrvold (SHPMP 2002) and Aharonov & Albert (Phys. Rev. D 1984) discuss why reduction need not be frame-dependent. None is cited. A preferred foliation is a *choice* with alternatives, and the paper should say why it chooses it.

(b) Bohm. §2: "Bohm's foliation is empirically inaccessible by construction; this paper's (§6) is carried openly and carries tests." By the paper's own no-signalling and "no prediction differs from quantum mechanics," its foliation is exactly as inaccessible as Bohm's. The "frame's tests" of §7 (sidereal GHZ, LIGO sidereal noise, MRI surface relaxation) are tests of *additional* hypotheses: the long form says of the one worked candidate, "The effect does not follow from the framework plus QED ... the experiment tests that postulate, not the core." The short form drops that sentence and keeps the tests. Since the framework reproduces quantum mechanics, it predicts nulls on all of them; "sidereal nulls retire a cosmic frame" retires nothing in the framework.

(c) "Explicit low-energy Lorentz violation confined to measurement." §1 endorses von Neumann's point that "nothing distinguishes a measurement from any other interaction," and §2 quotes Bell (1990) against FAPP. Bell's other target in that paper is the "shifty split" — the use of "measurement" as a primitive. On the paper's own ontology the event occurs at any absorption with a dense record channel, which is happening continuously in all ordinary matter. The Lorentz violation is therefore ubiquitous, not confined. "Ordinary matter couples to it, beyond the electromagnetic commitment itself, only gravitationally" is an assertion with no model behind it.

(d) The fork. Ledger E-12 (HIGH, "JB adjudication required"): the program holds two incompatible answers to what the frame is — a vacuum rest frame ("genuine Lorentz violation under the full weight of clock-comparison constraints") versus a matter or radiation rest frame ("SR-safe, Fizeau/MSW class, constraints largely inapplicable"). The short form's "explicit low-energy Lorentz violation" takes the first branch; "whether the frame is local or cosmic is open and testable" gestures at the second. The fork is a physical one and the paper should carry it.

**What would fix it.** Cite the relativistic-collapse literature and argue for the foliation rather than asserting its necessity; rewrite the Bohm contrast to say both foliations are unobservable and the paper's "tests" test extra postulates; either define the "measurement sector" physically and accept that the violation is everywhere, or drop the phrase; state E-12's fork.

### M5. §4.5's in-principle claim is a redefinition plus an admission, not an argument — and even if it were established it would not bear on the measurement problem.

The criterion (§4.1): "the first point in the chain at which no operation, however idealized, can restore the pre-event configuration." For unitary dynamics that operation exists (U†), so the paper immediately restricts to "the site's own Hamiltonian reversed ... the environment untouched." But then "in-principle irreversibility" is the irreversibility of the *reduced* dynamics, which is exactly what decoherence theorists assert; the disagreement with Schlosshauer becomes verbal. The argument offered (§4.5) is that "no agent — not merely no practical agent — holds what a reversal requires." "Holds" is epistemic and "agent" is not a physical category; the record's own E-14 makes the point that "unrecorded is not unrecordable" (wavefront shaping and phase conjugation invert media whose references nobody recorded) and that the correct ground is re-randomization on the T₂ timescale, not non-recording. The short form's wording, "random and unrecorded," only half-adopts that correction. Landauer's price TΔS = E is a cost, not an impossibility. The Poincaré point is conceded.

More important for a philosophy venue: the claim does no work. §1 states correctly that "Irreversibility is purchased; selection is not." What gives one world is the event of §5, which is postulated. In-principle irreversibility of the record would not produce an outcome; its only role in the paper is to make the cut "physical rather than conventional" (§2's contrast with Schlosshauer–Camilleri), and M6 shows the paper's own numbers undercut that. So "the one claim that is this paper's own" is both unestablished and, for the problem the paper is about, idle.

**What would fix it.** Either a theorem — a bound showing that no operation on site plus field restores the state for a medium with re-randomizing references, which as stated is false — or a reclassification: the cut is the decoherence crossover, the paper accepts FAPP, and §4.5 and §8 item 2 are withdrawn.

### M6. By the paper's own numbers the "located boundary" is a convention-laden crossover of relative width order one — which is the decoherence programme's position, not a refutation of it — and by von Neumann's cut-mobility result the "address" has no truth-maker.

Title and abstract: "a Located Boundary"; "a physical crossover with a computable location." §4.2 table: "rate-based crossover midpoint ... κ_ret/K = 1.3–2.0"; "a fixed-observation-time reading drifts outward" (the record: 1.7 → 2.6 → 5.1 as Γt runs 0.15 → 2 → 10); relative width "25–75 % points at 1.2–3." A midpoint that moves by a factor of three with the observation convention, inside a crossover of width order one, is a *scale*, not a boundary with an address. That is what Schlosshauer and Camilleri mean when the paper quotes them as concluding that decoherence "justifies where the cut may be placed without making the cut a physical discontinuity"; the paper's tests confirm their view.

Further, the exact model contains no stochastic event at all: it is linear quantum mechanics with a bath, and what was measured is where reduced-state recoverability decays. That the *event* occurs there is placed by fiat. And by von Neumann's result (and Bell's "shifty split"), moving the collapse from the vertex to the amplifier within a decohered chain changes no prediction — the paper concedes "no prediction differs from quantum mechanics." So §4.3's "the commonplace that collapse happens when the record is amplified misassigns the cut by one stage" is a claim the world cannot contradict. The only thing that could give it content is the in-principle claim of M5, which the paper does not establish.

**What would fix it.** Present §4 as what it is — results about crossovers in small open-quantum-system models, which have independent value — and state that the interpretation's placement of the event is empirically inert, or produce the result of M5.

### M7. Whether the position is distinct from Copenhagen-with-realism, or from a collapse theory without a law, is not settled because the law is not stated. (Maudlin's trilemma.)

In Maudlin's terms (Topoi 1995: ψ complete, ψ evolves linearly, measurements have unique outcomes — jointly inconsistent) the paper rejects linear evolution: it is a collapse theory. GRW and CSL are collapse theories with a law that applies to every system and carries constants. This paper's event applies at "a detector site" (§5.1), and "detector" is nowhere defined physically; the dense-record requirement of §4.3 makes the event conditional on decoherence having occurred; and "site" is unpinned — ledger E-18 records that "the size of the region within which sites actually compete becomes a physical parameter the Born result depends on," never resolved, and the long form's own table says that for silicon the peak's width is "undefined" and "every site is eligible." The resulting position is "the projection postulate, in the site basis, with Born weights, applied where decoherence says" — which is textbook von Neumann–Dirac quantum mechanics taken literally, with a decoherence-set address. The paper half-says this ("Clause 2 is Fermi's golden rule read as an event rather than a rate ... The paper derives none of the three"), but §2's table lists GRW's "postulated noise" and this paper's "postulated event at the vertex" as if they were the same kind of thing. "No new constant" is true only because the constant is borrowed from the absorber's own coupling; a law parameterized by the absorber's coupling is still a law with a parameter.

The question the paper must answer: is the sink law universal? If yes — every absorber in the universe — it must face reversible absorption in cavity QED (vacuum Rabi oscillations would be damped at the hazard rate unless the dense-record clause switches the law off). If the dense-record clause does switch it off, the law is "collapse iff decoherence," a familiar position that philosophers regard as not solving the problem because the criterion inherits decoherence's FAPP character. If no, what physically selects detectors?

**What would fix it.** State the law as a stochastic master equation for the matter–field state with the dense-record condition made a precise dynamical criterion (a spectral-density or recurrence-time threshold), and confront its consequences in reversible-absorption experiments. Then "is it distinct from Copenhagen-with-realism" has an answer.

### M8. The comparison of interpretations (§2) is uneven in ways that favour the paper.

- **Everett** "buys locality at the price of many worlds" — whether Everett is local is contested in my literature (Maudlin, *Quantum Non-Locality and Relativity*; Norsen; versus Wallace & Timpson, BJPS 2010, and Vaidman), and the decision-theoretic programme the paper dismisses as "contested both ways" is not cited (Deutsch 1999; Wallace 2012; Saunders et al. 2010). Everett 1957 is the only citation.
- **Bohm** — the salient difference is not "where the probability measure sits" but that Bohm gives a deterministic law with no stochastic event and no cut, and thereby solves the measurement problem; this paper postulates around it. **Nelson** is listed without noting the Wallstrom problem (Phys. Rev. A 1994) or that Nelson himself abandoned the programme over nonlocality; it is not obviously a "neighbour."
- **GRW/CSL** — "Their natural parameter space has been largely removed by X-ray emission bounds and underground tests [Donadi et al. 2021]." Donadi 2021 constrains the parameter-free Diósi–Penrose model; the X-ray bounds exclude Adler's CSL values and part of the GRW range, not "largely" the space (see Carlesso et al., Nature Phys. 2022). Outside my specialism on the numbers, but the phrasing is tendentious. Also Penrose 1996 is grouped under "new constants" when the point of the DP proposal is to have none.
- **Copenhagen "and QBism"** are lumped, but Bohr, Heisenberg's *potentia*, and von Neumann–Dirac projection differ precisely on whether the wave is real and whether the cut is a process. The paper's actual nearest relative — von Neumann–Dirac/Wigner objective collapse somewhere in the chain, Bell's "jump" — is unmentioned, and modal interpretations (single-world, ψ-real, a rule for which observable has a value) are closer kin than Nelson.
- "**No interpretation pays neither price**" (§6) is false as stated: retrocausal accounts (Price; Wharton & Argaman, RMP 2020), the transactional interpretation itself (which the paper calls its nearest neighbour and which claims to escape foliation-nonlocality via advanced waves), superdeterminism, and relational/QBist views all decline the dilemma's premises. Qualify to "no interpretation that accepts Bell's premises and unique outcomes."
- The abstract lists "the one-world commitment with the nonlocality it costs" among what is "distinctively the interpretation's own." Every single-world interpretation shares it.

### M9. What remains as a contribution for a philosophy-of-physics venue.

The paper says, in its own words: no prediction differs from quantum mechanics; §4 is compatible with decoherence; §4.5 is an argument it cannot make a result; §5 is a postulate; §6's no-signalling proof is owed; the entangled-sector ontology is open. What is left as candidate contributions: (a) the "specification" of any mechanism — but this is stochasticity, hazard linear in |ψ|², site-independent timing, and nonlocal exclusivity, which is the Born rule, one world, and no-signalling restated in detector language, and M1 suggests it is not even consistent with quantum mechanics as written; (b) the exact-model results — physics, not philosophy; (c) the detector taxonomy — good pedagogy; (d) the honesty — a virtue, not a contribution.

I do not say reject, because I think there is a paper here that the authors have nearly written. The sharpest thing in the manuscript is a *negative* result they state only in passing: that a real-wave, one-world, no-new-constant programme forced to reproduce quantum mechanics is pushed to exactly the projection postulate at the decoherence crossover, and that nothing in the physics of the detector — not the record, not the amplifier, not the cascade, which is "photon-agnostic" and "contributes no statistics" — can move the outcome weights. That is a detector-physics argument for why the Born weights cannot come from detector dynamics, and with M1's time-ordering result added it becomes a no-go for the whole "hazard at the absorber" family. A philosophy venue would count that.

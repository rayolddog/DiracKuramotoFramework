## 2. Summary (to fix terms)

The paper states an interpretation and prices it: the quantum state is real (on configuration/Fock space for more than one quantum); there is one world; the Heisenberg cut is the decoherence crossover, whose location and width are decoherence theory's own, and it is given a physical *address* — the absorption vertex of a detector site — by postulate; and outcome selection is the quantum-jump unravelling of photodetection read as what happens, with site-local absorbing jump operators, Born branch weights put in, a nonlocal no-jump renormalization (the accountable author's "pull on the shared waveform"), Lüders update on the partner wing, no-signalling from the Lindblad average, and a preferred foliation chosen rather than forced. Its one result is negative: a two-month programme to derive the Born weights from a synchronization substrate at the detector produced exponents of 1.5 (first-to-align) or 2.1–2.2 (memoryless energy-linear hazard) or spectrum-dependent values 1.08–2.21, never Born without importing the golden rule's structure, and staggered-arrival runs falsified the v1.0 hazard; the paper reads this as one model family being pushed onto the projection postulate, and notes that the general form is Gleason/Busch plus Pearle/Gisin. The nearest neighbour is the transactional interpretation; the paper claims to add to RTI's placement of the cut only a rate-balance location with a regime, a dense-record requirement, an echo/flip distinction, and the negative result.

## 4. Major concerns

**M1. The transactional derivation dispute is still misattributed, and §2 and §5.3 now disagree with each other about what was disputed.** §2 says: "Kastner and Cramer claim to derive the Born rule for radiative processes from the requirement that a real photon needs both an emission and an absorption vertex; that derivation has been disputed [Marchildon 2017; Kastner 2017 reply]." I re-read Marchildon 2017. It does not dispute the Born-rule derivation. Its target is Kastner's *quantitative criterion for absorber response* — the identification of the coupling amplitude e as the amplitude, and α as the probability, of confirmation-wave generation — which he attacks with the charge-12e argument (a probability exceeding one) and with the buckyball-interference estimate; his conclusion is that this leaves "the measurement problem unsolved and absorbers ill-defined." The emission-times-absorption squaring argument (Kastner & Cramer 2018 §2, eqs. 13–15) is not engaged. §5.3, by contrast, gets the second half right — "the coupling amplitude's role as a probability of absorber response have been disputed [... Marchildon 2017; Kastner 2017]" — but then attributes a dispute of "the confirmation wave's role in that argument" to Boisvert & Marchildon 2013, a paper about contingent absorbers and interaction-free measurements in Cramer's original TI, published five years before "that argument" existed. The v1.2 header's "the transactional dispute attributed correctly" is therefore not yet true. **Fix:** in §2, replace "that derivation has been disputed" with what was disputed — Kastner's quantification of absorber response (coupling amplitude as probability), by Marchildon 2017, with Kastner's 2017 reply — and state separately that the paper's own objection to the squaring step (that identifying the confirmation amplitude with ψ* stipulates the square) is the paper's, or cite where it has actually been made (it is a standard objection to Cramer 1986; Kastner's own 2013 book discusses why the original TI's Born account was incomplete). In §5.3, drop Boisvert & Marchildon from the sentence about the 2018 argument, or cite it for what it does: the absorber-definition problem in original TI.

**M2. The TI price comparison is one-sided and states TI's claim as a fact about TI.** §2: "this paper carries a foliation (§6) that TI does not, which is the largest difference of price between the two and cuts against this paper." §5.3: "TI claims to be so without a foliation and this paper is not." The second is charitable; the first is not, and the paper never says what TI pays instead. The literature's principal objection to TI is exactly here: Maudlin's contingent-absorber challenge (in Maudlin 2011, which the paper cites for other purposes) argues that Cramer's pseudotime account of transaction formation cannot be made consistent without an ordering that TI officially disowns; Cramer's reply is a hierarchy of transactions; Kastner's reply (2013 book; "immune to the Maudlin challenge", 2016) is that offers and confirmations are not in spacetime — which is precisely the possibilist ontology the paper, one sentence earlier, says makes PTI "not a neighbour on this paper's first commitment." So the fair comparison is: TI avoids a foliation either by an ordering it does not name (Cramer) or by leaving spacetime (Kastner); this paper pays a foliation to stay on spacetime for one quantum and on configuration space for many. That is a trade, not a deficit. **Fix:** one sentence in §2 and one in §5.3 stating the counter-price and citing Maudlin 2011 (the TI chapter), Kastner 2013 (CUP — the canonical PTI source, currently absent from a reference list that cites four other Kastner papers), and at least one of Berkovitz 2008 / Lewis 2013 / Marchildon 2006 on causal loops. Also reconsider "largest difference of price": on the paper's own §3.1, configuration-space realism is the larger bill.

**M3. The abstract's headline sentence contradicts §5.2.** Abstract: the negative result is "that nothing in the physics of a detector, not its record, its amplifier, or its cascade, and not a synchronization substrate, can move the outcome weights." §5.2 then reports that a synchronization substrate moves the outcome exponent to 1.5, to 2.1–2.2, and from 1.08 to 2.21 as the detuning spectrum is narrowed. The substrate moves the weights freely; what it cannot do is *reproduce Born* without importing the golden rule's structure, and where it does not import it, it predicts departures that are not observed. Two different claims are blended: for the record/amplifier/cascade the claim is "no interference-sensitive weight" (von Neumann mobility plus a POVM efficiency); for the substrate the claim is "wrong exponent unless the answer is put in." The abstract sentence, read literally, is false against the paper's own §5.2. **Fix:** "…and that a synchronization substrate reproduces the Born weights only by importing the golden rule's structure, and otherwise predicts departures from them that are not seen." Same sentence recurs in §9 ("all leave the outcome weights where the projection postulate puts them") and needs the same fix.

**M4. Two numbers in §4.2 rest on a run that is not in the cited record.** "in the untested Γ/K = 1.5–7 region a re-run by a reviewer found the rate-based midpoint to move by a factor of four with the observation time" and "the rate-based midpoint drifting from 1.54K toward 2K/√3 at long observation times." `heisenberg_cut_recoverability/` contains no script, prediction, or result file for either; `REVIEW_RUNS_RESULTS.md` covers Γ/K = 0.2, 1, 10, 100, 1000 only; grepping the record for "factor of four", "1.5–7" and "2K/√3" returns nothing. The paper's stated method is that every number is cited to the calculation that produced it, predictions first; these two are cited to a reviewer's private re-run in a folder the reader is told exists but which is not the record. Two related uncited numbers: the §4.3 table's commitment timescale "of order 10 fs–1 ps (literature-typical, spanning two orders)" carries no citation in the short form (the long form cited Sabbah & Riffe 2002; the short form dropped it and the number); and "every room-temperature semiconductor detector, at Γ/K of order 10⁵" is asserted, not derived — on the long form's own estimate K depends on the photon's mode volume (≈ 7 × 10⁷ s⁻¹ for a nanosecond photon, ≈ 7 × 10¹¹ s⁻¹ for a 100-fs photon), so with Γ ~ 10¹³–10¹⁴ s⁻¹ the ratio spans 10² to 10⁶. Γ ≫ K survives; "of order 10⁵" does not. **Fix:** add the reviewer's sweep to `heisenberg_cut_recoverability/` as a scripted run with predictions and results, or drop the two numbers and say only "protocol-dependent, untested"; cite the 10 fs–1 ps figure; state the assumption behind 10⁵ or replace it with the range.

**M5. §7 contradicts §4.2 on the crossover width.** §7: the crossover "would show in a strongly coupled cavity-QED or rare-earth system as a detuning crossover of half-width 2K in the echo-recoverable fraction." §4.2 (and the abstract) say the echo/rate-based half-width is √2 K (drifting from 1.54K), and 2K is the half-point of the *occupation*, a different convention by √3. The v1.2 header says this very confusion was corrected; it survives in §7. **Fix:** "of half-width of order K (√2 K in the rate convention, 2K in the occupation convention; §4.2)."

**M6. The header's account of the adjudications is incomplete, and its ratification claim is stronger than the ledger's.** Header v1.1: "Three of these were adjudications the sponsor had reserved; they were made on the first author's stated recommendations while the sponsor slept, and each is reversible with one edit." Header v1.3: "the three adjudications made overnight stand under that instruction unless he reverses them." The manuscript never says which three. In the text only one is flagged as such (§3.1: "an adjudication the accountable author had reserved and the first author made on his stated recommendation"). The ledger names them: concede §4.5; adopt configuration-space realism; centre the paper on the no-go — and, at v1.2, says they are "three adjudications awaiting the accountable author" which "the sponsor must ratify or reverse." v1.3 converts a blanket instruction to "apply the post-review modifications" into standing ratification of three interpretive decisions the accountable author had explicitly reserved. That may be what he intends; the manuscript should say so in his voice, per item. And "reversible with one edit" is not true of two of them: conceding §4.5 and centring on the no-go are carried by the title, the abstract, §1, §4.1, §4.5, §5.2 and §9. **Fix:** name the three in the header; for each, one clause stating whether the accountable author has ratified it; delete "with one edit."

**M7. The general form of the negative result is stated without its premise.** §5.2: "any probability assignment on the projectors of a Hilbert space of dimension three or more is the Born rule [Gleason 1957; Busch 2003]." Gleason's theorem needs the assignment to be additive over orthogonal projectors (a frame function; noncontextuality); without that premise the sentence is false, and with it the following sentence — that hidden-variable theories "that reproduce the Born rule by a different route" are untouched — has its explanation, which the paper currently omits: they escape by contextuality. Busch 2003 extends the result to POVMs in every dimension including two, so "dimension three or more" is Gleason's scope, not Busch's. This matters because the paper's one result is now framed as a corollary of these theorems. **Fix:** "any additive (noncontextual) probability assignment on the projectors … [Gleason 1957], and on the effects of any dimension [Busch 2003]," and one clause saying that contextual assignments are what the theorem does not reach.

## 6. Specific questions for the author

1. Which are the three adjudications, and has the accountable author ratified each one individually, in his own words, or only issued the blanket instruction the header quotes?
2. Where is the reviewer's Γ/K = 1.5–7 sweep — script, predictions, output? If it cannot be added to the record, will the two numbers be removed?
3. On TI: does the paper accept that its objection to Kastner & Cramer — that identifying the confirmation amplitude with ψ* stipulates the square — applies symmetrically to its own clause 1, "the golden rule's matrix element squared"? 4. If neither, "a foliation that TI does not [carry]" should become "a foliation TI claims not to need."
5. In the Γ ≫ K regime, where the absorber is adiabatically eliminated and "the address is then by postulate only," what physical content does "located" retain beyond the decoherence crossover being at Γ/2?
6. Clause 3's kinematic exclusivity is argued for a one-photon state. For a weak coherent state, or a two-photon state on two detectors, what does the postulate say about multiple sinks, and is the "one sink per quantum" wording still right?

## 7. Rubric scores (1–5)

- **Novelty: 2.** What is new is, by the paper's own statement, narrow — a regime-qualified location and a dense-record requirement that are decoherence theory's, an echo/flip distinction that is a definition, and a negative result for one discarded model family at diagnostic budget; the novelty claim is accurate, which is to the paper's credit and not to its score.
- **Internal consistency: 3.** The formal postulate, §4, §6 and §7 hang together; but the abstract contradicts §5.2 (M3), §7 contradicts §4.2 (M5), §2 contradicts §5.3 on what Marchildon disputed (M1), and §5.1 contradicts itself on how many clauses are postulated.
- **Evidential grounding: 3.** Exact models and a scripted race tie the cut claims and the negative result to something outside the text, and the numbers match the record; two §4.2 numbers and two detector timescales do not (M4).
- **Reproducibility: 4.** Scripts, predictions-first files, scored results, hashed validation campaigns, and diagnostic labels carried through; the missing reviewer sweep is the one gap.
- **Citation integrity: 3.** Every reference I checked is real and bibliographically correct, and the quantum-optics, collapse-model and relativistic-collapse citations are apt; against that, the transactional dispute is misattributed in two places after two rounds of "fixed," Boisvert & Marchildon is cited anachronistically, Salart is mislabelled, Gleason is quoted without its premise, the Copenhagen/QBism paragraph is uncited, Kastner 2013 is absent, and two entries are orphans.

## 8. Overall assessment (0–5)

**2** — weak but in scope: an honest, priced restatement of the projection postulate at the decoherence crossover with a well-documented negative result, still carrying claim-level errors in its abstract and in its treatment of its nearest neighbour that a third pass should not have left.

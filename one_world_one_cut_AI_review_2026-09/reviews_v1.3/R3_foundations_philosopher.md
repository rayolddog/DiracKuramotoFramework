## 2. Summary (to fix terms)

The manuscript states an interpretation of quantum measurement and prices it. The quantum state is real and lives on configuration (Fock) space; there is exactly one world; the Heisenberg cut is the decoherence crossover, whose location and width in the smallest exact models are decoherence theory's own, and which is given — by postulate — a physical address at the absorption vertex of a detector site; and the outcome is selected by a stochastic jump at that vertex, formally the quantum-jump unravelling of photodetection (site-local absorbing jump operators, no-jump renormalization between jumps, Lindblad ensemble average) read as what actually happens. The paper says explicitly that this is the projection postulate placed at the vertex, that it derives none of its three clauses, that no-signalling is a theorem of the Lindblad average, that the nonlocal update is a "pull" on one shared configuration-space object with no propagation speed, and that it carries a preferred foliation chosen rather than forced. Its one claimed result is negative: a real-wave, one-world, no-new-constant programme built on detector-side hazards is pushed to Born branch weights at the decoherence crossover, and nothing downstream of the vertex or in a synchronization substrate can move them — which the paper then says is a corollary of Gleason and Gisin for one model family. No prediction differs from quantum mechanics.

Very few interpretation papers state their non-novelty this plainly. The disclosure in §4.1 (the coupling-flip definition failed calibration and was replaced before results were opened) and the pre-registered predictions in the record are a model of how such work should be logged.

## 4. Major concerns

### M1. §6 mislocates the nonlocality: "the nonlocality is the sharing" contradicts "nonlocality in Bell's sense" two paragraphs later, and would make Everett nonlocal too.

The picture paragraph says: *"The nonlocality is the sharing; nothing crosses the space between."* The trade paragraph says: *"this paper buys one world with nonlocality in Bell's sense, never signalling."* These are not the same claim. Non-separability of the state — "the shared waveform of an entangled pair is one object" — is common to every ψ-ontic view including Everett, and Wallace & Timpson (2010, which the paper cites) argue that a theory with exactly this non-separable state and *no* collapse is local in the relevant sense. What is nonlocal in Bell's sense in this paper is not the sharing but the **pull**: a real, outcome-dependent change of the object's amplitude at the partner's wing, occurring at the moment (on the foliation) of a click or a null elsewhere. That change is what §5.1 correctly calls "itself nonlocal." The paragraph then blunts its own point by adding "nothing crosses the space between" — true, but the standard collapse-theory line, and not what distinguishes nonlocality from mere non-separability.

*Fix.* Replace the sentence with something like: "The nonlocality is the pull — a settings- and outcome-dependent change of one object with support at both wings — and the sharing is what lets one pull reach both. Everett has the sharing without the pull; that is why it can claim locality and this paper cannot." The §2 Everett entry should say the same thing, since it is the whole content of the Everett–collapse contrast on locality.

### M2. §6 is not self-consistent on "first": it says "first" is frame-dependent and, in the same paragraph, that the paper's preferred slicing decides it.

Quoting: *"'First' is frame-dependent for spacelike-separated wings, and the joint statistics do not depend on it, because the two wings' jump operators commute; the paper's slicing decides which wing's silence or click pulled the waveform, and nothing observable turns on the decision."* On a preferred foliation "first" is **not** frame-dependent; it is fixed by the foliation and merely unobservable. Frame-dependence of temporal order is the relativity-of-simultaneity fact that obtains *in the absence* of a preferred slicing. The sentence wants the relativistic shrug and the foliation at once. The same slippage appears in §5.1 clause 3 — "no delay and no speed" — which is fine as a statement about a change of one configuration-space object, but the paper has also committed (§6, foliation paragraph) to a slicing on which that change is simultaneous, i.e. has a definite "when" in a definite frame. That is exactly the "infinite speed in a preferred frame" that Salart et al. 2008 bound from below; the paper cites Salart as bounding "a finite collapse speed" without saying that its own picture is the limiting case of what Salart's experiment addresses.

Two residues of the withdrawn v1.1 Lorentz-violation claim survive and contradict §6's "there is no bound to clear":
- §8 item 2: *"…and the clearance of the Lorentz bounds if it cannot."* §6 says the v1.1 phrase "low-energy Lorentz violation whose clearance is owed" "was a contradiction and is withdrawn with it." Then §8.2 cannot keep the clause.
- The long form (`drafts/PAPER_one_world_one_cut.md` §5, "The foliation, priced"), which the manuscript cites as its record, still reads *"As written this is explicit low-energy Lorentz violation confined to measurement… the quantitative demonstration that the residual anisotropy clears every established bound remains owed."* Either update it or add one line to the short form saying the long form's §5 is superseded.

*Fix.* Rewrite the "first" sentence: "'First' is fixed by the preferred slicing and is unobservable; because the wings' jump operators commute, no correlation depends on it." Delete the clause in §8.2. Reconcile the long form.

### M3. The foliation is recorded, not argued — and the argument the paper's own premises require is missing, with Myrvold cited on the wrong side of it.

The paper's own formal statement makes the beable *"the conditional state on that split, not the universal state"* (§5.1). A conditional state is conditional on *what has happened*, i.e. on the jumps and nulls to the past of something. The something must be either (i) a preferred hypersurface (a foliation), (ii) an arbitrary spacelike hypersurface, with the state hypersurface-relative and no hypersurface privileged — the Aharonov–Albert / Fleming / Myrvold option — or (iii) nothing, because the beables are the events themselves and the state is bookkeeping (Tumulka's flash ontology). The paper chooses (i) and says only *"The paper does not argue that its choice is forced; it records it, with its cost."*

But it has just shown that (ii) is available at **no additional cost**: *"The jump operators of §5.1 are site-local and commute at spacelike separation, so the joint statistics of spacelike-separated jumps do not depend on their order and no global ordering is needed [Aharonov & Albert 1984; Myrvold 2002]."* That is precisely Myrvold's argument that collapse is compatible with relativity *via hypersurface-relative states*, not an argument that one may as well pick a foliation. Citing Myrvold for "no ordering needed" and then choosing an ordering anyway, with the foliation left on the price list, is citing him on the wrong side. Worse for the paper: Maudlin's standing objection to hypersurface-dependence (that a hyperplane-relative state is not "the state of the world") bites only if one wants local matters of fact to be hyperplane-independent — and this paper, having no local beables (M4), has nothing for that objection to bite on. The paper is thus the *best-placed* ψ-ontic collapse view to take option (ii), and it does not say why it declines.

*Fix.* Either (a) argue for the foliation — e.g. that the paper wants a unique state-of-the-world at each time as part of what "real wave" means, and pay Maudlin's price consciously; or (b) adopt hypersurface-relative conditional states, cite Myrvold correctly, and strike the foliation from the price list in §6, §9 and the abstract; or (c) say plainly that (ii) is cheaper and the foliation is a preference. "Records it" is not an option when the cost is avoidable by the paper's own lights.

### M4. Configuration-space realism is adopted but the three-space bill is not paid: the "physical address" is a label on an operator, and the paper's own reference list (Norsen 2010, uncited) names the debt.

§3.1: *"on this ontology a click is the projection onto a site's absorbing state, and the site's location is inherited from the site operator, not from the state; what the three-space world contains between clicks is the field's expectation values, and that is all the paper can say."* On wavefunction monism (Albert; Ney & Albert — both cited), the site operator's spatial label refers to nothing in the world unless the detector's atoms *have* positions, and on this ontology they have positions only as expectation values of the very state whose "address" is at issue. So the address is either nominal or presupposes a classical background of located sites — Bohr's classical apparatus by the back door, in a paper that distinguishes itself from Bohr precisely on the cut "having an address" (§2). The abstract's new distinction between "computable location" (decoherence's) and "physical address" (by postulate) is welcome, but the postulate confers an address only if there is a three-space to put it in.

Two consistent ways out exist, both already in the reference list:
- **Local beables for clicks**: treat the click as a flash at the site's record — Bell's GRWf, Tumulka 2006 — so the three-space world is the set of absorption events. Then the paper is a *detector-triggered flash theory*, which is an honest and interesting thing to be (and connects to M5).
- **Spacetime state realism** (Wallace & Timpson 2010): the reduced states on spacetime regions are beables, which upgrades "the field's expectation values" from "all the paper can say" to an ontology.
Norsen 2010, *The theory of (exclusively) local beables*, is in the references and is cited nowhere in the body; it is exactly the paper that states this problem for ψ-ontic views and should be cited where §3.1 admits it. The paper also correctly says PBR does not decide the space; it should add that PBR does not decide *between* configuration-space and spacetime state realism either, so the "further choice, made here" is a choice against a named alternative, not against nothing.

*Fix.* Choose one of the two and price it; cite Norsen at §3.1; or state that the address is nominal and drop "physical address" from the abstract.

### M5. Bell 1990 turns against the paper: its law contains a FAPP-defined "detector," and the unravelling underdetermination is not "the residue of §4.5" but an independent stipulation.

§2 uses Bell to motivate adding an event: decoherence justifies the cut's placement "without making the cut a physical discontinuity — the 'for all practical purposes' Bell would not accept [Bell 1990]." §5.1 then concedes: *"this paper selects the jump unravelling on the site–record split that the dense-record criterion of §4.3 defines, and that criterion is graded, so the ontic reading inherits a for-all-practical-purposes element at the split, which is the residue of §4.5 and is not removed."* But the site–record split is not a residue of the recoverability discussion; it is the *definition of where and when the fundamental jump happens*. A law whose jump operators are "the absorbing transition at site *i* into its record" with "record" defined by "dense on the capture timescale" is a law containing the word "measurement" spelled out FAPP — the thing "Against 'measurement'" was written against. The paper cannot both invoke Bell's rejection of FAPP as its reason to add a postulate and define the postulate FAPP without saying that it has failed Bell's test by his own standard.

The second underdetermination is separate and understated. Wiseman & Gambetta 2012 show that *which* unravelling (jump or diffusive) describes the trajectory depends on how the environment — here the record — is read out. So "the jump unravelling is what happens" requires the record channel to be, ontologically, a counting channel, which pushes the von Neumann chain one link down (who counts the record?). The paper's v1.2 paragraph names the non-uniqueness and then attributes the residue to §4.5. It is not the same residue. It is the measurement problem re-appearing at the record.

There is a fair-comparison consequence for §2. Formally, this paper is a **detector-triggered collapse model**: a stochastic jump on the state, ψ-ontic, one world, nonlocal, with Born weights — GRW's structure with the hit rate set by the detector's Γ_i rather than by a universal constant λ, and the localization operator the site's absorbing transition rather than a Gaussian. GRW/CSL are therefore its nearest *formal* relatives, and the genuine trade between them is: GRW's trigger is universal, sharp, and costs new constants that experiments can bound; this paper's trigger is detector-set, FAPP, and costs nothing that any experiment can touch. §2 hides this trade by filing GRW under "new constants / mass or size" and this paper under TI and von Neumann. That is not yet a fair comparison.

*Fix.* In §2's spontaneous-collapse entry, state the structural identity and the trade. In §5.1, separate the two costs: the FAPP split (Bell's objection, owned) and the unravelling choice (Wiseman–Gambetta, a stipulation about the record). Consider whether the flash option of M4 makes the split sharper: a flash theory needs no "record."

### M6. §7's new order-independence paragraph (v1.3) makes a claim that is false for any preferred-frame picture, including the paper's own, and the "branch selected, not phase rotated" contrast it is meant to support is basis-relative.

Quoting §7: *"…a picture in which the first interaction physically sends a phase to the partner would fail the second and third."* The second is the before-before experiment, the third delayed-choice entanglement swapping. Neither refutes a preferred-frame nonlocal update of any kind. Before-before (Zbinden 2001; Stefanov 2002) refutes *multisimultaneity* — the Suarez–Scarani hypothesis that each detector is "first" in its *own* rest frame; a picture in which the first interaction *in a preferred frame* sends anything to the partner passes it, and Salart 2008 then bounds that something's speed from below (> 10⁴ c). Delayed-choice swapping is passed by Bohmian mechanics, which is a preferred-frame theory in which the earlier detections do fix the later ones through the configuration-space wave. And a "phase sent to the partner" that *locally* fixes the partner's outcome is a local hidden variable, excluded by Bell's theorem before any of these experiments were done. So the sentence conflates three rival pictures — LHV (excluded by Bell), multisimultaneity (excluded by before-before), and a preferred-frame nonlocal phase update (excluded by nothing, and the paper's own type) — and claims the experiments discriminate the sponsor's "pull" from the last of these. They do not. Both "a branch selected" and "a phase rotated" are preferred-frame nonlocal updates and pass every test in the paragraph identically.

The contrast itself is imprecise as physics. §6: *"for a polarization singlet, a horizontal click at one wing leaves the partner vertical, which is a branch selected, not a phase rotated."* The partner's conditional state after an H-click at analyzer angle *a* is the pure state |V_a⟩ — vertical *in A's analyzer basis*. Relative to B's own analyzer at angle *b* it is the superposition cos(a−b)|V_b⟩ − sin(a−b)|H_b⟩ (up to convention), a state whose orientation, and therefore whose relative phase in B's basis, was fixed by **A's setting**. That is EPR steering, and the ensemble at B hides it only because no-signalling averages over A's outcomes. "A branch selected" is true in one basis; "not a phase rotated" is false in the other. The honest statement is that the partner's conditional state becomes a setting-dependent pure state, which is what a realist reading of the Lüders rule commits one to and what the picture must own.

*Fix.* Delete the last clause of the §7 paragraph and state what before-before refutes (multisimultaneity) and what Salart bounds (a finite speed in a preferred frame — the paper's own limiting case). Restate the singlet example with the setting-dependence explicit. Do not present the order-independence experiments as adjudicating between two preferred-frame pictures that make identical predictions.

### M7. §5.2's negative result is stated at its true altitude in one sentence and above it in the bold sentence, the abstract, and the conclusion — and the "no new constant" clause does no work in the general form.

The "true altitude" sentence is right: *"this is a negative result for one model family at diagnostic budget, and its general form is not the paper's — [Gleason 1957; Busch 2003] … [Pearle 1976; Gisin 1984] … [Gisin 1989]."* But the bold sentence immediately preceding it is universal — *"nothing in the physics of a detector and no synchronization substrate can move the outcome weights, and the family of 'hazard at the absorber' mechanisms collapses onto the projection postulate"* — and the abstract and §9 repeat it in that form ("its one result is that a real-wave, one-world programme that tries to cannot do so from the physics of the detector"). Three specific problems:
- "Nothing … can move" was tested on one race family, one gated-energy hazard, and one classical amplifier; the universal form is the theorem's, as the next sentence admits. Say the theorem's form once and drop the bold.
- "No new constant" does no work in the general form: Gleason and Gisin do not mention constants, and GRW/CSL, which have them, also reproduce Born weights (to within their bounded deviations). What forces Born is no-signalling plus linearity of the ensemble map. The qualifier reads as if it identified the reason; it does not.
- "Collapses onto the projection postulate" should be "collapses onto Born branch weights": the diffusive unravellings the paper itself mentions have Born weights and are not the projection postulate. The negative result fixes the weights, not the unravelling.

*Fix.* One statement of the result, in the corollary form, used verbatim in the abstract, §5.2, and §9; remove "no new constant" from the general statement (it may stay as a description of the programme tested); replace "the projection postulate" with "the Born weights" in the collapse clause.

### M8 (outside my specialism; an arithmetic check). The three-convention sentence in §4.2 and the abstract does not add up as written, and two of its numbers are not in the permitted record.

§4.2: *"√2 K in the rate convention, 2K for the half-point of the occupation 2K²/(Δ² + 4K²), a factor √3 apart … with the rate-based midpoint drifting from 1.54K toward 2K/√3 at long observation times."* √2 K and 2K are a factor √2 apart, not √3; the √3 relates 2K/√3 to 2K, i.e. a *long-time limit* to the occupation half-point, and that limit is a third number the sentence introduces after asserting the factor. The abstract repeats "convention-dependent by a factor √3" without the qualifier. Neither 2K/√3 nor the "factor of four with the observation time" in the untested Γ/K = 1.5–7 region appears in `RESULTS.md`, `REVIEW_RUNS_RESULTS.md`, `STAGE2_RESULTS.md` or the ledger; the manuscript attributes them to "a re-run by a reviewer," which lives in a folder I was instructed not to read. I cannot audit them and a reader of the public record cannot either.

*Fix.* State which two conventions are √3 apart and derive 2K/√3 in one line or cite the script; put the reviewer re-run into the public record beside the other runs; make the abstract's clause match.

The novelty claim is now stated accurately, which is why this is a 2 and not a 1.
- **Internal consistency — 3.** Much improved since the formal postulate was rewritten, but §6 is not self-consistent on "first" (M2), §6 and §8.2 disagree on Lorentz bounds (M2), "the nonlocality is the sharing" contradicts "nonlocality in Bell's sense" (M1), §7's new paragraph claims a discrimination its own picture cannot survive (M6), and the √3 sentence does not add up (M8).

- **Reproducibility — 4.** Scripts, predictions fixed before running, and results scored; every number I checked against the permitted record matched. Docked one for the two §4.2 numbers that live only in a non-public reviewer re-run.
- **Citation integrity — 4.** The sources are real and, in my specialism, correctly characterized — with three exceptions: Myrvold 2002 is cited on the wrong side of the foliation question (M3); Norsen 2010 and Kjaergaard 2020 are orphaned; Mandel 1958 is stretched.

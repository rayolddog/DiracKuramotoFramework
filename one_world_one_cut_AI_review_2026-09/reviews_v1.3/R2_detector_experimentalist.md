## 1. Recommendation

**Major revision.** *Venue note:* a foundations-of-physics / philosophy-of-physics venue (the paper is an interpretation with a priced restatement of the projection postulate); it is not a quantum-optics or detector-physics paper and would not survive review as one. *Authorship:* byline honest as it stands.

## 2. Summary (to fix terms)

The paper is a collapse interpretation in Maudlin's sense: the wave (the field's quantum state, on configuration/Fock space) is real; there is one world; the Heisenberg cut is the decoherence crossover, whose location and width the paper computes in toy models and finds to be decoherence theory's own; and the one thing decoherence lacks — an outcome — is put in as a postulate located at the absorption vertex of a detector site, in the form of the quantum-jump (photodetection) unravelling of the Lindblad dynamics read as what happens, with the no-jump renormalization ("the pull on the shared waveform") supplying the nonlocality and a preferred slicing supplying the order. The paper's one claimed result is negative: a real-wave, one-world programme that tries to derive the outcome weights from detector-side stochastic hazards (a synchronization race, an energy-linear hazard) is pushed back onto the Born branch weights, which it recognizes as a corollary of Gleason and Gisin. No new constant; no prediction differing from quantum mechanics.

## 3. Strengths

- The postulate is now written as a stochastic master equation with the correct absorbing jump operator, and the two costs of reading an unravelling realistically (non-uniqueness of the unravelling; the beable being the conditional state on a chosen site–record split) are stated as costs. That is the right form and the right honesty.
- Run B is a genuine self-refutation, run on the programme's own model, recorded, and propagated into the postulate. The identification of the accountable author's "pull" with the no-jump renormalization is correct quantum optics: for a single photon split α|A⟩ + β|B⟩ with A reached first and a null there, the conditional weight at B rises exactly as the picture says, and the unconditional click law stays linear in |β|². The picture is a fair plain-language rendering of that.
- Run A reproduces exactly: I re-ran the record's script and obtained its five rows to three decimals.
- The registration row (§4.3) is now right about real detectors: the position- and energy-dependent internal efficiency of a nanowire [Renema et al. 2014; Engel et al. 2015], the position-dependent breakdown probability of a SPAD, afterpulsing and crosstalk as clicks with no vertex. The v1.0 "photon-agnostic, no which-site weight" was wrong and the correction is correct.
- The echo/coupling-flip distinction (§4.1) is a useful methodological point for anyone proposing to "locate the cut by a reversal protocol", and the paper records that it got it wrong first.
- The experiments cited in §7 for order independence are the right family, and the statement that a local-clock model is sub-classical (CHSH ≤ √2, `tests/bell_phase.py` in the record) is correct and correctly used.

## 4. Major concerns

**M1. "Set by the record channel and hence by temperature" is not true of the detectors the paper names.** Abstract and §4.2: the Γ ≫ K crossover is "Γ/2, set by the record channel and hence by temperature ... the regime of every room-temperature semiconductor detector". The inference comes from Run A's A2 ("since Γ(T) is the temperature-dependent quantity, temperature-set"). For a band absorber the record channel at the vertex is hot-carrier relaxation: carrier–carrier scattering at 10–100 fs and optical-phonon emission at ~100 fs–1 ps for a carrier ~1 eV above the band edge. Those rates are set by the excess energy and the density of final states, with temperature entering only through Bose factors that are negligible at optical excess energies. The same is true of NbN or WSi at 1–2 K and of a TES at 100 mK: Γ is still 10¹³–10¹⁴ s⁻¹, set by disorder and excess energy. Temperature governs the dark-count rate, the superconducting gap, and the *slow* stages — not the vertex-stage Γ. So "hence by temperature" is a non sequitur for every detector in the regime, and the §2 analogy to Joos–Zeh and Zurek (whose rates are temperature-dependent because the scatterer flux is thermal) does not transfer. The genuinely temperature-set Γ(T) is that of the T₂-limited absorbers (Er:YSO per Böttger et al. 2006), which are in the *other* regime. *Fix:* say "record-set"; delete "hence by temperature" from the abstract and §4.2, or restrict it explicitly to T₂-limited absorbers; remove "the temperature dial" from the list of things the regime statement vindicates. Also: "Γ/K of order 10⁵" is asserted with no derivation. The ledger shows where it came from (a single-photon Rabi frequency of ~20 MHz for an atomic dipole in a micron spot against a carrier dephasing rate of 10¹³ s⁻¹) — i.e. K is borrowed from an atom while the same paragraph says that for a band absorber "neither Δ nor a 'site' is given by the Hamiltonian". State the estimate and its borrowed K, or drop the number.

**M2. The headline crossover formula is a five-point fit, and the abstract's small-Γ limit is not the rate convention's.** The §4.2 table says "√(Γ²/4 + 2K²) to 15 % from Γ/K = 0.2 to 1000", while the prose says "and not in between". The table is wrong as a range statement. Re-running the record's own script in the untested region gives rate-based midpoints (Δ/K) of

| Γ/K | t = 1 | t = 3 | t = 10 | t = 30 | formula | Γ/2K |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3.76 | 1.01 | 1.04 | 1.09 | 1.73 | 1.00 |
| 3 | 3.92 | 1.16 | 0.93 | 0.92 | 2.06 | 1.50 |
| 5 | 4.41 | 2.35 | 1.90 | 1.81 | 2.87 | 2.50 |

— off the formula by factors 1.6–2.2 at long observation time, and moving by a factor 4.3 with t at Γ/K = 3, which confirms the prose's "factor of four" (a claim the candidate attributes to "a re-run by a reviewer" with no output file in the record; put one beside `gamma_regime_sweep_output.txt`). Separately, the abstract's "√2 K in the rate convention" for Γ ≪ K is the fit formula's Γ → 0 value, not a limit the rate convention reaches: at Γ/K = 0.2, 0.05 and 0.02 the rate-based midpoint passes through 1.41 transiently and continues to 1.18–1.19 at Γt = 60 and is still falling toward 2/√3 = 1.155 — which is where the slower eigenmode's excited-state weight (1 − Δ/√(Δ²+4K²))/2 reaches 1/4, a two-line derivation the paper should give. The paper's own sentence concedes the drift ("toward 2K/√3 at long observation times") while presenting √2 K as the rate convention's number and calling the 2K/2K/√3 gap "a factor √3" as if it were a two-convention matter; it is three numbers (fit limit √2 K; occupation half-point 2K; long-time rate half-point 2K/√3) plus the 256-mode runs (1.3–2.0). *Fix:* present the formula as an interpolation checked at five points with regime-limit validity only; give the two derived small-Γ limits; drop √2 K from the abstract; correct the table row to "Γ/2 for Γ/K ≥ 10; between K and 2K for Γ/K ≤ 1; not characterized between".

**M3. The ontic jump unravelling is silent about the very experiments the paper cites from circuit QED.** §7: the coupling dial "has in substance been done [Katz et al. 2008; Murch et al. 2013; Hatridge et al. 2013]"; §4.3: "the coherent, reversible onset of a monitored quantum jump [Minev et al. 2019] is suggestive of the same [reversible capture] for a single system". All four are continuous dispersive-readout experiments with a parametric amplifier and heterodyne/homodyne detection — diffusive records, in which no photon-counting jump occurs anywhere the experimenter looks. §5.1 selects the *jump* unravelling "on the site–record split" as what happens. Then on the paper's own ontology the jumps in Murch or Hatridge are the absorption vertices of the readout photons in the amplifier chain — the HEMT's 2DEG, the mixer diode, the ADC comparator — band absorbers in the Γ ≫ K regime where "the address is then by postulate only", thousands of them per measurement, each projecting the qubit slightly (Wiseman & Gambetta's result that jump unravellings with a strong local oscillator tend to diffusive ones). That is consistent, but the paper never says it, and saying it makes the "address" of most contemporary quantum measurement a room-temperature digitizer. Minev 2019 is the sharper problem: its finding is that under continuous monitoring the transition has a deterministic, coherent, reversible pre-history given the null record — i.e. that clause 2's "memoryless event ... by chance ... at any moment" is the photon-counting unravelling and *not* what a differently monitored system does. Citing it as "suggestive" of reversible capture mischaracterizes it; it is direct evidence for the paper's own stated cost (the unravelling is detector-dependent), and belongs in §5.1's cost paragraph, not in §4.3's capture row. *Fix:* one paragraph in §5.1 saying what happens, on this ontology, in a dispersive-readout experiment; move Minev to that paragraph and characterize it as detector-dependence of the trajectory.

**M4. The order-independence experiments are the right ones but are not described correctly.** (a) "the before-before experiments, in which each detector is first in its own rest frame ... [Zbinden et al. 2001; Stefanov et al. 2002]": Zbinden 2001 moved an *absorber* (a black surface on a rotating wheel, with no readout) and is correctly described; Stefanov 2002 moved *beam splitters* (travelling acoustic waves in acousto-optic modulators) with the detectors at rest, testing Suarez's beam-splitter version of multisimultaneity. The paper's description fits the first and not the second. Note also that Zbinden's moving absorber is an unread absorber — exactly the kind of "absorption with a dense record channel, not in a laboratory" at which §6 says the event occurs — so it is the one experiment that tests the paper's own placement with a frame-dependent ordering; that is worth saying, and it passes. (b) §5.1 clause 3: "a finite collapse speed is exactly what the before-before experiments bound [Salart et al. 2008]". Salart 2008 is not a before-before experiment: it is an 18 km Franson interferometer run through the Earth's rotation to bound the speed of a hypothetical influence in any preferred frame with v < 10⁻³ c to > 10⁴ c. §7 gets this right ("the before-before and 24-hour experiments"); clause 3 conflates them. (c) "delayed-choice entanglement swapping ... gives the same correlations as when it is made before [Ma et al. 2012]": Ma et al. ran no "before" arm; the comparison is with quantum mechanics and with earlier swapping. More importantly, on the paper's own slicing the honest description is that photons 1 and 4, jumping first, were *never* entangled; Victor's later Bell-state or separable-state measurement on 2 and 3 sorts the 1–4 record into entangled-looking or separable subensembles. The paper should say that, because it is the cleanest demonstration that "the pull" on a partner is conditional-state bookkeeping in the preferred frame with no observable trace — and it commits the paper to a definite (unobservable) claim, which is what an ontology should do. (d) "Bell tests with spacelike-separated, fast-switched measurements" carries no citation (Aspect et al. 1982; Weihs et al. 1998; the 2015 loophole-free tests). (e) "a picture in which the first interaction physically sends a phase to the partner would fail the second and third" is asserted, not argued: a phase message at > 10⁴ c passes Salart, the before-before experiments refute a *per-device-frame ordering* not a message, and delayed-choice swapping refutes nothing about messages because all the measurements commute. Say what the experiments refute (multisimultaneity; a preferred-frame influence slower than 10⁴ c) rather than a straw alternative.

**M5. The commitment/registration rows are not yet right for real detectors.** (a) §4.3: commitment is "the absorption vertex and thermalization at one site", and three lines later "in a calorimeter thermalization is the registration". For a TES the chain is absorption → hot-electron cloud (fs) → electron–electron thermalization (ps) → temperature rise of the whole electron bath read by a SQUID (μs). Which thermalization is which? Say "local carrier thermalization is part of commitment; thermalization of the electron bath is registration". (b) Dark counts are placed at the vertex in clause 1 ("a background rate, thermal or current-assisted, exists at every site and gives the dark counts a weight the wave alone would not") and at registration in §4.3 ("adds photon-independent clicks"), and the formal statement has neither: c_i = √Γ_i|g_i, r_i⟩⟨e_i| has zero rate on an unexcited site. SNSPD dark counts (vortex crossing, no absorption) and SPAD dark counts (thermal generation and tunnelling in the multiplication region) are registration-stage; photocathode thermionic emission is vertex-stage. Add the dark-count jump operators or move dark counts to registration; do not have both. (c) "of order 10 fs–1 ps" is the hot-carrier thermalization time, not the vertex's duration — a golden-rule vertex has none — and §7's "vertex latencies of 10 fs are inaccessible to any readout [Korzh et al. 2020]" is false as a general statement: Korzh bounds an SNSPD's timing jitter (sub-3 ps); attosecond streaking resolves photoemission delays from solids at 10–100 as (Cavalieri et al., *Nature* 449, 1029 (2007); Ossiander et al., *Nature* 561, 374 (2018)). Say "inaccessible to a photon-counting readout". (d) "one quantum, one closure", "one sink per quantum": two-photon-absorption vertices (one site, two quanta) exist and are used as detectors (silicon TPA at telecom wavelengths); the formalism accommodates them (c ∝ a²|g⟩⟨e|), the slogan does not. State the exception.

**M6. §5.2 and Open Problem 4 characterize the race record selectively, omitting its strongest finding.** The candidate: a memoryless energy-linear hazard "gives 2.1–2.2, the Born value plus a tenth of a power that persists across grid, window, pulse, noise, and commitment time". The record's spectral sweep (`adler_two_channel_exploratory/RESULTS.md`, 2026-09-04) shows the exponent runs from 1.08 (all clocks resonant) through 1.52, 1.78, 2.01 to 2.21 (flat) as the detuning spectrum is narrowed, that "Born is exact at one width only", and — finding 3 — that the mechanism "is falsified in the narrow-band regime by the same rule it was built to reproduce" (two strongly coupled identical absorbers would split a photon by amplitude, 0.86 : 0.14 at 10°, where quantum mechanics gives 0.97 : 0.03). That is a stronger and more physical reason for dropping synchronization than the extreme-value residual the candidate reports, and it is a statement about cavity QED that belongs in a paper citing cavity QED as its Γ ≪ K regime. The ledger's item 8 says the "compatible domain" and the "discriminating regime" were stated in the single paper's §4.2; they are not in v1.3. *Fix:* one sentence in §5.2 on the spectrum dependence and the narrow-band falsification; recast Open Problem 4 accordingly ("consistent with an extreme-value ingredient" is the record's reading of only the deterministic ~0.09 part at late commitment).

**M7. The record and the long form still contradict the candidate on the rows v1.1–v1.3 changed.** (a) `NEGATIVE_RESULT.md` (edited 2026-09-05 for the registration correction) still says exclusivity "must act within about a thousandth of the commitment time, orders of magnitude faster than light crosses a beamsplitter" and still specifies the actualization law as "linear in the site's absorbed energy ... plus a one-quantum constraint acting faster than light across the detector" — both withdrawn or falsified in the candidate (clause 3; Run B). (b) The long form's §4.1 clause 3 retains "within a thousandth of the time the sink took to open, faster than light crosses the detector" with no v1.2 note, although §5 of the same file received a v1.3 paragraph; its §3.4 prose still says "Everything downstream is a deterministic cascade that copies what the vertex decided and contributes no statistics of its own" directly beneath a v1.2 registration row that says the opposite; its §5 still carries "explicit low-energy Lorentz violation confined to measurement ... the quantitative demonstration ... remains owed", which the candidate's v1.2 calls a contradiction; its width table keeps the transmon row the candidate withdrew; and its Bloch–Siegert range is −(1.1–1.5) K²/ω against the candidate's −(1.1–1.7) (the record supports 1.10–1.72 across Γ = 0.2–0.5, so the candidate is right and the long form is stale). The ledger's item 12 lists "candidate/long-form divergence" among the residues "fixed in v1.2"; on these rows it was not. Since the paper repeatedly says "the repository is the record of how every number here was obtained", the record must not contradict the paper on its load-bearing rows. *Fix:* propagate, or mark each divergence in place with a pointer to the candidate.

**M8. What "silence" means in the pull picture needs one sentence, or the picture misleads.** §5.1 clause 2 and §6: "while the wave passes the nearer detector and no sink opens there, the shared waveform is pulled toward the farther one ... the nearer detector's silence has already acted on the farther detector's chance". In a real Bell test the nearer detector has η ≈ 0.1–0.9, and on this paper's ontology every unread absorber in the path (fibre, coatings, the SPAD's dead layer) is a site with a dense record channel at which real jumps occur. The laboratory "silence" (no registered click) is therefore a mixture over (no jump yet) and (jump into an unread record); the pull acts on the *ontic* conditional state, and the experimenter's conditional state is the mixture. As written, a reader takes the sentence as a claim about the recorded null, and the accountable author's picture — which is a good one — is then heard as saying more than the unravelling says. This is expository, but it is the paper's one plain-language rendering of its central postulate.

## 5. Minor / presentational

- §4.2: "the photon amplitude decays directly at rate K²Γ/(Δ² + Γ²/4)" — that is the probability rate; the amplitude rate is half of it. The paper elsewhere is careful about exactly this convention.
- Kjaergaard et al. 2020 and Norsen 2010 are in the reference list but cited nowhere in the text (the transmon sentence in §4.2 now has no citation).
- Quantum-dot width: Kuhlmann et al.'s transform-limited linewidth (~1 μeV at ~1.3 eV) gives Γ/ω ≈ 7 × 10⁻⁷; "10⁻⁶–10⁻⁵" is high by up to an order. Alkali (1.6 × 10⁻⁸) and clock (~10⁻¹⁸) are right.
- HOM sentence in clause 3: "half the time" is for a 50:50 splitter; say so.
- §4.4: "one click at saturated internal efficiency, the only class for which per-event Born claims are properly made" — internal efficiency saturates in SNSPDs at high bias and in TESs, never in SPADs; say that the Born claim is conditional on a click and the POVM is ηΠ, so that the class is not empty.
- Steck 2025 "rev. 2.3.4": I could not verify the revision number; check.
- The title's "One Cut" against §6's "the slicing enters ... at every absorption with a dense record channel and not only in a laboratory": there are as many cuts as absorptions. Fine as a title if the text says so once.
- The provenance header is a page long; move it to a revision-history appendix and leave a one-line pointer.
- §7 "Retrieval efficiency versus storage time ... collapses in Γ(T)·t ... which is the definition of T₂" — for an AFC the storage-time decay also carries the comb finesse and, after spin transfer, the spin T₂; fine as a consistency test, but say which T₂.
- Clause 1: "weighted in its detuning by the crossover of §4.2" mixes the branch weight (|ψ_i|²) with the rate (Γ_i × excited-state population, which carries the detuning); the formal statement has it right, the prose does not.

## 7. Rubric scores (1–5)

- **Novelty — 2.** What is new is narrow and the paper says so: the echo/flip distinction, the dense-record requirement, a regime statement for the location, and a negative result for one model family that the paper itself reduces to a corollary of Gleason and Gisin; the "pull" is the no-jump renormalization. The novelty claim is accurate.
- **Internal consistency — 3.** Much improved (the formal postulate, the foliation argument, the registration row); remaining contradictions are in the paper's own numbers and rows (M2 table vs prose; √2 K vs the drift; thermalization in two rows; dark counts in two places; Salart as before-before).
- **Evidential grounding — 3.** Exact toy models, all textbook and correctly labelled as such; real experiments cited as consistency tests; the numbers I checked against the record match (Run A, Run B, the 36 %, the 1.3–2.0, the 1.7 → 5, the Bloch–Siegert and Stuart–Landau rows), with the exceptions in M1 (10⁵ underived), M2 (the 15 % range claim), and M6 (the spectrum dependence omitted).
- **Reproducibility — 4.** Scripts and outputs are beside the claims; Run A re-ran in seconds and reproduced every row; the race package is labelled diagnostic and the label is carried. Deduction for the "reviewer re-run" cited with no record file and for the record/long-form divergence (M7).
- **Citation integrity — 3.** The sources are real and the detector, photodetection and quantum-trajectory references are the right ones; but Stefanov 2002 is mischaracterized, Salart 2008 is mislabelled in clause 3, Korzh 2020 is over-read, Minev 2019 is under-read, Ma 2012's "same as before" is not something Ma measured, the Bell tests are uncited, and two references are orphaned.

## 8. Overall assessment (0–5)

**2 — weak but in scope.** A priced, now largely honest restatement of the projection postulate at the absorption vertex, whose own contributions (the location formula and its regime, the detector rows, the characterization of the record and of the order-independence experiments) still carry the errors listed above; nothing in v1.3 contradicts an existing experiment, but several of its descriptions of experiments are wrong in ways a detector physicist would catch on first reading.

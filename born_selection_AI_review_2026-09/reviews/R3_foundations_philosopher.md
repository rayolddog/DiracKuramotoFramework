## 2. Summary (to fix terms)

The paper proposes that outcome selection happens inside the detector: an incident quantum excites every site with energy ∝ |A_i|², a noise-driven exchange redistributes that energy as a martingale, and the site that first assembles the whole quantum registers, giving Born frequencies by optional stopping. Through v0.6–v0.9 it withdrew the derivation claim: the square enters as premise P1 (with wave realism P0), the exchange game "carries no statistical weight of its own", the Born weight enters through a commitment hazard linear in the share (Theorem 5 = golden rule), and exclusivity is a nonlocal premise P5. Addendum A (v0.10) reports a second construction (MathX) that arrives at the same two premises, and proposes as a "candidate replacement" for §§3–5 a first-passage race whose rates are those of the normalized conditional state, with P5 both cancelling losing clocks on a fire and renormalizing survivors on a null. The paper's stated position is that it supplies "at most the selector half" of a theory and derives neither the measure nor exclusivity.

## 3. Strengths

- The de-claiming trail (v0.6–v0.9) is real and unusually specific; each withdrawal names what was wrong and where. `NEGATIVE_RESULT.md` is a model of how a failed program should be written up.
- The MathX record is pre-registered, seeded, and keeps its misses (P5 V4, P12, P14). Its own ledger (L24–L28) is more candid than the addendum that summarizes it.
- Theorem 1 and Appendix A.1 are correct and cleanly stated; the uniqueness of the √e noise law for a driftless share is a nice small result about CIR-type processes, whatever its physical bearing.
- A.3's observation that reading A, without reabsorption, predicts *no counts* at a balanced split above 470 nm and *two* records below it is a genuine, data-facing exclusion of one reading, conditional on a stated assumption.

## 4. Major concerns

**M1. P5 after A.7 is the Lüders/quantum-jump conditional update, and the paper's dynamical description of it is one the program has already withdrawn.** A.4: the cancellation "is P5, acting across the port separation faster than light and, per this program's record, within about a thousandth of the commitment time." A.7: "P5 acts twice, cancelling on a fire and renormalizing on a null, both across the separation." But the sibling paper, at v1.1/v1.2 (§5.1 clause 3), says the opposite of the same object: the projection acts "with no delay and no speed (v1.0's 'within a thousandth of the opening time' was a bound from the discarded race model on a dynamical stop the projection does not have)", and its §6 withdraws the claim that a global ordering is *needed* because "the jump operators … commute at spacelike separation." Once A.7's rates are "those of the normalized conditional state", the "two actions" of P5 are the two halves of the stochastic master equation of photodetection — jump ψ→c_iψ/‖c_iψ‖ and no-jump evolution under H − (iħ/2)Σc_i†c_i, renormalized. Nothing acts across anything; the conditional state is updated. To describe a change of the conditional state as a superluminal action with a bound of 10⁻³ of a commitment time is the category error the program corrected nine days earlier. Correspondingly, P6 ("registration events are ordered by a global foliation … required for spacelike commitments", §2 third remark) is stated as consumed by the single-quantum sector, where the sibling paper now says it fixes nothing observable. *Fix:* state P5 as the conditional-state update (Lüders rule) of a photodetection unravelling on configuration/Fock space; delete the "faster than light" and "thousandth" language or confine it explicitly to the discarded reading-A dynamics; state P6 as an ontological choice with no observable content, as OWOC v1.2 does.

**M2. Once A.7 is the model, the paper has no content beyond standard photodetection theory plus the projection postulate, and §8 contradicts it.** Under reading B (v0.9) Theorems 1–3 govern a "reversible sub-threshold dynamics" that "carries no statistical weight of its own"; Theorems 4–5 are the golden rule. Under A.7 the selection stage is the SME unravelling with rates ∝ the conditional-state branch weight. MathX §12.7 says it exactly: the reading "derives nothing that the rate law and the stop do not already contain," and the sibling paper's §7 says "No experiment separates this interpretation from quantum mechanics with the projection postulate, because it is that." Paper 1 v0.10 does not say this; it says the opposite in three places that were left standing. §5: "Every dynamical account … obtains Born statistics by *engineering* the noise … The content of this paper is that no engineering is needed." A.1 refutes that sentence: the argmax rule gives Born "for exactly one noise family, the Gumbel form at unit scale", "Born is the point β = 1 and nothing in the rule fixes it." That is engineered noise by the paper's own definition. §8.4 and §8.7 still advertise curvature in the port statistics and an above-Tsirelson excess as things "no quantum-mechanical mechanism can produce" — but the candidate replacement mechanism *is* a quantum-mechanical unravelling, which is affine in ρ by construction (§6.3) and can produce neither. Table 2 still lists this work as "Fairness … **derived** (Theorems 1–5) · Born **exact** · Modifies quantum dynamics **no** · Predicts deviations **yes**"; the last two cannot both be true of a theory that predicts S > 2√2. *Fix:* choose. If A.7 is the model, §8.3–8.5 and §8.7 are predictions of the abandoned exchange game and must be relabelled or removed, Table 2's row must read "postulated / inherited / no", and the paper should say what OWOC §7 says. If §8's deviations are still claimed, A.7 cannot be the candidate replacement.

**M3. "Conditional compatibility" does not survive the double entry of Born.** The abstract frames the result as a pushforward of a "microstate preparation measure over the detector's own ready state" compared against the Born comparator on a different space. Under reading B and A.7 the outcome law is the Born weight inserted as the hazard (P1 + Theorem 5) and inserted again as the branch weights under staggered arrival ("the share between ports is the branch weight put in as an initial condition and is decided by no race", A.7). The pushforward then equals the comparator because the process was defined by the comparator; the preparation measure does no work on the outcome weights at all (at most on efficiency and dark counts). "Compatibility across two different spaces" is an identity, not a test, and the abstract's headline construct is empty. Worse, the abstract says the preparation measure has "independently measured provenance", while the G1/G2 contract it cites records "provenance populated by named source, not yet by measured value" and "not independently reviewed". *Fix:* state that under the adopted reading the outcome statistics are independent of the ready-state measure by construction; delete "independently measured"; either give the measure an observable role (A.2's Fano clause is the only candidate, and it bounds a hazard multiplier, not the measure) or drop the two-spaces framing.

**M4. A.4's "independent corroboration" is not independent in any sense that matters.** Three reasons. (i) Provenance: the same agent wrote Paper 1, `NEGATIVE_RESULT.md`, the MathX studies and the addendum (`MATHX_ADDENDUM_LEDGER`: "Not an independent audit: the same agent ran the MathX studies and wrote this"). The claim that the runs "were built … without reference to this paper's premises" is contradicted by MathX's own ledger: L27 was made by reading the race "against §2's third remark and `NEGATIVE_RESULT.md`", and L28/Table 8 were run because "the anticipated-findings ledger for the Paper 1 v0.10 round predicted the race would inherit [the staggered-arrival failure]" — i.e. the amendment that now defines A.7's premise 2 was designed to remove an anticipated review finding (MathX commit `0c24401` 06:52 UTC; Paper 1 commit `b1c5e20` 06:53 UTC, "review ledger item 3 removed before the freeze"). (ii) Logic: that a race among exponential clocks with rates ∝ d_i selects i with probability d_i/Σd_j, and that a single winner requires the other clocks to stop, are theorems (Gumbel-max; the memoryless property), not findings. A simulation of a theorem cannot corroborate anything; it could not have come out otherwise. (iii) The corroboration is of a *negative* result — "we could not derive X" — and a second failure by the same author to derive X is not evidence about X. *Fix:* retitle A.4 "the same identity met in a second construction, by the same author"; delete "independent"; delete "built without reference".

**M5. A.6 adds nothing to Bell's theorem and mislabels what it shows.** The runs: a shared hidden polarization read by a projection rule gives the sawtooth 1 − 4θ/π and S = 2 (Bell 1964's own local model); Malus gives S = √2; a common bulk phase is a common cause (S = 2); a phase-dependent efficiency rides the Garg–Mermin line 4/η_c − 2 to 2.83 at η_c = 0.828 (the detection loophole, closed in 2015); setting particle 2's polarization by A's outcome gives cos 2θ. MathX L20 says the last "is the projection postulate applied to the pair." Every line is textbook. The "one thing worth keeping" — that B must read by Malus rather than deterministically, else S = 4 — is not "Theorem 5's linearity at B"; it is that a deterministic reading of a definite polarization is not quantum mechanics. A.6's closing sentence ("MathX Part F finds [the gate] never opens in silicon") concerns the sponsor's model's phase-dependent efficiency, not fair sampling in any real Bell test, and should not be placed next to "fair-sampling violation" as if it bore on one. *Fix:* replace A.6 with one sentence: the sponsor's pair model is a local hidden-variable model and is excluded by Bell's theorem and the loophole-free experiments; the conditional update of §7 is the Lüders rule.

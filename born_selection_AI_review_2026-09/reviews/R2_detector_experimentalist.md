## 2. Summary

The paper proposes that outcome selection happens inside the detector: an incident quantum reversibly excites all candidate absorber sites with energy shares ∝ |A_i|², a noise-driven exchange redistributes the shares as a martingale, and optional stopping makes registration probabilities equal the initial shares. Through v0.6–v0.9 the claim was reduced to conditional compatibility with the Born comparator, with Born entering through P0+P1 (and, under the adopted reading B, through a commitment hazard linear in the share) and exclusivity through a nonlocal one-quantum stop (P5). Addendum A reports MathX runs said to reach the same conclusions independently and adds three items: an exact Gumbel-max/first-passage identity for the argmax rule (A.1); a clause that site-specific hazard factors must decorrelate between arrivals, with the Fano factor of a single detector's two-port split as its observable and "F = 1 in experiment" as the bound (A.2); and an exclusion of reading A at a balanced split from existing single-photon data, conditional on re-emitted sub-gap photons leaving the device (A.3).

## 3. Strengths

- The de-claiming is real and unusual: the abstract, §1, §9.1 and A.8 state that Born is inherited, exclusivity is a premise, and the paper is "at most the selector half." A referee rarely has to argue an author *down* to that.
- The MathX record is genuinely pre-registered, seeded, and reproducible; misses are kept (P12, the D30b closed-form FAIL). I re-ran two scripts and got the tabulated numbers.
- A.1 is correct: argmax over log e_i + Gumbel(0,1) samples ∝ e_i (McFadden/Maddison), and this is identical to a Gillespie race with rates ∝ e_i. The paper is right that this makes the "tuning" of the §4(a) argmax rule exact rather than rhetorical.
- The calorimetric argument against reading A (records carrying 0.45–0.72 of the quantum vs TES measuring the whole photon energy) is a good, physical, existing-data objection.
- A.5's qualification via Heberle et al. 1995 (coherent control of excitons is real for a driven absorber) is correctly characterized and correctly scoped to passive detectors.

## 4. Major concerns

**M1. A.2's "F = 1 in experiment" is not a fact any real counting experiment supports at the level claimed, and the derived bound ("under about 10 ns") is unsupported.**
The claim (A.2, `NEGATIVE_RESULT.md` addendum, MathX L26, `MODEL_SECTION.md` §12.5): "Every SPAD pair gives the Born split within one device with binomial statistics at every counting rate used, so the bound is under about 10 ns at the fastest rates." No citation is given anywhere in the record, and as stated it is false. (i) The counting statistics of a silicon SPAD at high rate are dominated by dead time: for a non-paralysable dead time τ_d the Fano factor of the count is ≈ (1+Rτ_d)⁻², i.e. ~0.9 at 1 Mcps and ~0.4–0.6 at 10 Mcps for τ_d = 30–50 ns. Sub-Poissonian counting from dead time is textbook. (ii) Afterpulsing (percent-level in Si SPADs, with trap release constants from ~100 ns to μs) and, in arrays, optical crosstalk make the statistics super-Poissonian at short times. (iii) "F = 1" for the *count* requires a Poissonian (coherent) source; thermal or bunched light gives F > 1, heralded or squeezed light F < 1, with no detector memory involved. The paper's observable must therefore be the *conditional split fraction* at fixed total, not the port count, and the addendum blurs the two: it defines F = Var/[p(1−p)/M] (binomial) and then writes "for the count at one port this is 1 + Q" (Poissonian, Mandel 1979) — different normalizations, different systematics. (iv) The "10 ns" bound requires F = 1 to be established at ~10⁸ arrivals/s; no silicon SPAD gives Poissonian counts there. A defensible statement from real data is that, after dead-time and afterpulse correction, binomial splits are consistent with F − 1 ≲ 10⁻² at ~MHz rates, bounding τ at the microsecond, not 10 ns, level. That still exceeds any fs vacuum timescale by many orders, so the *conclusion* survives — but the paper should claim what the data show. (v) The τ = ∞ ("frozen") case is excluded far more simply and far more strongly than by any Fano measurement: every calibration by detector substitution shows the same split ratio at every detector to < 1%, whereas the frozen model predicts an all-or-nothing split per device (step fraction 1.000). That is the existing datum, and it should be cited in place of an unmeasured F. **Fix:** replace "experiment has F = 1 at every counting rate" with a specific dataset and a dead-time/afterpulse-corrected F ± σ_F at a stated rate; derive the τ bound from that; separate the split-fraction Fano from the count Fano; cite detector-substitution constancy for the frozen limit.

**M2. A.3's wavelength lines are artefacts of the toy geometry, and the text is internally inconsistent about which side of the exclusion the 505 nm data bite on.**
The "470 nm" line comes from `release_retry.py`'s `SHARES[1.0] = (0.4254, 0.4254)`, i.e. the second study's 1-D packet with 15% of the density falling in a no-atom gap and outside |x| > 7 (`PREREGISTRATION.md` D3, P1: "fill 0.8507"). With θ = E_gap/E_photon and E_gap = 1.12 eV, share 0.4254 = θ at exactly 470.9 nm; with fill factor 1 the line would be 553 nm; with any fill factor above 0.912 reading A *does* register at 505 nm (θ = 0.456) — at both ports. The "r = 2 above 553 nm" line is likewise share 0.5699 = θ, which is 631 nm, not 553 nm; the grid only sampled 660 and 500 nm. The manuscript's own sentence "below 470 nm, where the half-share clears the gap" is wrong on its face — a half-share (0.5) clears the silicon gap at 553 nm; 470 nm is where a 0.4254 share does. Worse, §6.1 and Table 1 state that at 505 nm "both arms of a balanced split sit above θ from the outset" (share 0.5 > 0.456, so reading A predicts *doubles*, bounded by g²(0)), while A.3 states that at 505 nm reading A predicts *no counts*. Both cannot be readings of the same wavelength; the difference is the 0.85 fill factor. The robust content of A.3 is elementary and should be stated as such: dividing one quantum equally between two thresholded ports gives, under reading A with escape, either no record (share < θ) or two ports each able to eject (share ≥ θ, doubles = 0.435² ≈ 0.19) — never one-and-only-one — so reading A fails at a balanced split at *every* wavelength, and the wavelength lines add nothing. **Fix:** delete the 470/553 nm specifics or derive them with a physical fill factor; reconcile §6.1 with A.3; state that the no-count side is excluded by nonzero *heralding efficiency* at 505 nm, not by g²(0) (which is irrelevant to that side).

**M3. Grangier et al. 1986 is the wrong experiment for a silicon-gap argument.**
A.3 (and ledger M-8) applies θ = E_gap(Si)/E_photon to Grangier's 422.7 nm cascade photon. That experiment used photomultipliers, not silicon avalanche diodes. A photocathode threshold (~1.8–2 eV for bialkali) gives θ ≈ 0.6–0.7 at 422.7 nm, so under reading A the balanced split would give *no counts*, not coincidences — the opposite side from the one the "wording correction" now asserts. The exclusion of reading A by Grangier's data still holds (either way reading A fails), but the specific claim as written is detector-inconsistent, and the [verify] flag on the wavelength does not cover the detector. Separately, the 505 nm g²(0) = 0.0023 figure is taken from a review (arXiv 1806.08430) with, per the ledger, "detector model not stated"; the paper asserts "taken with silicon SPADs" without that support. **Fix:** cite the primary source, its detectors and heralding efficiency; drop the silicon-θ analysis of a PMT experiment or redo it with a photocathode threshold.

**M4. The "no-reabsorption" assumption is load-bearing for an unphysical process, and the silicon absorber picture is not silicon.**
Reading A's "re-emission" of a partial, sub-gap holding has no physical counterpart: there is no state to emit from. The escape-versus-retry fork is therefore a choice between two unphysical options, and "the physical case" language (A.3, D30b) overstates it. For genuinely above-gap re-emission inside a silicon device, "leaves the device" is also wrong: with n ≈ 3.5–4 the escape cone traps ~98% of internally emitted light, so photon recycling, not escape, is the physical case (the study's own final sentence half-concedes this). More fundamentally, the manuscript's absorbers are "bound electrons or atoms" at ~0.2 nm spacing (5×10¹⁰ in 0.25 μm³), but the states a 500 nm photon couples to in silicon are Bloch states delocalized over the coherence volume, and the transition at 2.48 eV is *indirect* (phonon-assisted; the direct gap is 3.4 eV). "The interband vertex" is a second-order vertex in silicon; "which site" at atomic spacing corresponds to nothing a SPAD can resolve, and the first physical which-position information is the photo-carrier's generation depth/lateral position at the ~10–100 nm scale. **Fix:** either treat the absorber as a set of k-space/coherence-volume channels and re-derive N, θ and the "sites," or restrict the working example to a genuinely discrete-level absorber (atom array) and drop silicon as the worked case.

**M5. P3(b)'s condition d ≫ ℓ fails by the manuscript's own numbers, and the numerical check used the wrong spacing.**
§2 states that "in a densely packed absorber array with d ≲ ℓ the nearest-neighbour noise correlation is O(1) and clause (b) simply fails," flags d ≫ ℓ as "not yet verified for the detector families discussed in §6.1." It *is* verifiable from §6.1: N ≈ 5×10¹⁰ sites in the diffraction volume gives d ≈ 0.17 nm (silicon atomic spacing 0.27 nm), against the paper's own ℓ "of nanometres." So d ≪ ℓ, clause (b) fails for the working example, and Theorem 3's protection (f ~ 10⁻⁶; §8.2's "six orders of magnitude to spare") does not apply to silicon. `code/check_g1g2_ready_state.py` places six sites at 20 nm with ℓ = 2 nm — a hundred times the actual spacing — which is why it reports 4.6×10⁻⁵. **Fix:** compute the residual at the physical spacing (it will be O(1)), and either relocate the working example to a sparse absorber or reformulate the noise as acting on coherence-volume modes rather than atomic sites.

**M6. §6.1's ladder is order-of-magnitude bookkeeping with internal slips, and under the adopted reading B it is declared non-load-bearing yet retained as if it were.**
Slips: the diffusion spread "≈ 8 nm" is √(2Dτ) at τ = 10 fs; at the paper's own upper bound of 1 ps it is ≈ 85 nm (D ≈ 36 cm²/s), and hot-carrier ballistic transport at ~10⁷ cm/s over 1 ps is ~100 nm. Neither harms the micron-scale-pattern point, but the number is quoted as if it held across the range. "N in the diffraction volume" assumes a focused spot; fibre-coupled or free-space SPAD illumination is tens of μm across, though only ln²N enters. The remark that τ_game "falls in the tens-of-picoseconds range of measured SPAD timing jitter" should be removed even "without weight": SPAD jitter is understood from carrier transit and avalanche build-up, and the sentence invites a reading the physics does not support. The top rung has been rewritten in v0.7, v0.8, v0.9 and now A.7 (where the exchange game itself is slated for replacement); if reading B makes the rung "not a condition," the section should say only that and demote the rest to an appendix. The NbN entries (θ ≈ 0.002, hotspot 10–50 ps) are literature-typical and adequately flagged in `snspd_ladder_check.py`.

**M7. The MathX SPAD model does not resemble a real device in any load-bearing way, and the manuscript overclaims what it shows.**
`spad_contact.py` D7–D8: a 121×60 lattice of i.i.d. lognormal "resistances," a charge that steps down 60 rows choosing the cheapest of three neighbours (or a DBM/diffusion variant), and a Galton–Watson branching with loss 0.1 and ionization 0.5 per row, "record iff ≥ 100 at row 60." There is no electric field, no depletion region, no impact-ionization coefficients, no self-sustaining two-carrier avalanche, no lateral avalanche spreading, no quench, no dead time, no afterpulsing, no depth-dependent triggering probability. The branching-process idea does echo the standard breakdown-probability treatment of avalanche triggering, but the parameters are arbitrary and the current is *seeded from the pixel selection already made*, so the record "decides nothing" by construction — the study's own RESULTS say so. A.7's "reached by simulation of a specific device rather than by argument" is therefore not supportable, and §5.4/A.7's "the cascade is photon-agnostic … carries no which-site weight" is contradicted by real devices (depth- and position-dependent avalanche-trigger probability, lateral PDE non-uniformity and edge effects at the percent level) *and by the record itself*: `NEGATIVE_RESULT.md` states that this phrasing "was corrected at review, 2026-09-05" to "multiplies the vertex weight by a stochastic, position- and energy-dependent efficiency." The manuscript did not propagate that correction. **Fix:** describe the model as a toy transport/branching sketch; propagate the 2026-09-05 correction to §5.4 and A.7; if a device claim is wanted, use a published SPAD triggering-probability model.

**M8. The "exclusivity must act within 10⁻³ of the commitment time" argument (v0.9 header, A.4) is made against a classical-field strawman, not against quantum mechanics.**
`vertex_exclusivity_discriminator.py`'s "ensemble reading" runs two independent hazards to completion and finds P(both) = 1. But the golden rule applied to a *one-photon* field state gives zero rate at the second port once the photon is absorbed — the quantum-jump renormalization that A.7's night amendment then imports as "P5 acting twice." The 400× exclusion and the femtosecond stop therefore constrain a semiclassical-field model, which was never the alternative on the table. The conclusion "the selection is one-world" does not follow from g²(0) ≈ 0; it follows from the input being a one-photon state.

**M9. §8's experiments are, for the most part, not executable as specified or have no live target.**
§8.4 needs a calibrated sub-wavelength atom-array *absorption-counting port*, which the paper concedes is unbuilt. §8.6(vii) (asymmetric split, two wavelengths around 553 nm, one SPAD pair) is executable — indeed every relative-PDE calibration against a monitored splitter at 488/532 nm is already a version of it — but under the adopted reading B it is "predicted null," and A.3 says reading A is already excluded, so the test has no target. §8.6(v) (warm-detector odds bias) would be swamped by dark counts, afterpulsing and dead-time systematics the paper itself lists as unanalysed. §8.6(vi) is fine as a consistency test but is already answered incidentally by TES/MKID calorimetry, as the paper notes. §8.7's "couple a common classical perturbation into both detectors in the selection band, at the absorbers" is not physically specified: what field, at what frequency, coupled how to a fs-scale intra-absorber process without adding quanta? **Fix:** keep §8.6(vii) as the one bench-executable item, state its predicted null under B, and mark the rest as programmatic.

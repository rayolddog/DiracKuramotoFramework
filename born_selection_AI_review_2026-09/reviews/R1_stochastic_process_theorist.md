## 2. Summary

The paper proposes that outcome selection at a detector is a stochastic game among candidate absorber sites over energy shares $s_i = e_i/\sum_j e_j$ deposited in proportion to $|A_i|^2$ (P0–P1), that ordinary detector noise makes those shares a martingale (Theorems 1–3), and that optional stopping — at first passage to $s_i = 1$ or at a commit time with a hazard linear in the share (Theorems 0, 4, 5) — returns registration probabilities equal to the initial shares, i.e. the Born comparator. Through v0.9 the paper has withdrawn the claim to derive anything: Born enters through P1 and the linear hazard, exclusivity through the nonlocal premise P5. Addendum A reports that an independently built two-spinor model (MathX) reached the same negative result, adds three items — the Gumbel-max completion of §4(a)'s extreme-value rule (A.1), a Fano-factor clause on the commitment hazard (A.2), a conditional exclusion of reading A (A.3) — and offers a "refined microscopic model" (A.7), a first-passage race with per-site exponential clocks whose rates are (after a late amendment) those of the normalized conditional state, as the candidate replacement for §§3–5's exchange game.

## 4. Major concerns

**M1. The exchange game of §§3–5 and the race of A.7 are two mechanisms, not one, and A.7 does not say so.** A.7 presents the race as "the candidate replacement for §§3–5's exchange game" while §5.4 (v0.9) keeps Theorems 1–3 as "what the substrate must not do to the shares before commitment." But the two constructions have different state variables and different martingales. In §§3–5 the share is an *energy* (P0), and its martingale property is produced by noise fairness — the $\sqrt e$ scaling of Theorem 1, the absent phase lever of Theorem 2, the diagonal $C$ of Theorem 3. In the amended A.7 race the share is the weight of a *normalized conditional state*, nothing is exchanged between sites at all, and the martingale property comes from Bayesian conditioning: between jumps the conditional weights drift deterministically ($ds_i/dt = -\lambda_i s_i + s_i\sum_j\lambda_j s_j$), and the jump terms cancel that drift exactly. I verified this cancellation by hand; it is the standard fact that the unravelling's conditional state is a martingale. Theorems 1–3 therefore constrain a process (noise-driven energy exchange) that the replacement mechanism does not contain, and the "fairness" of the replacement is the projection postulate's, not the detector's. The OWOC round's own authors' response (N1) already concedes this in so many words — "Clause 2 becomes 'the projection postulate with Born weights, applied at the vertex' … the paper should say so" — and Paper 1 A.7 should say it with the same plainness instead of "the noise-driven exchange game of §4 is replaced … by a first-passage race whose per-site clock is the vacuum term." *Fix:* state in A.7 and A.8 that installing the race retires Theorems 1–3 as load-bearing, and that the share's martingale property in the race is conditioning, not noise fairness.

**M2. Once the hazard is linear in the share, the fairness theorems do no work — in either reading of what the share is.** Theorem 4 gives $P_i = \mathbb E[s_i(T)]$ for any stopping time $T$; the fairness theorems' only contribution is to make $\mathbb E[s_i(T)] = s_i(0)$. Consider the two cases. (a) If the shares do not move between capture and firing (the A.7 race at $\Delta=0$; reading B's "amplitude bookkeeping" with no sub-threshold dynamics), the martingale is constant and Theorem 4 is the identity $P_i = \lambda_i/\sum_j\lambda_j$; Theorems 1–3 are vacuous. (b) If the shares do move, the theorems enforce $\mathbb E[s_i(t)] = s_i(0)$ for all $t$ — but quantum mechanics does not require this. A unitary hopping between sites makes $|a_i(t)|^2$ oscillate, is not a martingale, and yet the QM prediction $P_i = \int\Gamma|a_i(t)|^2\,(\text{survival})\,dt$ is Born-exact *for the evolved state*. The "Born comparator" that the fairness theorems match is Born-at-capture-time, which is QM's comparator only if the sub-threshold dynamics is trivial — case (a). Either way the theorems are idle. The paper's v0.9 formulation ("what the substrate must not do to the shares") turns a non-requirement into a requirement. *Fix:* either (i) show that some physically motivated non-martingale sub-threshold dynamics is *excluded by data* (then the theorems have content), or (ii) demote Theorems 1–3 to a robustness remark about one class of models and say that the comparator they match is not, in general, the one QM prescribes.

**M3. A.7's amended premise — "the rates are those of the normalized conditional state" — is not a race; it is the projection postulate written as a hazard, and A.1's identification does not cover it.** Three points, each checkable against `staggered_conditional.py` and D17.
(i) Under staggered arrival with $\Delta\ge T$ and $\eta_A=1$, the process is *sequential*: port A fires with probability $s_A$; if it does not, the A-branch is exhausted, $n(t)\to s_B$, and B's conditional rate becomes $\lambda W_B$ — a jump by the factor $1/s_B = 5$ relative to its own branch rate, triggered by a null at the remote port. The ports are never in competition. The unconditional probabilities $P(A) = s_A\eta_A$, $P(B) = s_B\eta_B$ (which I confirmed follow from the unnormalized rate integrating the unconditional jump density) are the branch weights put in; the "race" chooses only the atom within a port, and there it is again $w_i/W_P$, i.e. Born applied to the conditional state. The paper's own L28 says "decided by no race"; A.7's bullet list still calls the stage "the race."
(ii) The sponsor's stipulation that "each atom also carries its own vacuum term, unaffected by the number of atoms in the aperture" (PREREGISTRATION §0) is violated by D17: the rate of an atom in port B at time $t$ is $\lambda w_i a_B^2(t)/n(t)$ and depends on port A's exposure history. The per-site independent clock of A.1 no longer exists.
(iii) The Gumbel-max ↔ first-passage identity of A.1 holds for constant rates or rates with a *common* time profile (`NEGATIVE_RESULT.md`'s "site-independent time profile" is exactly this condition). Under staggered exposure the profiles are port-dependent, and the identity fails — which is precisely why reading S (the honest race) gives the nearer port 0.997 of records. A.1's sentence "the extreme-value rule is not an alternative to the first-passage race but the same object" is therefore true only at $\Delta = 0$; the amended A.7 is a third object, and neither the argmax rule nor the memoryless race with fixed rates.
(iv) On nonlocality: the manuscript says (§2) "Any single-world account of beam-splitter anticorrelation carries a nonlocal constraint — textbook collapse is nonlocal too," and A.4 presents the race's need for P5 as "independent corroboration." As a matter of stochastic modelling this is false in the single-quantum sector: a common-cause model in which the port is drawn at the source with probabilities $(s_A, s_B)$ and each port then fires with its own efficiency reproduces $P(A)=s_A\eta_A$, $P(B)=s_B\eta_B$, zero coincidences, at every stagger, locally. What forces the nonlocal stop is P0 — energy simultaneously present at both ports — not the anticorrelation data. The race "arrived at" P5 because it was built with live clocks at both ports; that is the premise returning, not corroboration. *Fix:* rewrite A.7 premise 2 as "the quantum-jump unravelling of photodetection, with Born branch weights, imported as the selection stage," cite it (Srinivas–Davies 1981; Dalibard–Castin–Mølmer 1992; Carmichael 1993; Wiseman–Milburn), drop the word "race" for the between-port decision, and restate the nonlocality claim as conditional on P0.

**M4. The Fano-factor clause of A.2 is mis-derived in two places and overstated in a third.**
(a) *The "does not average out with N" result is an artifact of an infinite-mean multiplier.* A.2: "frozen per-site structure averages out where it enters through a sum over many sites … and does not where a maximum can single out one site." Under the exponential-clock reading *everything* enters through a sum, $P(i\ \text{first}) = \lambda_i/\sum_j\lambda_j$; a frozen multiplier $m_i$ on the hazard gives a per-detector share $r\sum_A m_i/(r\sum_A m_i + \sum_B m_j)$, which self-averages by the law of large numbers for any multiplier with finite mean. The frozen MathX factor is $e^{G_i}$ with $G_i$ standard Gumbel, and $\mathbb E[e^{tG}] = \Gamma(1-t)$ diverges at $t=1$: the sum is dominated by its largest term for every $N$. That, not "a maximum can single out one site," is why F stays at $\sim 10^2$–$10^3$. I checked with a fresh Gumbel race on top of frozen light-tailed multipliers at $N = 10, 100, 1000$: lognormal (sd 0.5) gives F = 8.5, 1.8, 1.3; uniform on [0.5, 1.5] gives 3.5, 1.2, 0.9; exponential(1) gives 29, 4.8, 1.3; frozen $e^{G}$ gives 239, 142, 119. The clause "*any* site-specific random factor entering the commitment hazard must decorrelate between arrivals" is therefore wrong as stated. The correct clause is that a frozen factor must have a light enough tail for the rate-sum to self-average — which any bounded physical multiplier does, and which the paper's own Table 7 (frozen phase weight: F = 25.6, 2.7, 1.1) already illustrates.
(b) *The observable has no power in the physical regime.* With $N\sim 10^{10}$ sites per port (§6.1) and any finite-mean frozen factor, $F = 1$ to far below measurable precision regardless of the correlation time. F = 1 in experiment bounds nothing about frozen structure; it bounds only the unit-scale-Gumbel pathology that no one has proposed. The statement that this is "the only test in this program that the model passes for a reason it supplies itself" (MODEL_SECTION §12.5) should be withdrawn.
(c) *The Mandel-Q gloss is wrong for the quantity defined.* A.2: "for the count at one port this is $1+Q$ with $Q$ Mandel's parameter, $Q=0$ being the Poissonian, $F=1$ the binomial split." D11 defines F with $M$ fixed. For fixed $M$ the port count is Binomial$(M,p)$, so $Q = \mathrm{Var}/\text{mean} - 1 = -p$ at $F=1$ (sub-Poissonian: $-0.8$ here), not 0. The gloss $F = 1 + Q$ holds only if $M$ is itself Poisson (I checked: $\tau = 10$ gives $Q = 8.94$ against $\tau-1 = 9$). Say which normalization is meant; they differ by exactly the quantity the clause is about.
(d) *The empirical premise is unreferenced.* "Every SPAD pair gives the Born split within one device with binomial statistics at every counting rate used, so the bound is under about 10 ns at the fastest rates." No citation; SPAD dead times of tens of nanoseconds make "binomial at $10^8$ s$^{-1}$" a nontrivial claim; and the mapping F → τ is only exact for integer τ with deterministic within-block outcomes (no phase weight).
(e) What survives is that the hazard must be a renewal process — the memorylessness of the golden rule, which `NEGATIVE_RESULT.md` already lists. A.2 adds a citation to Fano 1947 and Mandel 1979 whose quantities (ionization-yield variance relative to Poisson; photocount variance relative to Poisson) are not the binomial-relative index D11 defines. *Fix:* replace the clause with the correct light-tail condition, delete the Q gloss or condition it on Poisson $M$, reference or remove the SPAD claim, and drop "observable" unless a regime is named in which it discriminates.

**M5. Theorem 1's model does not conserve the pot, and the uniqueness claim is proven for the wrong class.** Theorem 1 takes $de_i = \sigma\sqrt{e_i}\,dW_i$ with *independent* increments. Then $dE = \sigma\sum_i\sqrt{e_i}\,dW_i \neq 0$: the total is a Feller diffusion, each $e_i$ hits zero a.s., and $E$ does too. The share drift computation is correct and $P_i = s_i(0)$ holds (I reproduced 0.801 for an 80/20 start), but the survivor does not "assemble the full quantum": at absorption its energy has median 0.71, is below half a quantum in 34% of runs and above 1.5 quanta in 15% (Euler–Maruyama, $\sigma=0.3$, $dt = 2\times10^{-3}$, 4000 trials). The closed-pot arguments that P4(a) and §2's exclusivity rest on ("Under a closed pot $\sum_i e_i = \hbar\omega$ … at most one site can ever be above threshold") do not apply to the model Theorem 1 analyses; they apply to the discrete stakes-scaled engine of Appendix D and the geometric-mean kernel of `exchange_kernel.py`, whose continuum limit is a Wright–Fisher-type diffusion with covariance $\propto s_i(\delta_{ij}-s_j)$, not independent CIR noise. For that conserving class the "unique fair point" statement of §5.1 is not proven — the pairwise kernel is $\sqrt{e_ie_j}$, and the relevant question is which *exchange* kernels give zero share drift, which Appendix A does not address. *Fix:* either restate Theorem 1 for a conserving exchange SDE and prove uniqueness there, or drop "unique" and the closed-pot language from the continuum theorem and confine them to the discrete engine.

**M6. A.1 contains factual misstatements about what MathX reproduced and what the race gives at finite N.**
(i) "The MathX vacuum race … reproduces both numbers with the sponsor's rule … giving 0.790 at one atom per port and 1.000 from ten atoms per port at intensity ratio $r=4$." The first number does reproduce §4(a)'s two-site 0.791 (same rule: argmax $A_i|\cos\theta_i|$ with 2:1 amplitudes; I get 0.7905). The second does not: §4(a)'s second number is 0.79 on the *ten-site* configuration (nine unit sites, one triple-amplitude site, Born 0.500), which MathX never ran; MathX's 1.000 is ten atoms per port at $r=4$, a different configuration. I computed §4(a)'s rule on its ten-site configuration: 0.788, so the manuscript's 0.79 is right, but A.1's reproduction claim is wrong.
(ii) "Under it the port share is 0.702, 0.794, 0.801, 0.802 at $N = 1, 10, 100, 1000$ … independent of $N$." The row quoted is not independent of $N$. With the phase weight the race's exact output at every $N$ is $\mathbb E[rW_A/(rW_A+W_B)]$ with $W_P = \sum_{i\in P}\cos^2(\Delta\varphi_i/2)$ — I get 0.7046, 0.7588, 0.7946, 0.7995 at $N = 1, 2, 10, 100$, matching the sampled rule to the third decimal — which is Born only as $N\to\infty$ because the phase weight self-averages. So the "identification" at finite $N$ is exact as an identity (argmax with Gumbel = clocks with rates $d_i$) but the object identified is not Born: it is a phase-weighted rule that predicts a 12% deviation from Born for a photon split 4:1 between two single-atom absorbers. That is a *prediction of A.7's model* that Table 1 does not list and §8 does not test; either the phase weight is physical, and the deviation belongs in the ledger, or it is not, and it should be removed from premise 1.
(iii) The provenance paragraph: "at one atom per port every rule is within a few percent of Born." V1 gives 0.702, V2 0.641, V4 0.678 at $N=1$ against 0.800 — 12–20% off. Only V0 (0.790) fits the sentence.
(iv) "Exactly one family repairs the rule" is established in the record by trying six forms. It is in fact a theorem — Yellott (1977, *J. Math. Psych.* 15, 109) shows that for i.i.d. random-utility maximization with three or more alternatives, Luce's choice rule holds iff the noise is Gumbel — and should be cited as such; without it "exactly one" is unsupported.

## 5. Minor / presentational

- §4(a)'s exceedance heuristic "$\exp(-a^2/2\sigma_i^2)$" describes Gaussian tails; the rule as stated uses $|\cos\theta|$, a bounded arcsine variable, whose maximum concentrates for a different reason (the ceiling at 1). The conclusion (concentration with $N$) is right; the mechanism named is not the one operating.
- §6.1's $\tau_{\rm game}\sim 2\ln^2N/\Gamma$ ("transport log-shares a distance $\ln N$ diffusively") assumes unit-rate diffusion in log-share, but under $\sqrt e$ noise the log-share variance rate is $\sigma^2/e_i$, which grows as a site drains. The estimate is a heuristic and should be labelled one; the $\Gamma$-invariance of the ratio is fine.
- v0.9 header: "an exponent of 2.16 [2.14, 2.19], within about 0.02 of the Born curve at every angle." A confidence interval that excludes 2.00 by seven standard errors is not "the Born curve"; the text's own gloss ("tracking the time-integrated tongue flux, $\propto K^{2.2}$ on that grid") is the accurate statement. Outside my specialism but a statistics point.
- A.7: "Born once a port holds about ten sites, independent of the number of sites" is self-contradictory (see M6(ii)).
- `g3_drain_tests` results files are gitignored ("regenerate"); the numbers quoted in §5.4 and §6.1 are therefore not in the frozen record.
- Paper 1's reference list lacks the unravelling literature that A.7 now rests on (Srinivas–Davies; Dalibard–Castin–Mølmer; Carmichael; Wiseman–Milburn) and Yellott 1977; Gillespie, McFadden, Maddison, Fano and Mandel entries are real and correctly given but still carry `[verify]`.

## 6. Specific questions for the author

1. In the amended A.7, what is the state variable whose martingale property Theorem 4 uses — an energy (P0) or a conditional-state weight? If the latter, in what sense is P0 still a premise of the selection stage?
2. Under staggered arrival with $\eta_A = 1$, port B's conditional rate rises by $1/s_B$ at the instant A's window closes without a fire. Is that rate change a physical process at B (then it is a signal carrier, and the no-signalling argument of §7.4 needs to address it) or a bookkeeping update (then "the race" is a description, not a mechanism)?
3. Does the phase weight $\cos^2(\Delta\varphi_i/2)$ of A.7 premise 1 predict a 12% departure from Born for two single-atom absorbers at a 4:1 split, and if so why is it not in Table 1?
4. For a frozen hazard multiplier with finite mean at $N\sim10^{10}$, what value of F does the model predict, and what correlation time does F = 1 then bound?
5. Which conserving exchange kernels give zero share drift, and is $\sqrt{e_ie_j}$ unique among them?
6. A common-cause model reproduces single-photon anticorrelation locally. Which premise of §2, other than P0, excludes it?

## 8. Overall assessment

**2 — weak but in scope.** The paper's boundary statement (it derives neither the Born weights nor exclusivity) is correct and the record is auditable, but the addendum installs, as a candidate mechanism, an object that is the projection postulate written as a hazard while still calling it a race, and one of its three new items is derived from an infinite-mean artifact.

## 1. Recommendation

**Major revision.** *Venue note:* as it stands this is a research record with an appended negative result and a corroborating addendum; it fits a "negative results / research records" track or a preprint-plus-repository, not a foundations-of-physics research article. *Authorship recommendation:* byline direction honest; **revise** the version disclosure as above.

## 2. Summary

The paper proposes that a detector converts an incident quantum's distributed, reversible excitation into one registration through a noise-driven energy game whose fairness follows from stated detector premises, so that registration frequencies equal squared-amplitude shares. Through v0.6–v0.9 it withdrew the derivation claim: Born enters as premise P1 with a hazard linear in the share (Theorem 5), exclusivity is the nonlocal premise P5, and the game supplies at most the selector half of a theory. Addendum A (v0.10) reports that six pre-registered MathX explorations, built in the sponsor's two-spinor language, reached the same negative result; it completes the extreme-value discussion of §4(a) (Gumbel-max ≡ exponential-clock race), adds a decorrelation clause with a Fano-factor observable, conditionally excludes reading A of the share ontology at a balanced split, and states a refined selection-stage model (a vacuum-clock first-passage race, amended overnight to run on the normalized conditional state) as a candidate for a later structural revision.

**M3. A.3's exclusion of reading A rests on a pixel-geometry artefact that the manuscript mislabels as a "half-share", and its wavelength line contradicts §6.1.** A.3: reading A "registers *nothing* at a balanced split above 470 nm … below 470 nm, where the half-share clears the gap, it predicts a record at *both* ports". §6.1 (line 236) puts the point where a half share clears silicon's gap at **553 nm** ("The boundary E₀ = 2E_gap sits at 553 nm"; "at 500 nm, with θ = 0.45, two sites can be exposed at once"). The 470 nm figure comes from `spad_surface_contact/PREREGISTRATION.md` D11/D29: the balanced-split pixel *norm* is **0.4254, not 0.5** — 15 % of the wave falls outside the two pixels — and C7 says so: "it holds at 500 nm because 0.425 < 0.45 and fails at 450 nm and below"; P20: "r = 1, θ ≥ 0.45: no pixel reaches θ (0.425)". So (i) "half-share" is wrong for the run cited; (ii) 470 nm is 1.12 eV/0.4254, an interpolation between the grid points 500 nm (nothing) and 400 nm (0.68), never itself run; (iii) the silence-side exclusion against the 505 nm datum rests on a margin of 0.025 set by a modelling convenience. Had the pixels collected the whole wave (0.5 > 0.45), reading A would register at 505 nm and the exclusion would have to run through coincidences instead. Paper 1 does not reconcile 470 with its own 553, and does not disclose that the line is a geometry artefact. The conditional exclusion is therefore weaker than "excluded outright" (A.3, quoting L23), and the M-3 ledger row inherits the overstatement.

## An item the panel did not raise

**§4's identification of the fair noise with an interference cross term between the site's amplitude and the vacuum field at the surface.** Recorded by the first author against the paper, from the sponsor's question of 2026-09-14 whether the vacuum wave interferes with the incident packet at all. At a photon-counting detector it does not: Glauber's counting rate is a normally ordered expectation, and the vacuum contributes nothing to it. The vacuum interferes with a signal only in the presence of a reference field (homodyne detection, where it enters the empty port); a passive absorber has none. What the vacuum does is dress the atom, setting its spontaneous rate and level shifts before the packet arrives, which is a factor on the transition rate and not a term added to the field. Two consequences. For §4, the cross-term origin of the P2 noise scaling is the stochastic-electrodynamics picture (Marshall and Santos, whom §4 cites for the threshold detectors), and that picture is the one Glauber's theory nullifies for photon counting; the thermal fields of the other bound charges can still supply a cross term, but the vacuum cannot, and the sentence should say so. For Addendum A, the same point settles the V1/V2 question on physical rather than numerical grounds: the vacuum term belongs to the atom's response (V1, Born), not to the incident amplitude (V2, the square root of Born), and the sponsor's ordering — vacuum on the fermion first — is the Glauber ordering. Disposition: ACCEPT against §4; add to A.1 as the physical reason for V1; and note, from the exchange of the same day, that once the selection is "first to fire" rather than "highest wins", any fresh light-tailed dressing factor gives Born at large $N$ and the Gumbel requirement of A.1 is an artefact of the maximum (R1 M4(a) read forward).

Yes — those are the right distinctions, but they need to be made as explicit parts of the model.
For energy conservation, say that conservation applies to the closed field + detector + material bath system, not necessarily to the reduced site variables alone:
\[ d(E_{\text{field}}+E_{\text{detector}}+E_{\text{bath}})=0. \]
If the site-energy equation is stochastic after coarse-graining the bath, derive its drift and covariance from that closed model. “Knowing all fluctuations” is not sufficient by itself; in standard quantum theory they are not simply a set of simultaneously known classical energies. If your ontology treats them as definite underlying variables, state that plainly as an additional premise. Then show the compensating energy currents, rather than using independent site noise as a closed exchange equation.
For amplitudes, I would make the sequence explicit:
\[ \text{complex incoming state} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{complex detector interaction} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{channel amplitudes } \alpha_\mu \;\longrightarrow\; \text{real channel weights } \lVert\alpha_\mu\rVert^2 \;\longrightarrow\; \text{single-outcome selection}. \]
That is more general than writing \(e_i\propto A_i^2\). In holography, for example, the relevant exposure is \(\lvert E_{\rm reference}+E_{\rm object}\rvert^2\): phase-sensitive interference is calculated before the quadratic intensity is obtained. For a general measurement, the analogous calculation must include the complex state, interaction Hamiltonian, detector degrees of freedom, and measurement channels before any proposed selection mechanism acts.
The key remaining burden is then very clear: derive the real channel “stakes” from a globally conserving microscopic interaction without already treating \(\lVert\alpha_\mu\rVert^2\) as an outcome probability. That would make the paper much more precise and substantially narrow the central criticism.

2. Summary (to fix terms)
The manuscript claims to derive the Born rule by treating quantum measurement as a physical, stochastic energy-exchange game among detector sites. Assuming that incident quanta deposit energy proportionally to the squared local wave amplitude (Premise 1) and that noise coupling is amplitude-linear (Premise 2), the authors argue that local energy shares evolve as a zero-drift martingale. Using optional stopping theorems, the registration probabilities at the end of the game match the initial Born weights. To extend this to multi-particle entangled states and Bell correlations, the authors postulate a nonlocal "shared registry" in the substrate (Premise 5) and a "preferred foliation" for temporal ordering (Premise 6) to enact exact conditional updates.

4. Major Concerns
	1.	Circularity in P5 (Shared Registry) and Entanglement: The paper heavily criticizes dynamical reduction models for "engineering their noise". However, the manuscript merely shifts the engineering to the substrate via Premise 5. Postulating a "shared registry" that physically instantiates the joint amplitude and faithfully renormalizes instantaneously upon the first registration event is mathematically and dynamically indistinguishable from the standard Copenhagen collapse axiom. The nonlocality and measurement problem have not been derived or solved; they have simply been relocated into an ad-hoc substrate mechanism.
	2.	P1 (Energetics) Begs the Square: Premise 1 asserts that the initial deposited energy is proportional to the classical squared amplitude via the energetics of driven oscillators. While this is a true classical phenomenon, identifying this classical energy density as the literal pre-collapse quantum seed for a probability game inserts the Born weight by hand as an initial condition. Mapping classical energy directly to quantum probability weights is a semantic repackaging of the Born axiom, not a derivation of it.
	3.	Perturbative vs. Non-Perturbative Mismatch (Theorem 5): The argument for kinematic linearity of the commit rate (Theorem 5) relies on lowest-order perturbation theory—counting absorption vertices. However, the stochastic selection game (Stage 2) is framed as a non-perturbative, long-time process involving continuous, highly coupled interactions with vacuum noise. Using a perturbative golden-rule rate for the exit channel while relying on a non-perturbative stochastic differential equation for the internal dynamics is a severe methodological inconsistency.
	4.	Strictness of Theorem 2 (Slaved Phase): The proof that sub-threshold sites lack an autonomous phase relies entirely on the discrete-level assumption of P4(i) (treating it as a simple damped linear mode). Real discrete absorbers (atoms, molecules) contain multi-level structures. Transient Stark shifts, multi-photon virtual paths, or dressing of the states by the continuous vacuum interaction could generate nonlinear phase dynamics, breaking the strict fairness required to prevent a rich-get-richer drift.
